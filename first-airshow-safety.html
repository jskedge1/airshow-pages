Safety and show rules

What airshows expect from their visitors, and why

An airshow puts live aircraft and thousands of people in the same field, and it works because everyone follows the same few rules. Pack with the bag search in mind, stay behind the crowd line and listen to the marshals.

UK airshows are among the most tightly regulated events in the country. The Civil Aviation Authority sets the rules, every display pilot holds a display authorisation, and every show has a flying display director who can stop the flying at any time. The distances between the crowd and the display line are fixed in law, which is why the crowd line is where it is and why nobody is allowed past it.

Visitors have a part to play too. The rules in the sections below are the ones you will find at almost every show. They are there so that thousands of people can share a field with live aircraft and go home safely. Marshals, stewards and the police are there to help, so if something looks wrong, tell one of them.

Aircraft taxi close to the crowd line. The fence is there for a reason.

Most ticketed shows search bags on the way in, and some have a bag size limit or a no large bags rule. Pack so that a steward can look through your bag quickly, and check the show's own list before you leave, because it is different everywhere.

Usually fine

Bring these

  • Folding chairs and picnic blankets
  • Food, soft drinks and flasks
  • Cameras, lenses and binoculars
  • Small cool bags and rucksacks
  • Sun cream, hats and waterproofs
  • Ear defenders for children
Check first

Depends on the show

  • Alcohol, allowed at some shows and banned at others
  • Large cool boxes, trolleys and wagons
  • Gazebos, windbreaks and large umbrellas
  • Tripods and step ladders outside the enclosures
  • Dogs, which some shows allow and most do not
  • Drones, which are banned at every show
Usually not allowed

Leave these at home

  • Glass bottles and jars
  • Barbecues and anything with a flame
  • Knives, tools and anything that could be a weapon
  • Laser pens and flares
  • Drones and model aircraft
  • Anything you would not take through an airport
I would suggest one bag per person, opened and ready as you reach the gate, with camera kit in its own bag where the steward can see it. It is quicker for you and for the queue behind you.

Stay behind the crowd line

The rope or fence marks the legal separation between you and the display. Nobody crosses it, nothing is thrown over it and children are kept back from it. If something lands beyond it, tell a marshal. Do not go and fetch it.

Keep clear of aircraft and vehicles

Taxiing aircraft, tugs, fuel bowsers and emergency vehicles use the showground all day. Keep to the marked walkways, never touch an aircraft on the static display unless the crew says you can, and never duck under a barrier for a better photograph.

Look after children

Agree a meeting point, write your mobile number on a wristband or a piece of paper in their pocket, keep them back from the crowd line fence and give them ear defenders for the jets. Most shows have a lost children point near the main entrance.

No drones, no lasers

Flying a drone anywhere near an airshow is a criminal offence and puts the pilots at risk. Laser pens are the same. Both will get you removed and reported to the police.

Alcohol and smoking

Some shows allow you to bring alcohol, others sell it on site and ban you bringing your own, and a few are dry. Smoking and vaping are usually restricted to marked areas, and never near aircraft or fuel. Check the show page.

Listen to the marshals

Marshals, stewards and the commentary team will tell you if anything changes, including if the flying is paused. Follow their directions straight away, especially around the car parks and the crowd line.

Summer

Sun and heat

There is almost no shade on an airfield. Hat, sun cream, a long sleeved top and more water than you think. Reapply sun cream at lunchtime.

Jets

Noise

Fast jets are loud enough to hurt. Ear defenders for children and ear plugs for anyone who is sensitive. Stand back from the display centre if the noise is too much.

Coast

Cold and wet

Seafront shows can be cold even in August. Layers, a waterproof and something to sit on that keeps the damp out.

Help

First aid

Every show has first aid posts, usually marked on the site map and signed from the crowd line. Tell a marshal if someone is unwell.

Plan

Medication

Bring what you need for a long day and keep it with you, not in the car. Some shows will let you store insulin in a first aid post.

Signal

Mobile signal

Signal drops when thousands of people share one mast. Agree your meeting point before you need it and do not rely on finding each other by phone.

Our view

Nothing here is difficult. Read the show's own list of what you can bring, pack so your bag can be checked in seconds, and keep children back from the fence. Do that and the stewards will barely notice you.
