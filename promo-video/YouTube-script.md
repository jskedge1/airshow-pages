## How to present like this

**1. Slow down.** About 2 words a second, with real pauses. Where it says *(beat)*, stop for a full second and hold eye contact with the lens. The silence is part of the joke.

**2. Facts first, then let them down gently.** Open with the big, impressive number, then deflate it with something small and honest:
> "The fourth biggest outdoor spectator event in the country. *(beat)* Which seems a bit harsh on the Red Arrows."

**3. Be precise.** "Four million", "since 2006", "fifty four bus routes", "eleven cameras". Precise numbers sound authoritative and quietly funny at the same time.

**4. Admit your failures cheerfully.** The car park and the last train home are your credentials: you built this because you've suffered for it. **Swap in your own real stories** where you can.

**5. Mean it.** When you talk about the budget calculator or the bus maps, let yourself sound pleased. Sincere delight in something slightly nerdy is the heart of the style. The last talking slot is the one place to drop the dryness completely.

**6. Let the world interrupt you.** The Red Arrows drowning you out, followed by a deadpan "They heard that.", is the cold open's punchline.

### Shooting it like a TV piece to camera
- **Open wide:** you small in the frame at a crowd line fence or museum hangar, sky above. Then cut to a mid shot for the talking.
- **Walk and talk** in slot 2, walking slowly towards the camera and stopping on "Who once missed the last train home."
- **Hold a prop:** a show programme for the facts, a flask, ear defenders.
- **The look:** after a dry line, hold a still, neutral look into the lens for a second before the cut.
- **Kit:** landscape, 4K or 1080p, 25 fps, a clip-on mic with a furry windshield, a bright overcast day. Do each slot 3 or 4 times and leave 2 seconds of silence at each end.
