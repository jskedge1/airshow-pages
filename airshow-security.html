Getting through airshow security

What happens at the gate and how to get through it quicker

Some shows search every bag that comes through the gate, others wave you straight in. With Martyn's Law expected to come into force in spring 2027, more shows may bring in checks, so I would advise planning for a bag search wherever you go. This guide shows you what to expect, how to pack so the queue moves, and how to load a trolley so it doesn't have to come apart at the table.

HOW STRICT IS THE GATE?

The first time I queued for a bag search at an airshow, I had a rucksack stuffed with lenses, a picnic and a camping chair strapped to the side. It took me a long time to get through, and I held up everyone behind me.

Security changes a lot from show to show. It mostly comes down to whether the show is ticketed, how many people go, and whether it is held on a military base. Have a look at the type of show you are heading to below.

Full checks

Big ticketed shows and military bases

Think of the Royal International Air Tattoo or RAF Cosford. When I last checked, the Air Tattoo planned to search every bag brought in, using an airport style setup with X-ray machines.

  • Every bag searched by hand or scanned
  • Ticket scanned after the bag search
  • Prohibited items list in the ticket terms
  • Queues build fast at opening time
Some checks

Smaller airfield and museum shows

Shows like Duxford or Old Warden may do a quick look inside your bag at the gate, and some days there may be no check at all. I would still pack as if there will be one.

  • Spot checks or a quick look in bags
  • Car checks at some gates
  • Rules can change between days
Light touch

Free seafront shows

It's rare to have your bag searched at a seafront airshow like Eastbourne, Clacton or Southport, as they are held on public ground with no single entrance, so you normally walk straight in. With Martyn's Law coming in, we might see this change, but nothing I have seen so far has been confirmed. I will keep an eye on it and update this page if anything changes.

I would adviseBefore you go, look at your show's own rules page for its prohibited items list. Lists change from year to year, and what is fine at one show can be turned away at another.

WHAT IS MARTYN'S LAW?

Martyn's Law is the name people use for the Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025. It is named after Martyn Hett, one of the 22 people killed in the Manchester Arena attack in 2017, and it follows years of campaigning by his mum, Figen Murray.

The law puts a duty on whoever runs a venue or event to plan for an attack and keep people safe if one happens. It does not ask anything new of you as a visitor, but it may change what you see at the gate.

200 to 799

Standard tier

Venues and events expecting this many people at once need a plan, trained staff and a way to evacuate, lock down or move people to safety.

800+

Enhanced tier

Bigger events must also look at how to reduce the risk itself. That could mean bag search policies, CCTV and vehicle checks, all written into a plan.

What it could mean on show day

More shows searching bags

Shows that only did spot checks may move to searching most or all bags, so I would pack as if every show will check.

Clearer exits and announcements

Expect more signs, briefed stewards and safety messages over the speakers. Take a look at where your nearest exit is when you set up your pitch.

Longer queues at first

New checks usually slow things down in the first season while staff and visitors get used to them. I would leave extra time in 2027.

Car and coach checks

Some shows may check vehicles coming into car parks or close to the crowd, which can add a little time to your arrival.

LOAD IT IN LAYERS

A trolley wagon is brilliant for a long day with children and chairs, but a badly packed one has to be emptied on the grass at the search tent. I see others do this every year. Load it so a steward can lift one layer and see the rest.

KEEP IT MOVING

The queue is at its longest just before the gates open and again mid morning. Think about what you are after on the day and pick your arrival time to suit you and your family.

Want a front row pitch?

Arrive before the gates open and be near the front of the queue. Head to the crowd line first and save your pitch, then look around the static show and trade stalls.

Bringing young children?

I would suggest arriving a little after opening when the first rush has gone through. Children love to see the static aircraft up close, and you won't be stood still in a long line with them.

Travel light

Leave the extras in the car. One bag between two people is quicker than two half full bags.

No bag lane

If you can carry everything in your pockets, look out for a no bag lane. It can save a lot of time.

Park and ride

At some shows the checks happen before you board the bus, so you walk straight onto the showground at the other end.

MY VIEW

Bag searches can feel like a hassle at the start of a long day, but they are there to keep everyone safe, and the stewards are usually friendly and quick when you are ready for them. Pack in layers, keep your ticket handy and you will be through before you know it.

With Martyn's Law on the way, I expect checks to become normal at most big shows from 2027. I will update each show's page when its plans are announced.

Jack Skedge, Founder and Managing Editor
