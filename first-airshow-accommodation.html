Where to stay

Hotels, camping and the other ways to make a weekend of it

A long day is a much better day when you have slept nearby. Hotels are linked from every featured show page, camping has its own guide, and this page helps you choose between them.

If the show is more than a couple of hours from home, staying over changes the whole trip. You arrive at the gate relaxed instead of having been up since five, and you can stay for the end of the flying without worrying about the drive home. For the two day shows, it is the only sensible way to do both days.

The downside is cost and availability. Hotels near the big shows book up months ahead and prices rise for the weekend, so the earlier you decide, the more choice you have.

Every featured show page has an accommodation section with hotels, inns and bed and breakfasts within 25 miles, with links to Booking.com, Airbnb, Premier Inn, Travelodge and Holiday Inn, so you can compare and book in a couple of clicks. I have set it up that way to make booking easier for you, rather than repeating the list here.

Reliable

Budget chains

Premier Inn, Travelodge and Holiday Inn Express near the motorway junctions. Predictable, often the cheapest, and usually with parking.

Character

Inns and pubs with rooms

Village pubs close to the airfield shows. Dinner downstairs, a short drive in the morning and a good atmosphere on show weekend.

Families

Bed and breakfast and Airbnb

Rooms and whole houses in the villages around the site. Good for families and groups. Book early for the big shows.

I would advise booking something with free cancellation as soon as the show date is announced, then shopping around once the line up is out. You can always cancel, but you cannot always find a room in July.

A pitch on the campsite. Pack for a cold night even in July.

Some shows have camping included or on site, with pitches a short walk from the gate and sometimes a view of the aircraft arriving. RIAT has official campsites and well known commercial sites such as Townsend Farm nearby. Duxford and the Midlands Air Festival have camping options too, and several other shows have ordinary campsites within a few miles.

Camping is the cheapest way to stay over and the most sociable. It is also the one that depends most on the weather. The camping guide covers everything in detail: which shows have camping, local sites not attached to a show, what to pack, what to buy, budgets and where to find second hand kit.

Touring

Caravans and motorhomes

Most show campsites take them, and Caravan and Motorhome Club and Camping and Caravanning Club sites are often within a few miles. Book early for the big weekends.

Comfort

Glamping and pods

Bell tents, pods and shepherd's huts on farms near the airfields. All the campsite atmosphere with a proper bed. Popular, so book in spring.

Value

Stay further out

Hotels in the nearest city, twenty or thirty miles away, are often half the price of those near the show and have more choice of food. Trade the saving for an earlier start.

Mixed

Travel the night before, drive home after

A hotel the night before gets you to the gate fresh. Driving home after the show is fine if you stay for the quieter car park and share the driving.

Make a week of it

Two shows, one trip

Duxford, Shuttleworth and Little Gransden are close together. RIAT and Cosford both sit within reach of the Cotswolds and Shropshire. A week away can take in two shows.

Free

Friends and family

The cheapest option of all if you have someone within an hour of the show. Offer to bring the breakfast.

Our view

For a first visit to a big show, I would advise a budget hotel near a motorway junction, booked with free cancellation the day the dates come out. Once you know you love it, camping on site is the way to get the most out of a show weekend.
