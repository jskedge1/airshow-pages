Your journey to a better airshow day

I started this guide after getting a few airshow days wrong myself. Arriving with everyone else, carrying too much, standing in the wrong place for the light, then two hours in a car park at the end.

None of it ruined the flying. All of it took the shine off. So here is the whole day laid out like a journey, from before you go to getting home, with a stop for every part of it.

Before you go

Most of a good airshow day is decided before you leave the house. Pick the right show, watch the forecast and pack for both sun and rain.

6 stops
Stop 01

Your first airshow

Start here if you have never been to a show

  • Expect a long day. Gates often open hours before the flying starts.
  • I would advise booking tickets and parking early for the bigger shows, as some sell out.
  • Your child's ears are delicate, so try to protect them as much as possible.
Read the full guide
Stop 02

Which airshow suits you?

Military, historic or seaside

  • Think about how into aviation you are, and who is coming with you.
  • Check whether the show is ticketed or free, and how many days it runs.
  • I would suggest looking at last year's line up to get a feel for the aircraft.
Read the full guide
Stop 03

Weather and will it fly?

What happens when the cloud comes in

  • Low cloud and strong wind can shorten or cancel displays.
  • Grass showgrounds turn muddy after rain, so sturdy footwear beats trainers.
  • The weather on the day will be what it will be. If it's fresh, dress in layers.
Read the full guide
Stop 04

What to pack

Top ten lists for every kind of show

  • Sun cream, a hat and a waterproof, as the weather can change several times in a day.
  • Folding chairs or a blanket, plenty of water, snacks and a power bank.
  • Ear defenders, binoculars and some cash, as card machines can lose signal.
Read the full guide
Stop 05

Places to stay

Hotels, camping and the night before

  • Book early. Hotels and campsites near the big shows fill up months ahead.
  • On site camping works well when you want to beat the morning traffic.
  • Staying further away? Check the journey time on show morning, when roads are busier.
Read the full guide
Stop 06

Questions and answers

Chairs, dogs, tickets and more

  • Folding chairs are allowed at most shows, sometimes behind a certain line.
  • Dogs vary from show to show, and the noise can upset some dogs.
  • Some shows ask you to book accessible parking or send proof before you arrive.
Read the full guide

Getting there

Getting in early and getting out easily. Whether you drive or take the train, the trick is planning the journey home as well as the one there.

2 stops
Stop 07

Getting there

Parking, park and ride and the queue to leave

  • I would advise arriving about an hour before the gates open.
  • Booked parking is often cheaper and closer to the entrance.
  • Park and ride may work for you if you would rather skip the car park queue at the end.
Read the full guide
Stop 08

Public transport

Trains, buses and coaches by show

  • Find the nearest station and whether a shuttle runs on show days.
  • Check the last train and bus times before you go.
  • Engineering works often happen at weekends, so check again a few days before.

On the day

Once you are through the gate. This is where to stand, what to see before the flying, the rules and where the food is.

5 stops
Stop 09

Security and bag searches

How the checks work at the gate

  • Most big shows search bags at the gate, so pack light and keep things easy to open.
  • Bringing a trolley or a big bag? Give yourself extra time at the gate.
  • The queue at the gate builds quickly once the gates open.
Read the full guide
Stop 10

Where to watch

Crowd line, enclosures and photo spots

  • Crowd line spots fill quickly, so head there first if you want a front row seat.
  • Having the sun behind you gives better light and is easier on the eyes.
  • Premium enclosures can offer seating, which may suit you for a comfortable day.
Read the full guide
Stop 11

On the day

Arriving, the static show and the flying

  • Save your pitch first, then look around the static show and the trade stalls.
  • Visit the toilet before the flying starts.
  • Some aircraft are opened up for you to look inside, and there is merch to buy.
Read the full guide
Stop 12

Safety and show rules

What is and is not allowed

  • Most shows ban drones, and many have rules on gazebos, barbecues and dogs.
  • Follow the stewards and stay behind the crowd line.
  • Agree with children what to do if they get lost.
Read the full guide
Stop 13

Food and drink

What is on offer and what to bring

  • Food queues are longest around lunchtime.
  • Rules on bringing your own food and alcohol vary from show to show.
  • I would advise a refillable water bottle, and finding the refill points early.
Read the full guide

Getting home

Everyone leaves at once, so this is the leg most people forget to plan. A little thought here saves hours.

Final leg
Last leg

The short version

Getting home is covered inside the getting there and public transport guides. These are the three things I would always do.

  • Leaving just before the final display, or waiting an hour after the end, can save a long queue.
  • Pack up gradually during the last few displays.
  • Came by train or bus? Allow extra time for the walk back to the stop.

    Our view

    The best airshow days usually come down to a little planning. Knowing when to arrive, where to stand and how you will get home lets you relax and enjoy the flying.

    Start with the route that matches your plans, then look at the show page for the event you are going to for the local detail.

    The Big Guide to UK Airshows is independent and not affiliated with individual airshow organisers unless expressly stated. Event arrangements, aircraft appearances, parking and facilities can change, so confirm final details with the official organiser before setting off.
