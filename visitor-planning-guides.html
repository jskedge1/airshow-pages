Planning your airshow visit

Planning a visit to an airshow can sometimes feel a little stressful. There is plenty to think about, from tickets, travel and parking to what you need to pack and where to stand once you arrive.

That is where this guide can help. It brings together the practical information you may need to plan your visit, avoid a few common mistakes and enjoy more of your day. Every airshow is different, but knowing what to expect before you travel can make a big difference.

Before You Go

Choosing the right airshow

Military, historic or seaside. Find the show that suits you.

  • Think about how into aviation you are. A big military show suits enthusiasts, while a free seafront show may work better for a relaxed family day.
  • Check whether the show is ticketed or free, how many days it runs and whether there is a static display to walk around.
  • I would suggest looking at last year's line up to get a feel for the aircraft that usually attend.
On The Day

Photography at air shows

Settings and kit for flying and static aircraft.

  • Use fast shutter speeds for jets and slower speeds for propeller aircraft, so the blades show some blur.
  • Practise tracking each aircraft smoothly rather than firing off long bursts.
  • I would suggest photographing the static aircraft early, before the crowds build up around them.
Before You Go

Planning your first air show

What to expect, and the common first timer mistakes.

  • Expect a long day. Gates often open hours before the flying starts, and the main display can run well into the afternoon.
  • I would advise booking tickets and parking early for the bigger shows, as some sell out and some do not sell on the gate.
  • Bring ear defenders for children. Fast jets are very loud, especially near the crowd line.
Before You Go

Accommodation & camping

Staying over makes an early start much easier.

  • Book early. Hotels and campsites near the big shows fill up months ahead.
  • On site camping will work well when you want to beat the morning traffic and enjoy any evening events.
  • If you are staying further away, check how long the journey takes on show morning, when the roads are busier.
On The Day

Viewing areas & photography

Where to stand for the best views and photos.

  • Crowd line spots fill quickly at popular shows, so head there first if you want a front row seat.
  • Think about where the sun will be. Having it behind you gives better light and is easier on the eyes.
  • Premium enclosures can offer seating and better facilities, which may suit you if you want a more comfortable day.
Before You Go

Weather & ground conditions

Sun, wind, rain and mud, and how each affects your day.

  • Low cloud and strong wind can shorten or cancel displays, so keep an eye on the forecast during the week.
  • Grass car parks and showgrounds turn muddy after rain. I would advise sturdy footwear rather than trainers.
  • Open airfields have little shade on hot days, so plan where you will cool off and drink more water than you think.
Getting There

Parking and park and ride

Where to leave the car and how early to arrive.

  • Check when the car parks open. I would advise arriving about an hour before the gates open.
  • Many shows sell parking in advance, and booked spaces are often cheaper and closer to the entrance.
  • Park and ride may work for you if you would rather avoid the queue out of the car park at the end.
Getting There

Public transport

Trains, buses and shuttles to the showground.

  • Find the nearest stations and whether the show or a local bus company runs a shuttle on show days.
  • Check the last train and bus times before you go. Services can be very busy after the flying finishes.
  • Engineering works often happen at weekends, so check your journey again a few days before.
On The Day

Planning your day

A simple plan helps you see more and queue less.

  • Check the flying programme when it is published, so you know when the displays you want to see are on.
  • Plan meals and toilet breaks around the displays you are less interested in.
  • Agree a meeting point with your group in case anyone gets separated in the crowds.
Before You Go

Check before you travel

The last minute checks that save a wasted journey.

  • Check the official website and social pages the day before and on the morning. Displays can change or be cancelled.
  • Look for road closures, diversions and the latest advice on which entrance or car park to use.
  • Save your tickets, parking pass and any accessibility confirmation to your phone, and print a backup.
Getting Home

Leaving the airshow

Get home without hours sat in the car park.

  • Leaving just before the final display, or waiting an hour after the end, can save a long queue.
  • Pack up gradually during the last few displays so you are ready to go.
  • If you came by train or bus, allow extra time for the walk to the station or shuttle stop.
On The Day

Safety and show rules

What is and is not allowed on the showground.

  • Most shows ban drones, and many have rules on gazebos, barbecues and dogs. Check before you pack.
  • Follow the stewards, keep walkways clear and stay behind the crowd line.
  • Agree with children what to do if they get lost, and show them where the stewards and information points are.
Before You Go

What to take

A packing list that keeps you comfortable all day.

  • Sun cream, a hat and a waterproof. British summer weather can change several times in one day.
  • Folding chairs or a blanket, plenty of water, snacks and a power bank for your phone.
  • Ear defenders, binoculars and some cash, as card machines can lose signal on busy show sites.
On The Day

Food and drink

What is on offer and what you can bring.

  • Most shows have a wide choice of food stalls, but the queues are longest around lunchtime.
  • Check the rules on bringing your own food, drink and alcohol, as they vary from show to show.
  • I would advise bringing a refillable water bottle and finding the refill points early.

On The Day

Toilets and facilities

Toilets, first aid, accessible facilities and more.

  • Visit the toilet before the flying display starts. Queues build quickly once the flying begins.
  • If you are not waiting to see a particular display, use it as your toilet break.
  • Check the site map for accessible toilets, first aid, baby changing and information points.

On the day

Good to know

Questions visitors often ask before their first show. Rules differ between events, so always check the show you are going to.

Can I take chairs?

Folding chairs are allowed at most shows. Some ask you to keep them behind a certain line so others can see.

Can I bring my own food?

Usually yes, but some shows limit glass and alcohol. Check the show's rules before you pack a picnic.

Are dogs allowed?

It varies. Some shows welcome dogs on leads, others do not allow them at all. The noise can also upset some dogs.

How loud is it?

Fast jets are very loud. I would advise ear defenders for children and anyone who is sensitive to noise.

What about accessibility?

Many shows offer accessible parking and viewing, but some ask you to book or send proof before you arrive.

What if the weather is bad?

Shows usually go ahead in light rain, but low cloud and strong wind can change the flying. Ticket policies vary.

Always check before you travel. Event arrangements, aircraft appearances, parking and facilities can change, so confirm final details with the official organiser before setting off.

Our view

The best airshow days usually come down to a little planning. Knowing when to arrive, where to stand and how you will get home lets you relax and enjoy the flying.

Start with the guide that matches your plans, then look at the show page for the event you are going to for the local detail.
