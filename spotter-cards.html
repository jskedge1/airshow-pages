Tick Off The Aircraft You Spot And Earn Your Badge

  1. Print The Cards Or Keep This Page Open

    Print them at home the night before, or tick them off on a phone or tablet at the show. Ticks save on the device you use, so they will still be there when you come back.

  2. Look Up, And Look Around

    An aircraft counts if you see it flying or parked on the ground. Some aircraft on the static display are opened up for you to take a look inside, and you can buy exclusive merch in or outside the aircraft.

  3. Tick Spotted! When You See One

    Tap the big Spotted! box on the card. On paper, give the box a big tick with a pencil.

  4. Add Up Your Points

    Common aircraft are worth 5 points. Rarer ones are worth 10. The really rare ones are worth 20, and some of those only fly at a handful of shows a year.

  5. Earn Your Badge

    Your badge goes up as your points do. Six badges in all. Can you reach Airshow Legend?

Children love to see the big jets and helicopters up close. Between displays, though, there can be a quiet ten minutes, and that's where a spotting game really helps.

I have been going to RIAT since 2006 and I still get a buzz when something rare turns up. That feeling is what these cards are for.

Not every show has every aircraft. A seaside show like Eastbourne is great for the Red Arrows and the Typhoon. Duxford and Shuttleworth are where the old warbirds and biplanes live. I would suggest a quick look at the line up on your show's page before you go, so you know which cards to hunt for.

Don't worry about filling every card. Nobody does in one day.

The crowd line is the best place for spotting. Keep the cards on your lap between displays.

Your child's ears are delicate, so try to protect them as much as possible. The Typhoon and the F-16 are very loud, even from the back of the crowd. Ear defenders are on my what to pack list.

    1. Press Print The Cards

      Your printer box will open with three pages ready to go. The printed cards come with empty boxes, so children can tick them with a pencil on the day.

    2. Pick A4 Paper And Colour

      Check the scale is set to 100% or Default, so eight cards fit on each page. If your printer asks about background graphics, turn them on so the colours print.

    3. Cut Them Out, Or Keep Whole Sheets

      Cut along the card edges for a pack to swap and share. For little ones, I would suggest keeping the whole sheets on a clipboard. They are much harder to lose.

    Ready when you are. If nothing opens on your phone, open the cards in a new tab and print from there.

    Unfortunately the weather on the day will be what it will be, so plan accordingly. If it's fresh, dress in layers to keep warm. A clear plastic wallet keeps the sheets dry, and a whiteboard pen will tick straight through it.
    I would advise taking a pencil rather than a pen. Pencils still write on damp paper, and you can rub a tick out if someone gets a bit excited and ticks the wrong helicopter.

    Our view

    The rare cards are the best bit. A child who has been hunting for the Blenheim all day will remember the moment it taxis past for years. Think about how into aviation your family is. If a couple of cards and an ice cream is enough, that might work for you too.
