New To Airshows?

I built this brand new airshow guide after getting my own planning wrong. Over the years I have learnt that a day out on my own needs very different planning to a trip with a partner, friends or the family, so I have written every page with that in mind.

Here is how the guide works.

  • Detailed show pages. Parking, timings, where to watch and how the sun moves across the crowd line, for every featured show.
  • What to pack. A checklist that changes with who is coming with you.
  • Photography. The settings I use for jets and propeller aircraft, and my advice on where to stand.
  • My Airshow Hub.New A brand new airshow planner, unlike anything I have seen for UK airshows. Log in to chat with other visitors, share live updates on the day, follow the flight tracker and keep up with live show times.

I would advise starting with who is coming with you. That decides which show will suit you best.

    Your Day, Hour By Hour

    How I would plan a typical show day, from packing the car to backing up your photos. Times change from show to show, so check the timeline on each show page. Scroll down and watch the airfield change through the day.

    06:0009:0012:0015:0018:00
    06:00
    Before You Go
    Final Checks Before You Leave

    My Arrival Tip

    If you want a front row seat, head there first and save your pitch. Then look around. When I last checked, the toilet queues were shortest just before the flying started.

    06:00Final Checks Before You Leave

    Check the official website and social pages for last minute changes, and look at the forecast. I would suggest packing the car the night before if it’s possible, so the morning is calm.

    Before
    Families: Pack snacks, ear defenders for little ones and a change of clothes.
    Photographers: Charge every battery, clear your cards and set your camera before you leave.
    07:00Arrive Before The Car Parks Open

    I would suggest getting there about an hour before the car parks open. The roads in are the slowest part of the whole day.

    Busiest roads of the day
    Families: Toilet stop on the way. Queues at the gate do not move fast.
    08:00Gates Open

    Have your tickets ready on your phone with the brightness turned up. Check what you can bring in before you go, as some shows have rules on gazebos and chairs at the front.

    Photographers: Expect a bag check. Keep lenses in one easy to open bag.
    08:30Save Your Spot On The Crowd Line

    Head to the crowd line first if you want the front row. Put down your chairs and blanket, then you can relax.

    Families: Pick a spot near the toilets and food if you have young children.
    Photographers: Check the sun guide on the show page. I like the sun behind me for photos.
    09:00Static Aircraft And Pilots

    Walk the static show while it is quiet. This is when you can speak to pilots and ground crew.

    Families: Kids can often climb into cockpits and collect stickers here.
    Photographers: Best time for clean static shots before the crowds arrive.
    10:00RAF Village And Activities

    Most bigger shows have an RAF Village with simulators, displays and the chance to meet display team pilots.

    Great for kids
    Families: Queues for photos with pilots build quickly after 11:00.
    11:00Stalls, Food And Toilets

    Check the trade stalls and food options early. Food queues are longest around lunchtime, so I eat early or late.

    11:45Back To Your Spot

    Settle in before the flying starts. Visit the toilet now, before the flying display begins.

    Families: Hand out the ear defenders now, before the first jet.
    Photographers: Do a few test shots on passing birds or the commentary tower to check your settings.
    12:00Flying Display Begins

    If you are not waiting to see a particular display, use it as your toilet or food break. I see others do this and it works well.

    Display time
    Photographers: Jets need fast shutter speeds; props need slow ones for blurred blades. My photography pages explain both.
    13:30Lunch Break Your Way

    Take lunch during a display you are less keen on. Keep watching from your spot if you can, as you can eat and watch.

    15:00Headline Acts

    Big names such as the Red Arrows often fly in the afternoon, but every show is different, so check the timetable on the day.

    Do not miss
    Photographers: Two camera bodies help here: one long lens for passes, one wider for formations and smoke.
    16:30Stay Warm, Stay Covered

    Airfields are open and windy. Reapply sun cream, or put a layer on as the breeze picks up.

    Families: Small children tire around now. A blanket and a snack go a long way.
    17:00Final Displays

    The last displays are often some of the best. Some people start leaving now to beat the traffic.

    Photographers: The evening light can be lovely if the show runs late.
    17:30Plan Your Way Home

    Leaving early or waiting an hour both beat sitting in the main queue. Each show page has the last trains and buses.

    Traffic peak
    19:00Home And Sorted

    Kettle on. If you took photos, back them up the same night and share your favourites in the community forum.

    Photographers: Back up the same night, before you format any cards.
