Which airshow suits you?

Who is your audience, and what kind of day do they want?

There is no best airshow, only the best airshow for you and the people you are bringing. Decide who the day is for first, then choose the show, not the other way round.

Ticketed

Airfield shows

RIAT, Cosford and Duxford. You pay to get in, you stand on grass along a crowd line with the runway in front of you, and the aircraft taxi past, take off and land in front of you. The static show is the big extra: dozens of aircraft on the ground to walk around.

  • Best for seeing aircraft up close
  • Biggest line ups and rarest visitors
  • Long walks from car park to crowd line
  • Toilets, food and shade are all some distance away
Free

Seafront shows

Eastbourne, Clacton, Wales, Blackpool, Southport and Ayr. Free to watch, with the display over the sea and the town behind you. There is usually an RAF Village, trade stands, funfair rides and the town's own cafés and shops.

  • Best for families and first visits
  • No ticket needed and food everywhere
  • No static aircraft to walk around
  • Crowded promenades and tricky parking
Relaxed

Vintage and community shows

Shuttleworth at Old Warden and Little Gransden. Small grass airfields, historic aircraft flown close and low, and a relaxed feel. Shuttleworth also runs evening shows.

  • Best for warbirds and old aeroplanes
  • Close to the action on a small field
  • Very weather dependent flying
  • Fewer facilities and smaller line ups

The best show for you depends on who you are bringing. A keen enthusiast and a family with a toddler will both enjoy an airshow, but they will not enjoy the same airshow in the same way.

If you are bringing young children, I would advise a seafront show first. It is free, the day can be as long or as short as you like, there are rides and ice cream, and if it all gets too much you can walk away without feeling you wasted a ticket.

If you are an enthusiast, or you are bringing one, an airfield show gives you the static display, the taxiing and the line up. RIAT is the big one. Duxford and Shuttleworth are where I would start for warbirds.

If you are bringing grandparents or anyone who finds long walks hard, check the show page for the walk from the car park and the accessible parking. It varies more than you might think.

The Red Arrows appear at most big shows. Check the line up on the show page before you book.

Families with young children

Seafront shows, or an airfield show with a short day planned. Bring a buggy that copes with grass and plan around naps and food.

Start with

Teenagers and non aviation fans

Shows with plenty on the ground: an RAF Village, simulators, cars, funfair, trade stands. Eastbourne and Cosford do this well.

Start with

Aviation enthusiasts

RIAT for the military line up, Duxford and Shuttleworth for warbirds and vintage aircraft. Read the line up pages and the news page.

Start with

First time visitors

Any of the free seafront shows, or Cosford as a first airfield show. Both are easy to reach by public transport.

  1. How into aviation are you?

    If you want to name every aircraft, go to an airfield show. If you want a nice day out with some flying in it, a seafront show will suit you better.

  2. Who is coming with you?

    Children, grandparents, a partner who is not that bothered. Plan for the least keen person and the keenest will still have a good day.

  3. How far will you travel?

    A show two hours away means an early start and a late finish. The accommodation guide covers staying over, which turns a long day into an easy weekend.

  4. What is your budget?

    Seafront shows are free. Airfield show tickets, parking, food and fuel add up. The food and drink guide has a budget calculator.

  5. Do you want to see the aircraft on the ground?

    Only airfield shows have a static display. If walking around a Chinook or a Typhoon is part of the appeal, that decides it.

  6. How would you feel if the weather turned?

    Warbird and vintage shows are the most affected by cloud, wind and rain. Jets and helicopters keep flying in weather that grounds a Spitfire. The weather guide explains why.

I would suggest reading the "Is this show right for you?" section on each featured show page once you have a shortlist. It gives an honest verdict for families, children, teenagers, enthusiasts and first timers for that particular show.

Featured shows compared

Royal International Air TattooTicketed. Biggest line up in the UK. Park and ride, long days, huge static. Enthusiasts and confident families.
RAF Cosford Air ShowTicketed. Good first airfield show. Compact, reachable by train, photographers' enclosure. Slow exit.
IWM Duxford Air ShowsTicketed. Warbirds and the museum on site. Several shows a year. Famous queue to leave.
Shuttleworth Air ShowsTicketed. Vintage aircraft flown close. Evening shows. Most weather dependent.
Eastbourne, Clacton, Wales, Blackpool, Southport, AyrFree. Seafront, town facilities, RAF Village, funfair. Families and first visits.
Midlands Air FestivalTicketed. Flying plus balloons and an evening show. Families and campers.

Our view

If you are not sure, I would advise a free seafront show for a first visit. You lose nothing if the day is not for you, and if you love it, your next show can be one of the big airfield events with the ticket money well spent.
