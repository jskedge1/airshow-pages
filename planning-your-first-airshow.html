Planning your first airshow

My guide for first time visitors, and for anyone who wants a bit more from their day

If this is your first airshow, or even your fifteenth, I find a little planning can turn a good day into a really great one. I have put together everything I wish someone had told me before my first show, split into nine short guides you can read in any order.

Why plan at all?

Most of what goes wrong on show day is decided before you leave the house

I'd say an airshow is one of the best days out in the UK. It is also a long day in a big field with a lot of other people, and it's the small things that decide how it goes, like when you arrive, how you get in, where you stand, what you carry and how you leave.

I started this guide after getting those things wrong myself. I have arrived at the same time as everyone else, carried too much, stood in the wrong place for the light and sat in a car park for two hours at the end. None of it ruins the flying, but it does take the shine off the day.

The nine guides below take you through each part of the day in plain English. I have written them for anyone, from first timers to people who have been to a few shows and want to make more of the day. Where a featured show has its own detailed section, for example on parking or public transport, I point you to the show page so you have it all in one place.

The crowd line fills from the front. The people with chairs just got there first.

I would suggest thinking about how into aviation you are before you plan anything else. A keen enthusiast and a family with two young children can both have a brilliant day, but they will plan it very differently.

Check before you travel

The one habit that saves more days than any other

Airshows can change right up to the morning of the show. Aircraft drop out, car parks move, roads close for the event and trains get altered for engineering work. I would suggest five minutes of checking the night before and again before you set off. This has worked for me in the past more than any other planning.

Open before you leave

Google Maps

Every featured show page has a link that opens the car park or the show entrance in Google Maps. I would open it before you leave, check the live traffic and let it route you around anything that has happened since you last looked.

Rail

Train disruption

I would advise checking the operator's app or National Rail Enquiries for engineering work, strikes and cancellations. Summer weekends are quite a common time for track work, and a rail replacement bus can add an hour to your trip.

Roads

Road closures

Seafront shows close the promenade and nearby roads, and airfield shows run one way systems and temporary signs. You'll usually find them listed on the official website and the local council's traffic page, so I would take a look at both.

Buses

Bus and coach changes

Local bus routes are often diverted or stop short on show days. I list the services and the operator on each show page, so I would check their website for the show day timetable.

Official

The official website and social pages

The organiser's site and Facebook page carry the latest news on timings, aircraft, parking and weather. On the morning of the show, this is where I see any changes appear first.

The shape of a show day

A typical day, so nothing comes as a surprise

Every show is a bit different, but the day has the same shape almost everywhere. The times here are typical rather than exact, so I would check your show page for the real ones.

  1. Gates open
    Usually 08:00 to 09:00

    Car parks open an hour or so before the gates. If you want a front row spot, I would advise arriving about an hour before the gates open.

  2. First hour
    Save your pitch, then look around

    I would head to the crowd line first and save your pitch. Then you can walk round the static aircraft, speak to the pilots and crews, take a look at the trade stalls and check the food options.

  3. Late morning
    Flying usually starts 11:00 to 13:00

    I would suggest visiting the toilet before the flying begins. If you are not waiting for a particular aircraft, you can use a display you are less interested in as your toilet break.

  4. Afternoon
    The main displays

    You'll find the headline acts, display teams and warbirds tend to fly from early afternoon. This is the part of the day when I like to stay put with my drinks and snacks to hand.

  5. Finale
    Usually 17:00 to 18:00

    When the flying ends, everyone leaves at once, and some shows are known for a slow exit. I like to stay a while, let the traffic clear and walk out when it is quieter. You can try it yourself as it may suit you.

My View

You do not need to know anything about aircraft to enjoy an airshow. What I would advise is arriving in good time, knowing where you are going to stand and having a plan for food, toilets and getting home. If you get those right, I find the flying takes care of itself.
