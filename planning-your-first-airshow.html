Your route

Four stops to your first show

Start with which show suits you, then getting there, then what to pack. Those three answer almost every question a first time visitor asks me. Keep your plans for the flying loose, and give yourself more time than you think for the journey in and out.

  1. Start

    Home

    Read them in order. A few evenings before the show works well.

  2. 1
    Stop 1, before you go

    Which airshow suits you?

    • Think about how into aviation you are, and who is coming with you.
    • Check whether the show is ticketed or free, and how many days it runs.
    • I would suggest looking at last year's line up to get a feel for the aircraft.
    Read the guide
  3. 2
    Stop 2, getting there

    Getting there

    • I would advise arriving about an hour before the gates open.
    • Booked parking is often cheaper and closer to the entrance.
    • Park and ride may work for you if you would rather skip the car park queue at the end.
    Read the guide
  4. 3
    Stop 3, before you go

    What to pack

    • Sun cream, a hat and a waterproof, as the weather can change several times in a day.
    • Folding chairs or a blanket, plenty of water, snacks and a power bank.
    • Ear defenders, binoculars and some cash, as card machines can lose signal.
    Read the guide
  5. 4
    Stop 4, on the day

    On the day

    • Save your pitch first, then look around the static show and the trade stalls.
    • Visit the toilet before the flying starts.
    • Some aircraft are opened up for you to look inside, and there is merch to buy.
    Read the guide
  6. Arrive

    Your first airshow

My advice

If it’s your first time, I would advise going to a seafront airshow first, or a smaller event like Little Gransden or even Cosford, to see if you like airshows. Make sure you read our guides to help you plan. Don’t take shortcuts, because planning will make your day so much smoother.

Check before you travel

Airshows change right up to the morning of the show. Five minutes of checking the night before, and again before you set off, is the best planning you can do.

Open before you leaveGoogle Maps

Every featured show page has a link that opens the car park or entrance in Google Maps. Check the live traffic and let it route you around anything that has changed.

RailTrain disruption

Check the operator's app or National Rail Enquiries for engineering work, strikes and cancellations. A rail replacement bus can add an hour.

RoadsRoad closures

Seafront shows close the promenade and nearby roads, and airfield shows run one way systems. The official website and the council's traffic page list them.

BusesBus and coach changes

Local buses are often diverted or stop short on show days. The show page lists the services, so check the operator's show day timetable.

OfficialThe official website and social pages

The organiser's site and Facebook page carry the latest on timings, aircraft, parking and weather. Changes appear here first.

GuideOur airshow news page

I check the official websites every day and post what has changed, so you can see at a glance if anything has moved.

My check before you travel routine

1The night before

Official website, airshow news, weather forecast, tickets and parking passes on your phone

2Before you set off

Google Maps for live traffic, train app for disruption, show Facebook page for morning updates

3On arrival

Note where you parked, find the toilets and agree a meeting point

Your day and your questions

The two things first timers ask me most

Gates usually open between 08:00 and 09:00, the flying tends to start between 11:00 and 13:00, and it finishes around 17:00 to 18:00. The full day, and the questions people send me, each have their own page.

Our view

You do not need to know anything about aircraft to enjoy an airshow. You do need to arrive in good time, know where you are going to stand and have a plan for food, toilets and getting home. Get those right and the flying takes care of itself.
