The shows at a glance

IWM Duxford is a real Battle of Britain airfield south of Cambridge, and home to the largest collection of airworthy historic aircraft in Europe. Every show ticket also gets you into the museum hangars, so plan time for both.

Duxford runs two Big two day Air Shows, the Summer Air Show in June and the Battle of Britain Air Show in September. These are the busy ones, with the largest line-ups, a flightline walk in the morning, traders and food vendors, and a free shuttle bus from Cambridge. Parking at these two needs a permit booked with your ticket.

Around them sit three themed Flying Days, a Flying Evening built around sunset and a Flying Finale to close the season. They're smaller and calmer, parking needs no booking, and the Flying Days are free for IWM members. I would suggest a Big Air Show for your first visit, then one of the smaller events if you want a quieter day with the same aircraft.

The museum

Every Duxford show ticket also gets you into the museum, with hangars full of historic aircraft, and the American Air Museum. It’s a real Battle of Britain airfield, so you’re walking through history before the first aircraft starts up.

It’s a great way for your kids to learn about the different roles in the armed forces, past and present, and the hangars are the best shade on the site on a hot day. I would suggest planning a couple of hours for the museum around the flying.

    Our view

    Few places match Duxford for historic aircraft. Watching a Spitfire scramble from the same grass it flew from in 1940 is something special, and the hangars give you a full day out before the flying starts. I would advise the Battle of Britain Air Show if you love warbirds, and the Summer Air Show for a broader mix. If what excites you is fast jets and military noise, I would suggest pairing Duxford with RIAT or the RAF Air Show at Cosford.

    IWM Duxford sits right beside junction 10 of the M11, south of Cambridge. At the Summer and Battle of Britain Air Shows every car needs a parking permit booked with your ticket, and a free shuttle bus runs from Cambridge station and Trumpington Park & Ride. At the smaller events parking is on site with no booking.

    How arrival works

    At the two big Air Shows a traffic plan is in place on the roads around Duxford. Here's what to expect, so a short wait in the queue doesn't catch you out.

    1

    Follow the event signs

    The car parks open at 07:00, so I would advise arriving about an hour before entry starts. As you leave the M11 at junction 10, follow the yellow event signs and the marshals rather than your sat nav, and have your parking permit ready to show.

    3

    Gates open at 08:00

    Ticket scanning starts at 08:00. Have your ticket ready on your phone and allow time for bag checks. If you want a front row spot, head to the crowd line first and save your pitch, then explore.

    IWM recommends arriving before 11:00. Parking is on grass close to the historic buildings, and the walk from the main car parks to the showground is only a few minutes and well signposted. At the Flying Days, Flying Evening and Flying Finale parking is on site with no permit needed.

    Ways to arrive

    Driving, the train and the free Air Show shuttle, local buses and taxis. Times and fares change, so check them before you travel.

    Do I need to book parking?
    Yes for the Summer Air Show and the Battle of Britain Air Show. Every car needs a parking permit, booked when you buy your tickets, and spaces are limited, so you can't turn up and park without one. At the Flying Days, the Flying Evening and the Flying Finale, parking is on site with no booking needed. Motorbikes park free in the visitor centre car park with no permit.
    Is there parking for Blue Badge holders?
    Yes. Anyone with mobility issues can park in the main car park, but proof needs to be provided to park there, and staff will be on hand to answer any questions. At the two big Air Shows, book an accessible parking permit with your ticket and bring your Blue Badge (or international equivalent) on the day. If you haven't been before, you'll see a Hurricane on a pole at the main gate: this is the way in for anyone with a disabled badge or mobility issues. A wheelchair accessible shuttle bus runs from the accessible parking to the main showground.
    Should I follow my sat nav?
    Only until you're close. IWM runs a special traffic plan on Air Show weekends, so once you leave the M11 at junction 10, follow the event signs and the marshals instead.
    Will there be traffic?
    Yes, on the big Air Show days. Traffic builds on the M11 slip roads and the A505 from about 08:00, so arriving around 07:00 makes a real difference. Check National Highways for road works before you set off.
    How do I get home easily?
    It's very common for leaving a Duxford Air Show to take a long time. Either leave before the flying display ends, or, if you're able to, stay on: look around the static displays and the other museum hangars, grab some food and wait for the rush to leave. The site stays open until 19:00 at the big Air Shows. If you're on the free shuttle, the last bus leaves at 18:30.

    Where to stay

    Hotels and campsites within about 25 miles of Duxford, from Whittlesford and Cambridge to Royston and Saffron Walden. Rooms go quickly on the Big Air Show weekends, so I would advise booking as soon as you have your tickets.

      Pick your spot

      The crowd line runs along the grass on the north side of the runway, so the whole length of it is open to everyone. What changes is how close you are to show centre, how busy it is, and how far you are from the Visitor Centre, food and toilets.

      Photographers’ map

      Every good place to watch and photograph the flying, inside the show and around the airfield, with where the sun sits through the day. Teal spots are inside the show, purple spots are outside the fence.

      Inside the show

      1. 1
        Crowd line centre

        Opposite show centre, with the commentary and the Visitor Centre food close by. Light: into the sun around midday, sun behind your right shoulder from about 16:00.

      2. 2
        East end fence, by the grass runway

        Walk out from the shop towards the fence, with the small hangars on your left. Close landings on the grass and aircraft turning tightly to join the display line. Light: gets better as the afternoon goes on.

      3. 3
        Control tower and the flightline

        Clean shots of aircraft parked and taxiing on the big pan past the tower, and helicopters often land here. The paid flightline walk at the Big Air Shows gets you closer. Light: into the sun in the morning and early afternoon.

      4. 4
        The Tank Bank, west end

        Past the American Air Museum towards the far west end of the site. The closest you’ll get to the paved runway, with aircraft taxiing within feet and tailchases coming in overhead. The grass bank gives you extra height, but it gets busy and there’s little room for a big lens.

      Around the airfield

      1. 5
        Farm track, in line with the runway

        Off the A505 just after the Thriplow turn coming from Royston. Good for approaches to 06 and take offs from 24, mostly undersides. Usually closed to cars on show days, so walk or cycle in. No parking, and the track can be very muddy.

      2. 6
        Road along the south side

        Pull offs along the road past the T-junction at the end of the farm track. A good view over the airfield and some aircraft pass close on the downwind leg, but it’s too far for most photos.

      3. 7
        Public footpath towards the M11

        A public footpath round the field edge from Duxford village towards the 24 end. Good for approaches to 24, but it’s under the approach path and not the safest place to stand. There’s no parking on the village road on show days.

      Spots outside the fence are not official viewing areas, so there’s no commentary, toilets or food, and you can be moved on. Never enter fields without the farmer’s permission, and drones aren’t allowed. Spots based on the Thunder & Lightnings airfield viewing guide.

      Shooting Duxford

      The crowd faces south across the runway, so the sun is to your left in the morning, ahead of you around midday and swings round to your right through the afternoon. That makes the morning flightline walk and the Flying Evening the best light at Duxford, and the quieter west end the place for close taxi shots.

      Get creative on the airfield

      Duxford gives you a real Battle of Britain airfield as your backdrop, with hangars, the control tower and grass under the aircraft. The crowd faces the sun around midday, so I would suggest the flightline walk and the hangars in the morning, side light in the early afternoon, and the best flying shots from about 16:00.

      Ideas to try

      • Slow the shutter for the warbirds. 1/160 to 1/250 gives a proper prop disc, so pan smoothly and take a few frames each pass.
      • Head to the Tank Bank at the west end for take offs and aircraft taxiing within feet of you.
      • Wait for the Big Wing and the formation passes, and zoom out so the formation fills the frame with sky around it.
      • Include the hangars or the control tower now and then, so the photo says Duxford.
      • Use the photographers’ map in the Where to watch section to pick your spot for the light.

      Food, facilities & what to expect

      IWM Duxford has several places to enjoy food and refreshments throughout the museum. Whether you're looking for a hot meal, a quick sandwich or a coffee, there are options available during your visit.

      Food & drink

      There are three places to eat in the museum, and during the big Air Shows extra outdoor food vendors are spread across the site, offering everything from burgers and hot dogs to snacks, ice cream and refreshments. Licensed bars serve craft beer, cider, wine and soft drinks. Locations and availability may vary between events.

      • Spitfire Café ↗ Near the main Visitor Centre and museum entrance. A convenient place to stop when arriving or before leaving, with hot meals, sandwiches, cakes, snacks and hot and cold drinks.
      • 19 Squadron Restaurant ↗ In the central area of the museum, near the historic hangars. A good option for a lunch break while visiting the surrounding exhibitions, with cooked meals and refreshments.
      • American Air Museum Café ↗ At the American Air Museum, towards the western end of the site. A convenient stop when visiting the American Air Museum, with lighter refreshments including sandwiches, cakes and hot and cold drinks.
      • Bringing your own food. Picnics are welcome and a great way to save money, particularly for families. Bring plenty of drinking water and a few snacks, especially if you plan to spend most of the day watching the flying. Free water refill points are around the site.

      Our advice: food outlets get busy during the big Air Shows, particularly around lunchtime. Consider eating a little earlier or later to avoid the queues. Opening times and food options may vary, so we recommend checking the official IWM Duxford website before your visit.

      Getting around

      Duxford's own shuttle

      It’s around 700 metres from the shop to the American Air Museum. Duxford has its own shuttle that takes people up and down the airfield, which is a real help with little legs or tired ones. At the big Air Shows a wheelchair accessible shuttle bus also runs from the accessible parking to the main showground, and on museum days a free site vehicle with room for a manual wheelchair is available on request.

      What to expect on the day

      Toilets

      Accessible toilets across the site, with extra units and baby changing at the Air Shows. A Changing Places toilet with a shower is in Hangar 1, opened with a Radar key from staff or volunteers.

      Shade & weather

      There's very little shade on the crowd line. Small shade tents for babies are fine if they don't block views; large tents and gazebos aren't. The hangars give shelter from sun and rain.

      Paying

      The museum cafés take cards. There's no cashpoint on site, and some traders only take cash, so bring a little with you.

      Banned items

      Barbecues, drones, step ladders and stools, large tents and gazebos, children's scooters, balls and balloons, flags and banners, replica weapons and art supplies.

      Accessibility

      Accessible parking. Anyone with mobility issues can park in the main car park, but proof needs to be provided to park there. Staff will be on hand to answer any questions. At the two big Air Shows, book an accessible parking permit with your ticket and bring your Blue Badge (or international equivalent) on the day.

      Finding it. If you haven't been before, you'll see a Hurricane on a pole at the main gate. This is the way in for anyone with a disabled badge or mobility issues. The main car park for the shows is signposted, and the walk is only a few minutes.

      Viewing. There's a wheelchair viewing area for visitors and their personal assistants, with accessible toilets nearby, and an accessible quiet zone beyond the American Air Museum at the west end, away from the main crowd line. The flightline is on level tarmac.

      Mobility scooters and wheelchairs. At the big Air Shows, electric scooters (£35) and manual wheelchairs (£15) can be booked in advance through Event Mobility. On museum days, wheelchairs and scooters are free to borrow, but numbers are limited, so book ahead.

      Assistance dogs are welcome. Other dogs aren't allowed on site at the Air Shows, including in the car parks.

      Dogs
      Only guide and assistance dogs are allowed at the Air Shows. This includes the car parks, so leave pet dogs at home.
      Lost children
      Agree a meeting point when you arrive, such as the control tower or a hangar, and write your phone number on younger children's wrists.
      If it rains
      Historic aircraft are sensitive to wind and low cloud, so some displays may be shortened or cancelled. The hangars and the American Air Museum give shelter. Check IWM Duxford's social media on the morning, and pack a waterproof whatever the forecast says.
      Programmes
      Programmes cost £8 in advance or £10 on the day while stock lasts. Pre-ordered items are collected on site by showing your email confirmation.
