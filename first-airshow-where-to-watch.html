Where to watch

The free crowd line, the paid enclosures and the best spots for the light

Where you stand decides what you see, what you hear and what you photograph. The front row is free if you get there first. The enclosures buy you comfort, not a better view.

At an airfield show the crowd line is the rope or fence that runs along the length of the display, with the runway and the display line beyond it. Every ticket gets you to the crowd line. The front row is first come, first served.

If you want a front row seat, head there first and save your pitch with your chairs and a blanket before you do anything else. Then go and look around the static show, speak to the pilots, visit the trade stalls and check the food. Your spot will still be there.

The centre of the crowd line, opposite the display centre, is where the aircraft perform their main passes. The ends are quieter and give you aircraft taxiing, taking off and landing closer. I would suggest thinking about what you most want to see. Loops and formations favour the centre, take offs and landings favour the ends.

At a seafront show the promenade is the crowd line, with the display over the water. The beach itself is often the best spot, and the higher ground or cliff tops give a wider view of the whole display.

A front row pitch saved early with chairs and a blanket.

I would advise a low folding chair rather than a tall one if you are at the front. It keeps the view clear for the people behind you and it is lighter to carry.

Most of the bigger shows sell enclosures, which are fenced areas on the crowd line with extras included. They cost more on top of the standard ticket. Whether they are worth it depends on what you want from the day.

Perks

What you usually get

A reserved area so you do not need to arrive early, seating, private toilets with shorter queues, sometimes a marquee for shade and rain, and sometimes food and a programme. The exact mix is different at every show.

Trade offs

What you give up

Cost, first of all. Enclosures can double the price of the day. You are also fixed in one place, you may be further from the static display, and some are busy with people who are not there for the flying.

Enclosures at the featured shows

Royal International Air TattooSeveral enclosures with seating, toilets and catering, plus the FRIAT photographers' enclosure with elevated views and early access. Park and View areas on the approaches are a cheaper alternative with a different view.
RAF Cosford Air ShowA photographers' enclosure on the crowd line with a raised position and room to work, plus hospitality options. Books out early.
IWM Duxford Air ShowsReserved and hospitality areas on the crowd line, with the Tank Bank and the museum buildings giving different views across the field.
Shuttleworth Air ShowsA small field where most of the crowd line is close to the action. Premium seating and picnic areas are offered for some shows.
Seafront showsGrandstand seats and hospitality on the promenade at some of them, such as Eastbourne and Bournemouth. Most of the seafront is free.

It suits you if

Maybe not

Think again if

You are on a budget, you like to wander, you want to spend the day in the static display, or you are happy to arrive early and claim a free front row spot.

Camera

Photographers

The photographers' enclosures at RIAT and Cosford give you a raised position, fewer heads in shot and room for a tripod. If you are serious about the pictures, they are the best value extras at any show.

Pick your spot by the light. The sun guide on each show page shows which side is lit at each time of day.

If you are bringing a camera, where you stand decides your pictures. Sun behind you lights the aircraft. Sun in front of you gives you silhouettes. Most UK shows fly on a display line north of the crowd, which means the sun is behind you for much of the afternoon, but not all of them.

Every featured show page has a sun guide that shows the sun's position across the day, which side of the display line is lit, and the best spots on the crowd line. Read it before you choose your pitch.

You do not always need to be inside. RIAT sells Park and View tickets on the approaches to the runway, which are cheaper than the showground and give you aircraft arriving and departing right over your head, but no static display and limited facilities. Other airfield shows have well known spots on nearby roads and hills where regulars watch for free, although these are often closed or policed on show day, so check locally.

Seafront shows are free along the whole promenade, so watching from outside is the normal way to do it. The question there is where. The beach puts you closest, the cliffs and higher ground give you the whole display in one view, and the far ends of the promenade are quieter.

A word on the roads. Stopping on a road or a verge to watch is dangerous and the police move people on. Use the official Park and View areas or a proper viewpoint.

Toilets at an airfield show are portable units in blocks along the crowd line and near the food. The queue builds in the breaks between displays and at lunchtime. Go before the flying starts, and if you are not waiting for a particular aircraft, use a display you are less interested in as your toilet break.

Shade is rare on an airfield. Trees are a long way from the crowd line and marquees are usually inside the enclosures. Bring your own in the form of a hat, sun cream and a light long sleeved top. Water refill points exist at some shows, so check the site map.

Toilet blocks sit in rows along the crowd line. Find the nearest one when you arrive.

Our view

For most visitors, I would advise arriving early, saving a free front row pitch and spending the money on food and a drink instead. If you need a seat and a quiet toilet, or you are serious about photographs, the right enclosure is worth it. Read the show page before you decide.
