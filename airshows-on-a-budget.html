Airshows on a budget

A day at an airshow doesn't have to cost a fortune. This is how I keep the cost down for a family day out, from picking the right show to what to leave at home and what is actually worth paying for.

Free shows

If money is tight, a seaside show is the place to start. There are no tickets and no gates, so you can just turn up, find a spot on the prom or the beach and watch the whole display for nothing.

Free doesn't mean nothing to payParking, seating areas, the programme and the funfair all cost money, and they can add up quickly. I would suggest deciding before you go what you are happy to spend on. The free shows also rely on donations and programme sales to carry on, so if you can, do pop something in a bucket.

Saving on tickets

For the airfield shows like RIAT, Duxford and Cosford you will need a ticket. That said, there are a few easy ways to pay less for one.

1

Book as early as you can

Most shows have early bird prices, and the price can go up as the show gets closer. Try to book when tickets first go on sale. Keep an eye on the airshow calendar so you know when that is.

2

Check what children pay

Some shows let younger children in for free or for a lot less. That would be worth looking at before you choose between two shows, as it can really change the cost of a family day.

3

Think about one day, not two

A weekend at a big show is great, but one well planned day can give you most of the flying. How about picking the day with the aircraft you most want to see?

4

Look at memberships

If you go to the same place a few times a year, a membership can work out cheaper than separate tickets. Please see the rules before you go, as some shows still need a show day ticket on top.

Getting there for less

Getting there can easily cost more than the show itself, so it's well worth a bit of thought.

Share the car. Four people in one car costs the same in parking as one person, so this can be the biggest saving of the day. The forum is a good place to find others going to the same show.

Use a railcard. If you go by train, a Family and Friends Railcard takes money off adult fares and a lot off children's fares. Book in advance where you can, as it's usually cheaper than buying on the day.

Compare the parking. The nearest car park is not always the cheapest. A park and ride or a car park a short walk away might work out better, and you often get away quicker at the end too.

Food and drink

This is where most of the money goes on the day. Buying lunch, snacks and drinks for a family at a show can easily cost more than the tickets.

1

Bring a picnic

Most shows let you bring your own food. Sandwiches, fruit and a few treats for the kids will save you quite a lot. I would still treat yourself to one thing from the food village, as it's part of the day.

2

Refill your water bottle

Bring a refillable bottle each and look out for the water points. On a hot day this saves a small fortune on bottled water.

3

Check the bag rules first

Some shows don't allow glass or alcohol, and every show has a bag search. Have a quick look at my security guide so nothing gets taken off you at the gate.

4

Pack for the weather

Unfortunately the weather on the day will be what it will be, so plan accordingly. If it's fresh, dress in layers to keep warm. Bringing your own suncream and a coat saves buying them at the show.

Jack’s tipEat your lunch while a display you are not too bothered about is flying. The food queues are shorter, and you won't miss the ones you came for. My food and drink guide has more on what you can find at each show.

Worth paying for

Saving money is great, but there are a few things I would always spend on. How would this affect you? Have a think about what matters most to your group.

I would pay for

  • Ear defenders for childrenYour child’s ears are delicate, so try to protect them as much as possible. A basic pair doesn't cost much and lasts for years.
  • The programmeIt tells you what is flying and when, it makes a nice keepsake, and at the free shows it helps keep them going.
  • A good camping chairYou will be sitting for hours. Second hand ones on Facebook Marketplace or Vinted can be a real bargain.

I would happily skip

  • Buying every meal at the showBring your own and keep one treat for the day.
  • Bottled waterA refillable bottle does the same job for nothing.
  • Kit you will only use onceBorrow a pair of binoculars or a camera lens from a friend before buying your own.
A little treatSome of the aircraft on the ground are opened up for you to take a look inside, and that is free. You can often buy exclusive merch in or beside the aircraft too. That might work as the one souvenir of the day, so maybe set the kids a small budget for it before you go.
