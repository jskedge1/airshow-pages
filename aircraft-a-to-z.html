This is my list of aircraft you can actually see at UK airshows, from the Red Arrows to a First World War Camel at Old Warden. Under “Seen at” I have only listed a featured show where I know the aircraft displayed in the 2025 or 2026 season. So it is a guide to what turns up, not a promise for next summer.

Use the letters to jump about, type a name or a team into the search box, or pick a filter. Some aircraft sit under two filters, so a Chinook shows up under modern military and under helicopters.

I would suggest searching for a team you have heard of, like Navy Wings or the Battle of Britain Memorial Flight, and seeing everything they fly. It is a quick way to work out which show suits you and your family.

If you are new to this, you might only tell a jet from a propeller at first. That is fine. You pick it up quicker than you think, and it makes the day a lot more fun, especially for children who like to shout out the answer first.

The commentator will name every display as it starts, so you are never left guessing for long. These are the things I look and listen for in the meantime.

Two afterburners and canards by the cockpit. That one is a Typhoon.

  1. Listen before you look

    A Merlin engine has a smooth, deep growl you will hear in Spitfires, Hurricanes, the Lancaster and Mustangs. Big radials like the Thunderbolt and Bearcat sound rougher and lumpier. The Harvard has a harsh rasp all of its own, from propeller tips going supersonic.

  2. Count the engines and the rotors

    Two rotors means a Chinook. Three engines and five blades means a Merlin. A Lancaster has four engines, a Blenheim and a Dakota have two. It sounds obvious, but it narrows things down fast.

  3. Look at the wing shape

    The Spitfire’s rounded wing is the giveaway. The Hurricane is chunkier with a hump behind the cockpit. With modern jets, the Typhoon has its intake under the nose, the Rafale has intakes on each side and the Gripen is smaller with a single engine.

  4. Watch the smoke

    Red, white and blue is the Red Arrows. Green, white and red is the Frecce Tricolori. Most civil teams and solo aerobatic pilots use white smoke, so look at the aircraft itself.

  5. Use the programme and the static

    Buy or download the show programme if there is one, and take a look at the static aircraft in the morning. Seeing one on the ground first makes it much easier to pick out later.

How about this for younger ones. Pick a couple of aircraft from the A to Z before you go and make it a game to spot them. That might work for your family on the long drive home too.

Big helicopters on the static are always busy, so get there early.

Plenty of the aircraft in the A to Z sit on the static display for the day, and that is where you get close. Some aircraft are opened up for you to take a look inside, and you can buy exclusive merch in or outside the aircraft. The crews are usually standing right by them and are happy to talk, so I would advise you to ask questions. I see others walk straight past the crews, which is a shame, as they often have the best stories.

Where the static is depends on the show. Cosford has retired jets like the Tornado and Jaguar in its hangars, Duxford has its museum hangars full of aircraft and Old Warden lets you walk among the Shuttleworth Collection. The seafront shows have far less on the ground, so the flying is the main event there.

For the routine of the day, when to walk the static and when to save your pitch, take a look at my On the day guide.

Ear defenders. Your child’s ears are delicate, so try to protect them as much as possible. The fast jets like the Typhoon are the loudest things on this list, and a Chinook passing low is not far behind. I would recommend proper ear defenders that fit snugly, put on before the display starts rather than when the jet arrives.

Hangar days and static parks are the best chance to see a cockpit up close.

Flying aircraft often park up where you can see them before the display.

Every line up moves about. Aircraft go unserviceable, crews get called away and display permissions arrive late. In 2026 the Red Arrows missed several UK shows while they were touring North America, the Flying Bulls pulled out of Cosford, and RIAT did not go ahead at all, which is why its entries here come from 2025.

So use this page to learn what you are looking at, and use the show pages to see who is booked. When I last checked, most shows were still adding aircraft through the spring and early summer, so I would advise taking a look again the week before you go.

Unfortunately the weather on the day will be what it will be, so plan accordingly. If it’s fresh, dress in layers to keep warm. Low cloud is what usually trims a programme, as the fast jets and the teams need a certain cloud base to fly their full routine.

Our View

If you only learn one sound, make it the Merlin. Once you can pick out a Spitfire or a Hurricane by ear before you see it, every show feels different. The jets are thrilling, but it is that growl coming round the back of the crowd that gets me every time, and I have been going since 2006.
