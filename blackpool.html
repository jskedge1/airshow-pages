Blackpool Air Show

Free on the seafront. Two days every August

Blackpool has been putting on flying displays since 1909, and today it’s one of the biggest free shows in the country, with hundreds of thousands of people along the promenade. The flying happens out over the Irish Sea between North Pier and Central Pier, with the Tower right behind the crowd, and you don’t need a ticket to watch. You get the RAF Typhoon, the Battle of Britain Memorial Flight, warbirds, wingwalkers and aerobatics, plus an Air Show Village on the Tower Festival Headland and the whole of Blackpool on your doorstep.

The show at a glance

Blackpool is a free, two day seaside show over the Irish Sea. There are no gates and no tickets, you just turn up and find a spot on the promenade. More than 350,000 people were expected over the 2026 weekend, so the prom fills up early.

The display is flown out over the sea in front of the Tower, roughly between North Pier and Central Pier, and the promenade is closed to traffic for the day. In 2026 the flying ran from about 14:00 to 17:40 on both days, with the RAF Typhoon, the Battle of Britain Memorial Flight, the MiG-17 on its first UK display, US warbirds, the Aerosuperbatics Wingwalkers, Team Raven and plenty of aerobatics. The Red Arrows are usually the headline act, but they missed 2026 because of their North America tour.

Before the flying there’s the Air Show Village on the Tower Festival Headland, with stalls, food, armed forces stands and things for children to do, and around it you have the whole resort: the Tower, the piers, the beach and more chip shops than you can count. That makes it a good choice for a mixed group, as not everyone has to love aircraft.

The main things to plan for are getting there early enough to claim a spot near the centre, the road closures on the promenade, and the split tram service on show days. I would advise coming by train if you can, or parking in one of the big town centre car parks and walking down to the sea.

The Air Show Village

The Air Show Village sits on the Tower Festival Headland, between Central Pier and North Pier, and in 2026 it was open from 11:00 to 18:00 on both days. You’ll find stalls, food, armed forces stands with their ground crews and activities for children, and it’s right behind the best viewing on the promenade.

It’s a great way for your kids to learn about and explore the different roles in the armed forces, and if anyone in your group would like to join up, it’s the place to ask any questions they may have. I would suggest walking round it before the flying starts at about 14:00, as it gets very busy once the crowds arrive.

    Our view

    If you want a big, free day out with the family, Blackpool is hard to beat. The flying is in front of the Tower, the beach and the Air Show Village keep everyone busy, and nobody has to buy a ticket. Come by train if you can, claim your spot by late morning, and stay for the last display while the roads clear.

    Blackpool Air ShowParking & arrival guide

    Blackpool is built for big crowds. The motorway runs almost to the beach, there are thousands of spaces in the town centre car parks, and Blackpool North station is about 15 minutes’ walk from the Tower. The catch is that the promenade closes to traffic on show days and the trams run in two sections, so plan your last few hundred metres on foot. I would advise the train if you can, or arriving by car before 11:00.

    Do I need a ticket?
    No. Blackpool Air Show is free and there are no tickets or gates. You just find a spot on the promenade. The only things you might pay for are parking, the trams and anything you buy in the Air Show Village.
    Which roads close?
    In 2026 the promenade was closed to all traffic from 10:30 to 19:00 on both days, between Chapel Street and Springfield Road, with signed diversions. That covers the main viewing stretch around Central Pier, the Tower and North Pier, so it is pedestrian only for the day.
    Do the trams run?
    Yes, but not past the display. In 2026 the trams ran in two sections, Starr Gate to Manchester Square every 12 minutes and North Pier to Fleetwood, with extra trams every 7 minutes between Little Bispham and North Station. No trams stopped at the Tower or Central Pier.
    Where can I park?
    In the council car parks. Central car park on Seasiders Way has over 1,300 spaces and is a short walk from Central Pier. South car park and Bloomfield Road are cheaper and a little further out, and Talbot Road multi storey is handy for North Pier. Prices were checked in October 2026 and may change for event days.
    Is there a park and ride?
    I couldn’t find one for the 2026 show. Blackpool Airport ran a summer park and ride in 2021, but it hasn’t been confirmed since, so check Visit Blackpool closer to the show.
    Can I fly a drone?
    No. A temporary restricted area covers the display, and police seize drones flown inside it. Leave the drone at home.

    Where to stay

    Blackpool has more hotel beds than almost anywhere in the UK, from big chain hotels by the piers to hundreds of guest houses a few streets back from the sea. Air show weekend is in the middle of the summer holidays, so the sea front fills early. I would advise booking as soon as the dates are confirmed.

    Shooting at Blackpool

    The crowd faces west over the Irish Sea. The sun is behind you in the morning, beside you on your left in the early afternoon, and in front of you from about mid afternoon, which is when much of the flying happens. Displays are flown further out than at an airfield, so 400 to 600mm helps.

    Get creative on the sea front

    Blackpool gives you something an airfield can’t: the Tower, the piers and the sea. The flip side is the light, as the sun moves in front of you through the afternoon. I would suggest using the sun guide to plan where you stand, and treating the later displays as a chance for silhouettes, smoke and sparkle on the water.

    Ideas to try

    • Walk out on North Pier or Central Pier and look back along the display line, so you can get aircraft with the Tower or the other pier in the frame.
    • Later in the afternoon, turn the backlight into a feature. Expose for the sky and go for silhouettes, smoke trails and sun on the water.
    • Stand south of the Tower, towards Central Pier, so more of the display is to your right and away from the sun.
    • Leave room in the frame for smoke, so it leads the eye to the aircraft.
    • Zoom out now and then to include the crowd, the beach or the Tower, so the photo says Blackpool.

    Food, toilets & access

    This is a seaside resort, so food, drink and toilets are never far away. The Air Show Village has its own food stalls, and there are cafes, chip shops and pubs all along the front and in the streets behind it. The queues peak just before the flying starts.

    Food & drink

    Food concessions in the Air Show Village, plus hundreds of cafes, chippies and restaurants in the town. Picnics are fine, but the promenade has a ban on drinking alcohol in public, and glass isn’t allowed on the beach or sea wall.

    Kids’ activities

    Activities in the Air Show Village, the beach in front of the display, and the Tower, the piers and the arcades nearby if they need a break from the flying.

    Toilets

    Promenade toilets, including the Undercroft at North Pier, open 08:00 to 20:00 in summer, with accessible toilets on a RADAR key. Go before the flying starts, and if you aren’t waiting for a particular display, use that slot as your break.

    Shade and seating

    There is almost no shade on the prom, so bring hats, sun cream and your own chairs. The sea wall steps make a handy seat if you get there early.

    Accessibility
    The promenade is flat and paved, and the trams are wheelchair accessible. Blue Badge holders get 3 hours free with a clock in most council car parks, and Central car park has 42 Blue Badge spaces. Accessible toilets along the prom use a RADAR key, and there are Changing Places toilets at Blackpool Tower and the Holiday Inn on Talbot Road. I couldn’t find a dedicated accessible viewing area, so I would advise arriving early for a clear spot by the sea wall.
    What can I bring?
    Folding chairs, blankets, food and soft drinks. No drinking alcohol in public on the promenade, as police and council staff can confiscate it, no glass on the beach or sea wall, no camping or tents overnight, and no drones in the restricted area over the display.
    Display times and commentary
    Visit Blackpool publishes the display times for each day on its website before the show, and a show radio station on DAB carries the commentary and traffic updates.
    Medical help
    Ask any steward or police officer and they will point you to the nearest first aid point.
    The tide
    If you sit on the sand, keep an eye on the tide. On a big high tide the sea comes up to the sea wall, so be ready to move up to the promenade. The weather box on the Plan your day tab shows the approximate tide times.
    If the weather turns
    Low cloud, sea mist or strong wind can change the programme or stop some displays. It is a two day show, so if Saturday looks poor, Sunday may be better. Pack a waterproof either way.
