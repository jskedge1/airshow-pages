Shuttleworth Air Shows

Seven shows at Old Warden in 2027

Vintage aircraft flying from a grass airfield in the Bedfordshire countryside, from Edwardian machines to Second World War fighters. The evening displays, with the sun behind the crowd, are what make Shuttleworth special. Here’s how to plan a day at any of the seven 2027 shows.

The shows at a glance

Shuttleworth is a living museum of flight on its own grass aerodrome at Old Warden, near Biggleswade. Most of the aircraft in the hangars still fly, and on show days they take to the air alongside visiting warbirds and display teams.

Shows here feel very different from a big military air show. The airfield is small, the crowd line curves round the grass and you're close to the aircraft, both in the air and in the hangars. Most shows run from 09:00, with the flying starting at about 14:00, and several carry on into the evening with a second display as the light softens.

Your ticket also covers the Collection's hangars, the Swiss Garden and ground attractions such as the fun fair, Discovery Zones and vintage bus rides. Children go free at most shows, and parking is free, so it's one of the better value days out in the calendar. I would suggest picking your show by theme: the Military Air Show and Summer Air Show suit warbird fans, while Flying Proms is a special evening out with a live orchestra.

The Collection

Your ticket includes the Shuttleworth Collection itself, with hangars full of aircraft that still fly, from Edwardian pioneers to Second World War fighters. Many of them take part in the display later in the day, so walking the hangars in the morning is a great way to see them up close first.

The crews are often working on the aircraft between flights, and the relaxed atmosphere means they’re usually happy to chat, so it’s a great place for children to ask questions and for enthusiasts to learn the history behind each aircraft.

    Is Shuttleworth right for you?

    An honest look at who gets the most from a Shuttleworth show. Think about how into aviation you are, and what the rest of your group will enjoy.

    Great fit

    Families

    Children go free at most shows, there's a free fun fair and plenty of grass to spread out on. The site is compact, so you're never far from the car, food or toilets.

    Great fit

    Children

    Vintage bus rides, Discovery Zones and aircraft close enough to see the pilots wave. The flying starts at about 14:00, so the morning is for exploring.

    Good fit

    Teenagers / non-aviation fans

    There's no fast jet noise or big military presence, which some teenagers miss. The evening shows, with music, fireworks at the Season Finale and the bus bar, have a festival feel.

    Great fit

    Aviation enthusiasts

    Aircraft you won't see flying anywhere else, evening light from behind the crowd and hangars full of history. For many enthusiasts this is the favourite show of the year.

    Great fit

    First-time visitors

    Smaller crowds, free parking and a relaxed pace make it an easy first air show. If you enjoy it, the big military shows are a natural next step.

    Our view

    If you love historic aircraft, Shuttleworth is a must. For families it's a gentle, good value day out, and first-timers will find it far less daunting than a big show. If what excites you is fast jets and military noise, I would suggest pairing it with RIAT or the RAF Air Show at Cosford.

    How arrival works

    On show days the car parks don't open the moment you reach the gate. Here's what to expect, so a short wait in the queue doesn't catch you out.

    1

    Ticket check

    Arrive around 08:00, an hour before the gates open (09:00 for the Flying Proms), and follow the yellow Shuttleworth signs to the Alder Drive entrance off the B658. Have your e-tickets ready on your phone, or a screenshot in case the signal drops, and stewards check them at your car window.

    3

    Directed to the car parks

    When the airfield opens, stewards wave each line of cars through to the car parks. Follow their directions rather than your sat nav, and note where you've parked before you walk to the showground.

    Shuttleworth doesn't publish a separate car park opening time, only that the gates open at 09:00 (10:00 at the Flying Proms). If you're driving, I would advise arriving about an hour before, around 08:00 (09:00 for the Flying Proms). You'll be near the front of the holding queue when the car parks open, with time to claim a spot on the crowd line before exploring.

    Ways to arrive

    Driving, the train and Shuttleworth's shuttle bus, a weekday and Saturday bus, and local taxis. Times and fares change, so check them before you travel.

    Do I need to book parking?
    No. Parking is free at every 2027 show and you don't need to book it. Car clubs can arrange display parking through Shuttleworth's groups team (groups@shuttleworth.org).
    Is there Blue Badge parking?
    Yes. Shuttleworth has dedicated Blue Badge parking in a space central to the air show event area, entered through Alder Drive like everyone else. Its website doesn't mention booking it in advance or sending proof beforehand, so in practice it works as turn up on the day. Have your Blue Badge on display and tell the stewards at the ticket check that you need the Blue Badge area. Spaces aren't unlimited, so I would advise arriving early and calling 01767 627927 before the show if you'd like to check.
    Can I use the Hill Lane entrance?
    Not on show days. Hill Lane (SG18 9EP) is for daily admission only, and the lane is closed while the flying displays are on. Use Alder Drive (SG18 9DT) for every air show.
    Will there be traffic?
    The lanes round Old Warden are narrow, so queues build before the gates open and again after the last display. Check the A1 before you set off, particularly around the Black Cat roundabout north of the site, where major road works were under way in 2026.
    How do I get home easily?
    Everyone leaves at once after the finale. Waiting 30 to 45 minutes with a flask and the last of the picnic usually gets you home just as quickly and with far less stress. If you're on the shuttle bus, book a later return time if you want to see everything.

    Where to stay

    Hotels, campsites and B&Bs within about 25 miles of Old Warden. Rooms near the A1 go quickly on show weekends, especially for the Festival of Flight, so I would advise booking early.

      Get creative on the airfield

      Old Warden is a small, curved grass airfield, so the aircraft are close and you see a lot of topsides. The morning is into the sun, so I would suggest spending it in the hangars and on the aircraft parked on the grass, then saving your best cards for the afternoon and the evening display.

      Ideas to try

      • Slow the shutter for the old propellers. 1/125 or slower gives a full disc on the Edwardian and Golden Age aircraft.
      • Shoot the take offs and landings on the grass. The aircraft taxi close to the crowd, so a shorter lens works well here.
      • Wait for the banking passes as aircraft follow the curve of the crowd line. A topside view shows far more of the aircraft.
      • Stay for the evening display. With the sun low behind you, the light is warm and the wind usually drops for the oldest aircraft.
      • Walk the hangars and the aircraft on the grass before 12:00 for clean shots without the crowds.

      Food, facilities & what to expect

      Everything is a short walk from the crowd line. You're welcome to bring a picnic, and there's a good choice of food and drink on site if you'd rather not.

      Food & drink

      A line of hand-picked caterers sets up near the crowd line, alongside Shuttleworth's own Runway Café for a sit-down meal. Queues are longest just before the flying starts at about 14:00, so I would suggest eating early or bringing a picnic.

      • Picnics welcome. Bring your own food and drink, but leave sharp knives and glass bottles at home.
      • No barbecues. BBQs and open fires aren't allowed anywhere on site.
      • Alcohol. Shuttleworth reserves the right to confiscate alcohol brought in, so check before you pack any.
      • Paying. The site prefers card or phone payments. Cash is only taken when there's no alternative, and change isn't guaranteed.
      Seen in 2026

      The Bus Bar

      One of the highlights of the food area in 2026 was the Route 47 Events bar, served from the side of a converted green and cream double decker bus. It's a great spot to meet friends between displays or toast the evening flying. Caterers can change from show to show, so check what's on the day.

      What to expect on the day

      Toilets

      Accessible toilets at the Visitor Centre, the Runway Café, between Hangars 4 and 5 and in the Swiss Garden, plus extra show day units. Baby changing in the Visitor Centre and hangar toilets.

      Shade & weather

      There's no shade in the car parks and very little on the crowd line. Small beach tents up to 1.2m high are allowed if well tethered; gazebos and big umbrellas aren't.

      Ground attractions

      Included with your ticket at most shows: the fun fair, Discovery Zones, vintage bus rides, the Collection's hangars and the Swiss Garden.

      Banned items

      Drones, BBQs, fireworks and flares, Chinese lanterns, laser pens, ladders, powered segways, scooters and skateboards, glass bottles and weapons.

      Accessibility

      Blue Badge parking. There's dedicated Blue Badge parking central to the air show event area, entered through Alder Drive. Shuttleworth's website doesn't say you need to book it or send proof beforehand, so it's turn up on the day. Display your Blue Badge (the national disabled parking permit) and tell the stewards at the ticket check. Spaces are limited, so arrive early. If you want certainty, call 01767 627927 before the show.

      Carers go free. Carers of disabled visitors are entitled to a free ticket. Book it by emailing enquiries@shuttleworth.org. Random spot checks of carer documentation are carried out on the day, so bring proof with you, such as a benefit award letter or an Access Card.

      Getting around. The Visitor Centre has step-free access with a ramp, and the hangars have ramps throughout. The paddocks and car parks are grass, which can be uneven or soft after rain, and some parking near the hangars is on gravel.

      Mobility scooters. Shuttleworth's banned list includes powered segways, scooters and skateboards. This is aimed at leisure scooters, but if you use a mobility scooter I would advise contacting Shuttleworth before your visit to confirm.

      Assistance dogs are welcome across the whole site, including areas where pet dogs aren't allowed. The on-site Garden Suite and the Flat aren't wheelchair accessible.

      Dogs
      Well-behaved dogs on leads are welcome on the airfield paddocks, in the Runway Café Sunroom and in the Visitor Centre. They're not allowed in the hangars, the main café, the Swiss Garden, Shuttleworth House and its parkland, or at the Flying Proms. Vintage engines are loud, so think about whether your dog will enjoy it.
      Lost children
      Agree a meeting point when you arrive, such as a hangar number, and write your phone number on younger children's wrists.
      If it rains
      Vintage aircraft are more weather sensitive than modern ones, so some displays may be shortened or cancelled in strong wind or low cloud. The hangars give shelter. Check Shuttleworth's social media on the morning, and pack a waterproof whatever the forecast says.
