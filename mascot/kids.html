Pip for kids

See Pip? It must be airshow season.

Ideas that help families plan an airshow day with children, with Pip as the guide. Each one turns advice that's already on your site into something a child can do for themselves: a list to tick, a card to carry, a game for the quiet bits.

First version, November 2026

1The thinking

Parents plan, children wait. That's the usual pattern, and it's why the day can feel long when you're small. Pip gives children their own jobs before the day, so they arrive excited, know the plan, and already have their ear defenders on when the first jet comes.

Your advice stays the advice. Nothing new is invented. Every idea below is built from a page you've already written, and the page is named under each one.

Mostly print and social. Pip lives on social media more than the website, so most of these are printables to download and posts to share. Nothing goes on a live page without your yes.

Plan

Which show is right for little ones?

A "Plan it with Pip" post series, and later perhaps a family filter on the calendar, that points parents to gentler shows for a first visit. It uses your own groupings: vintage and grass airfield shows growl rather than roar, small country shows have room to sit back, seafront shows let you walk away from the busy middle.

Built from: "Choosing a quieter show or day" in the calm airshow visit guide, and Pick your show on each show page.

First airshow with little ones?

  • Vintage and grass airfield shows. Older aircraft growl rather than roar
  • Small country shows. Room to sit back from the fence
  • Seafront shows. No gates, so you can walk away from the busy middle
  • Big military shows. The loudest. Ear defenders a must

Pip's airshow words

A one page picture glossary, so children understand what the commentator is talking about. It also makes a good carousel post: one word per slide, with Pip acting it out.

Built from: words already used across your guides. Definitions in plain words for a seven year old.

Pip's airshow words

Crowd lineThe fence you watch from. Everyone stays behind it.
DisplayWhen an aircraft flies its show in front of you.
FlypastAn aircraft flying past the crowd.
FormationAircraft flying close together in a shape.
StaticAircraft parked on the ground to look at up close.
FlightlineThe row of aircraft parked, ready to fly.
MarshalThe person who guides aircraft with signals.
AfterburnerThe loudest bit of a fast jet. Ear defenders on!

6On social media

Pip's main home. Four series that families can join in with, without ever sharing a photo of their child.

Spot Pip

Pip is hidden in one of your airshow photos or graphics. Kids find Pip and parents comment where. Simple, repeatable, and it trains children to look for Pip.

Pip's countdown

A post a few days before each big show, with Pip packed and ready. It's the "see Pip, it must be airshow season" moment, every time.

Pip's word of the week

One of Pip's airshow words, with Pip acting it out. Short, easy to make, and something children can learn.

Pack it, Pip! challenge

Families share a photo of the backpack their child packed. The bag, not the child.

8Keeping it right for children

  • Printables need no email address, and nothing a child fills in is ever sent to you.
  • The phone number goes on the pocket card at home, on paper only.
  • Social series never ask for photos of children.
  • Pip never asks children to message, sign up or buy.
  • Every printable works in black and white on a home printer.
  • Safety wording stays exactly as on your pages: stand still, find a steward.
  • Nothing goes on a live page, including Kids' Corner, without a preview and your yes.
