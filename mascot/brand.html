The mascot brand book

Meet Pip

Plan.Inform.Prepare.

See Pip? It must be airshow season.

Pip is the little propeller plane who helps families and first timers get the most out of a UK airshow. This book sets out who Pip is, what PIP stands for, and how to use Pip so people come to know and love the character.

First version, November 2026

Three words that say what the whole site does. Each one has its own colour, so it can label a type of box, a video series or a social post.

2Who Pip is

Pip is a small, round propeller plane in the site's own blue, teal and red. Pip isn't any real aircraft type and never flies in the display. Pip watches from the crowd line, just like you.

Pip's personality, in four words: curious, helpful, calm, cheerful.

  • Curious. Pip loves finding things out and wants to know what every aircraft is.
  • Helpful. Pip points the way, holds the map and waves the marshal's paddles.
  • Calm. Queues, rain and crowds don't bother Pip, so they needn't bother you.
  • Cheerful. Pip is pleased to see you and happy when your day goes well.

The big idea: when your child spots Pip, they know it must be airshow season. Pip is the sign that the fun is about to start.

For children, Pip is a friend who makes a big, loud day feel manageable. Pip wears ear defenders when it gets noisy, which is exactly the habit we want kids to copy.

3Pip and Jack

You are the voice of the site. Pip is the sidekick. Keeping the two jobs apart is what stops Pip feeling like a gimmick, and keeps your tips trusted.

Jack does

  • Every tip, opinion and recommendation ("I would…")
  • Every fact, date, time and price
  • Reviews, scores and comparisons
  • The Jack's tip boxes, which stay yours

Pip does

  • Points the way and labels the type of box: Plan, Inform or Prepare
  • Stars in the cartoons, trailers and social graphics
  • Runs the kids' missions and spotter cards
  • Cheers, waves and keeps people company while a page loads

The golden rule: Pip never says a fact or an opinion that you haven't written first.

4How Pip talks

Pip speaks only in cartoons, kids' activities and social posts. Lines are short enough for a seven year old to read on their own.

  • Short: one idea, about twelve words at most.
  • Warm and encouraging, never scary or bossy.
  • Sentence case, no shouting in capitals, no dashes.
  • Explains any airshow word the first time it's used.
  • Never sells. Pip doesn't say "buy" or "book now".
Got your ear defenders? Little ears need looking after.
Let's plan your day. Which show are we going to?
You spotted them all. That's a proper spotter's eye!
A bit of rain? Pack a coat and we're still on.

7Where Pip goes, and where Pip doesn't

Pip is your mascot on social media more than on the website. On the site Pip appears in just a few spots, so Pip stays special and people notice when Pip turns up.

Yes, Pip goes here

  • Social media, most of all. Instagram, Facebook and YouTube are Pip's home
  • Videos, cartoons, trailers and video end cards
  • Posters, printables and checklists
  • A few spots on the website, including the calm airshow visit guide, where Pip keeps you company
  • Kids' Corner, spotter cards, colouring sheets and missions
  • Waiting and empty moments: loading, "nothing found", a missing page

No, Pip stays away from here

  • Most of the website. Pip is a guest on the site, not on every page
  • More than one Pip on a screen
  • Inside every box on a page. Pip labels the occasional box, not all of them
  • Serious news: accidents, cancellations, memorials and remembrance
  • Security rules and medical information, where plain words matter most
  • Next to a show's or sponsor's logo, so it never looks like Pip endorses them
  • On a live show page, until you've seen a preview and said yes

9Looking after Pip

A mascot gets recognised by looking the same every time. These keep Pip looking like Pip. One house rule: no curved edges on anything around Pip.

✓

Do use the drawings just as they are, on white, cream or navy.

✓

Do keep every box, label and frame square or slanted. Pip is the only round thing.

✕

Don't stretch or squash Pip.

✕

Don't change Pip's colours.

✕

Don't crowd a screen with lots of Pips.

✕
BOOK NOW!!! LAST CHANCE!!!

Don't make Pip shout or sell.

✕

Don't add national flags, military markings or weapons. Pip belongs to everyone.

✕

Don't redraw Pip to look like a real aircraft, or like any other cartoon plane.

11Before Pip goes big

  • Check the name. Before anything is printed or sold with Pip on it, search the UK trademark register (on GOV.UK) for "Pip" in toys, clothing and printed matter. If it's busy, "Pip the Plane" or "Pip from the UK Airshow Guide" can be used as the full name.
  • Keep the master files. Every pose is in the mascot folder as SVG (any size, for print) and PNG (for Wix and social). All the drawings come from one file, so Pip always matches.
  • New poses are quick. Ask for Pip in a new outfit or doing a new job and it can be added in minutes, in the same style.

The one line to remember

Pip is the friendly face, Jack is the trusted voice. Pip appears where a smile helps, and steps aside where plain facts matter.
