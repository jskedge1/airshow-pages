Midlands Air Festival

Over 100 hot air balloons, an afternoon air display and the Friday Nightfire

Midlands Air Festival is one of the most unusual days out in the air show calendar. Balloons fill the sky at dawn and dusk, aircraft fly in the afternoon, and on the Friday night the balloons glow to music before the fireworks. Here’s how I would plan your visit to Ragley.

The Festival At A Glance

Midlands Air Festival is something a bit different. It mixes a proper air display with over a hundred hot air balloons, all in the grounds of Ragley Hall, a grand country house near Alcester in Warwickshire. You get warbirds, jets and aerobatics in the afternoon, and balloons filling the sky in the early morning and evening.

The festival runs over three days, from Friday 4 to Sunday 6 June 2027. Friday is the big evening, called Nightfire, with balloons glowing to music, aircraft carrying fireworks on their wings and a firework finale. Saturday and Sunday have afternoon air displays, with balloon mass ascents at the start and end of the day, weather permitting.

I would suggest thinking about what you most want to see before you book. If it’s the balloons, an early start or a Friday evening is a must. If it’s the aircraft, the Saturday and Sunday afternoons are where the main flying is.

Jack’s tipTickets are advance only, with none sold on the gate. Book before you travel, and don’t turn up without one.

My View

If you’ve never seen a mass balloon ascent, this is the place to do it. Pair it with an afternoon of flying and the Friday Nightfire, and it’s a weekend unlike any other air show in the calendar. Just remember the balloons depend on the weather, so I would advise planning more than one chance to see them.

The Balloons

The balloons are what make this festival so special. Over a hundred of them come to Ragley, with up to a hundred taking off together in a mass ascent, plus special shapes you won’t believe can fly. Here’s when to see them, what happens if the weather says no, and where I would stand.

Why So Early And So Late?

Hot air balloons only fly in the early morning and in the evening. That’s when there’s no thermal activity, the rising pockets of warm air that build up on a sunny day and make a balloon hard to control. So the balloons wrap around the air display, with the flying in the afternoon and the balloons at either end of the day.

The festival plans four mass ascents, from the Friday evening to the Sunday morning, all weather permitting.

What If It’s Windy?

What’s the weather doing?

A Mass Ascent

The balloons inflate on the field, the burners roar, and up to a hundred of them lift off and drift away with the breeze. They can be in the air for a long time, so keep looking up as they float over the countryside.

A Tethered Or Burner Display

The pilots decide on the day whether it’s safe. If the balloons can’t fly freely, the festival team will try to put on a tethered display, with the balloons held to the ground, or a burner display instead. So it’s still worth heading over.

The Fireglow

Part of Nightfire. Up to 15 balloons fire their burners in time to music, lighting up the dark like giant lanterns, while other burners send flames around 20 metres into the sky. It finishes with a giant firework display.

Best Spots

Get there for the inflation

The best bit is watching the balloons come to life on the ground. I would advise being near the launch field before the ascent time, not just looking up once they’ve gone.

Special shapes up close

In 2025 the Cameron Arena gave you a close look at the special shape balloons, with commentary. Look for it on the 2027 site map.

Listen to the commentary

A commentary team talks you through the balloons and pilots, which makes it far easier to know what you’re looking at.

Don’t forget the models

There are model hot air balloons too, small but beautifully made, and great for younger children.

Jack’s tipBalloons depend completely on the weather, so I would suggest giving yourself more than one chance. If you’re staying over, a Saturday dawn and a Sunday dawn gives you two goes. Want to fly in one yourself? Balloon flights can be booked through the festival.

Getting There And Parking

Ragley Hall sits just south west of Alcester, in the Warwickshire countryside. Most people drive, and standard parking is free with your ticket. Everyone, whether by car, bus, bike or on foot, comes in through the Ragley Hall main entrance.

By Car

  • The roadsThe A435 runs past Alcester and crosses the M42 at junction 3. At Alcester it meets the A46. Head for Alcester, then follow the yellow event signs.
  • Follow the signs, not the sat navThe festival asks you to follow the yellow event signs once you see them, rather than your sat nav.
  • Where you parkThe main car park is on grass, between about 250 metres and a kilometre from the event field. Follow the stewards when you arrive.
  • Premium parkingBought with your ticket. It has its own entry gate, its own ticket lane and parking in the event field.
  • Coaches and motorhomesBoth are welcome for the day, but they can’t stay overnight in the car park.

Without A Car

  • By trainRedditch is the most suitable station for the festival.
  • By busDiamond Bus route 247, between Redditch and Evesham, stops right by the Ragley Hall main entrance. When I last checked, I couldn’t confirm a Sunday service, so please check Diamond Bus before you rely on it.
  • By bikeCome in through the main entrance and head for the disabled parking area, where bikes can be locked to the fence line.
  • On footIt’s about 1.5 miles from the main entrance to the house, so comfortable shoes, please.

Accessible Arrival

There’s a signed disabled car park directly opposite the main event field, with accessible toilets. You need a disabled parking pass, chosen when you buy your ticket, and you display your Blue Badge on the day. The stewards will help you when you arrive. The ground is a mix of tarmac roads and grass.

Plan Your Day

The 2027 timings haven’t been published yet, so here’s how I would plan a Saturday or Sunday, based on how the festival has run before. Balloons only fly in calm weather, so treat every balloon time as weather permitting.

A Saturday Or Sunday

  1. Early, if you want the dawn balloonsIn 2025 the gates opened at 05:30, with the dawn ascent at around 06:00. I would advise arriving about an hour before the gates open, and wrap up warm, it’s chilly at that time.
  2. Save your pitchIf you want a front row spot for the air display, head there first and put your chairs or blanket down.
  3. ExploreLook round the trade stalls, the food village and the families zone, and see the special shape balloons up close.
  4. Toilet before the flyingGo before the air display starts, the queues build quickly after that.
  5. The air displayThe main flying is in the afternoon. If there’s a display you’re less keen on, use it as your toilet or food break.
  6. Evening balloonsStay for the evening mass ascent if the weather allows. It’s a magical way to end the day.

Friday Nightfire

Friday runs to a different rhythm. In 2025 the gates opened at 11:30, with the balloons tethered on the ground in the day and the mass ascent in the early evening.

Photography At Ragley

Balloons and aircraft need very different approaches, and that’s what makes this festival such fun with a camera. Here’s where I would start. They’re starting points, not fixed settings, so adjust for the light on the day.

Dawn balloons

Soft, low light and plenty of colour. A wide lens shows the scale of a mass ascent. Keep the ISO at 1200 or below, and steady yourself or use a tripod as the light is low.

Propeller aircraft

Around 1/160 to 1/320 of a second gives you a nice prop blur, and helicopters suit the same range. Pan smoothly and slow down gradually as you get more confident.

Jets

Around 1/1000 to 1/2000 of a second freezes fast jets. Single point focus, and leave a little room in the frame for cropping.

Nightfire

A tripod is your friend for the Fireglow and fireworks. The balloons barely move during the Fireglow, so you don’t need a fast shutter. Keep the ISO at 1200 or below and let the tripod do the work.

Avoid machine gunWith balloons especially, there’s no need to hold the shutter down. They move slowly, so take your time, think about your composition and wait for the right moment.

Help On Show Days

Ragley is a big estate, with early starts, long walks on grass and a Friday that finishes in the dark. Here’s what to do if something goes wrong, plus quick answers to the questions people ask most.

In an emergency, call 999. For anything else, the nearest steward is the best person to ask.

If Something Goes Wrong

Lost Each Other

I would advise agreeing a meeting point as soon as you arrive, somewhere easy to find. If someone goes missing, tell a steward straight away.

Feeling Unwell

Find a steward and they’ll get help to you. On a sunny June day, drink plenty and wear sun cream.

The Weather Turns

The balloons are always weather permitting, so plans can change at short notice. Keep an eye on the festival’s social media for the latest.

Cold At Dawn

A 05:30 start in June can still be chilly, even if the afternoon gets hot. Layers are the answer.

Leaving In The Dark

After Nightfire it’s a walk on grass in the dark. The organisers recommend sturdy shoes and a torch.

Live Stream Scams

There’s no official live stream. The festival warns about scam links on social media claiming to offer one, so don’t pay for or click on them.

Quick Answers

When is the 2027 festival?

Friday 4 to Sunday 6 June 2027, at Ragley Hall near Alcester. In 2026 it ran from 29 to 31 May.

Can I buy tickets on the gate?

No. Tickets are sold in advance only, so book before you travel.

How much were tickets?

In 2025 a one day adult ticket was from £35, teenagers from £17.50, and under 12s were free. Two days were £63 and three days £89. The 2027 prices may be different.

Can I bring my dog?

No. Dogs and other pets aren’t allowed anywhere on site, including the car parks. Guide dogs are welcome.

Can I bring a gazebo?

No, gazebos aren’t allowed. Small pop up tents are fine.

Is there any seating?

No, so bring your own chairs or a blanket.

Can I bring food and drink?

Yes, you’re welcome to bring your own. There’s a food village on site too.

Can I have a BBQ?

No. BBQs and open fires aren’t allowed.

Can I leave and come back?

Yes. Get your hand stamped on the way out.

Is parking free?

Standard parking is free and included with your ticket. Premium parking is bought when you book. Disabled parking is free, but you choose it as a ticket. More in .

Are carer tickets available?

Yes, for carers and companions over 16. If someone under 16 needs a professional carer, contact the organisers.

Is there a live stream?

No. Watch out for scam links claiming to show it.

Will the balloons definitely fly?

Not always. The balloons are weather permitting. See for what happens if it’s too windy.
