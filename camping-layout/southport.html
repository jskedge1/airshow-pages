Southport Air Show

The North West’s big seaside air show

Southport is a proper seaside show with a strong RAF presence. You get the Red Arrows, the Battle of Britain Memorial Flight and the Typhoon over a huge sandy beach, with a Military Village and rides along Marine Drive. This guide is here to help you plan and organise your weekend by the sea, and I’ve shared how I would go about it.

The show at a glance

The show runs along Southport seafront, with the crowd on Marine Drive, the seawall and the beach, looking out over the sand towards the Irish Sea. It draws tens of thousands of people across the bank holiday weekend, so it has a lovely busy feel.

Southport is a proper seaside show with a strong RAF presence. In 2026 the Red Arrows flew both days, billed as their only public display in the North West that year, alongside the Battle of Britain Memorial Flight, the RAF Tutor, a P-51 Mustang and a full line-up of civilian acts. The RAF Typhoon closed the show on the Sunday.

Car parks open at 09:00, the gates open at 10:00 and the flying runs from about midday to 17:00, which gives you the morning to save your pitch, have a look around the Military Village and find the food traders on Marine Drive. Lord Street and the town centre are a short walk behind you, so this might work well for your family if some of your group need a break from the flying.

The main things I would plan for are the bank holiday crowds, the weather coming in off the Irish Sea and the road closures along Marine Drive. I would advise coming by train if you can, and getting there early, as this has worked for me in the past.

The Military Village

I would suggest heading to the Military Village before the flying starts. In 2026 it had a replica Chinook, the RAF Typhoon Display Team stand, the Mid Lancs Military Vehicle Trust, the Garrison Artillery Volunteers and the Veterans Living History Museum, plus flight simulators nearby. Children love to learn about the different roles in the forces here, and if anyone in your group is thinking of joining up, it’s a good place to ask questions.

At many UK shows the RAF display teams spend time on the ground too, like the famous Red Arrows pilots and their ground crew, and kids can often get stickers and photos with the pilots. I would keep an eye out for them between displays.

    Help On Show Days

    Southport draws tens of thousands of people over the bank holiday weekend, with a long seafront site, a huge beach and road closures to think about. Here’s what I would do if something goes wrong, plus quick answers to the questions I see asked most before the show.

    In an emergency, call 999. If someone is in trouble on the beach or in the sea, call 999 or 112 and ask for the Coastguard. For anything else, I would find the nearest steward.

    Sea Mist And Wind

    A seafront show has its own weather. It can be bright and sunny in town while a bank of mist or low cloud sits out over the Irish Sea, right where the aircraft fly. In 2026 heavy rain cut the Red Arrows’ Sunday display short. I think it’s worth knowing what to look out for, so a grey morning doesn’t put you off.

    Why mist forms

    When warm, damp air drifts over the cooler sea, it cools down and turns into mist or fog. It often sits just off the coast while the town stays sunny.

    It can lift quickly

    A misty morning doesn’t always mean a misty afternoon. As the day warms up, the mist often thins or drifts away, so I would suggest staying put rather than giving up early.

    Lower cloud, different show

    Teams like the Red Arrows fly a full looping display when the cloud is high, and a rolling or flat display when it’s lower. You’ll still see them, just flying a different routine.

    The wind off the sea

    Strong or gusty wind can stop some displays, especially lighter historic aircraft and the wingwalkers. Southport faces straight out to the west, so a westerly wind comes right off the sea and makes the beach feel much colder. I would bring layers.

    Jack’s tipOn a misty day, I would keep an eye on the show’s Instagram for any changes, and look out to sea rather than at the sky above you. In my experience the mist is often worse over the water than over the town.

    Get creative over the beach

    Southport has its challenges for photographers. Aircraft fly further out than at an airfield, sea haze can soften detail, and from mid afternoon the sun moves round in front of you, so aircraft can look dark against a bright sky. The good news is that the sun is behind you for the first half of the flying. I would suggest using the sun guide to plan when you shoot, saving your close up work for the early displays, and adding a little exposure compensation later in the afternoon.

    Ideas to try

    • I would wait for the banking passes. A topside or underside view shows far more of the aircraft than a straight flypast.
    • Try leaving room in the frame for smoke trails, so they lead the eye to the aircraft.
    • I like to zoom out now and then to include the pier, the beach or the crowds on the seawall, so the photo says Southport.
    • Late in the day, I would use the backlight for silhouettes and glowing smoke rather than fighting it.

    Food, toilets & access

    Southport has a real advantage over airfield shows, as a whole town sits right behind the seafront. You can eat from the traders on Marine Drive, bring a picnic, or step back to Lord Street or Ocean Plaza for a sit down meal, whichever suits your family.

    Food & drink

    There are food traders and market stalls along Marine Drive, plus the cafés, pubs and restaurants in town. You can bring your own food, but BBQs, gas and open fires aren’t allowed, so please see the rules before you go.

    Kids’ activities

    Children love the bumper cars, bungee trampolines, Hook a Duck, flight simulators and the Military Village, which keep them busy between displays.

    Toilets

    Toilets are within the site, with accessible toilets at the two accessible viewing areas. I would go before the flying starts, and if you aren’t waiting for a particular display, use that slot as your break.

    The beach

    The beach is soft sand, so I have found a blanket or low chair makes a long afternoon far more comfortable. Seating on site is very limited.

    Accessibility
    There are two fenced, hard standing accessible viewing areas on the seawall, near the fish wind display and near the Pier, with accessible toilets and stewards. The promenade has ramped, step free access. There is no Changing Places toilet on site, but a pass out stamp lets you leave and come back, and the nearest are at Victoria Park, Dunes Splashworld, Southport Market, The Life Rooms and The Atkinson. I would bring ear defenders, and arriving early is quieter. If you have questions, I would email Tourism@sefton.gov.uk.
    Carers
    In 2026 carers went free with proof of Carer’s Allowance, Attendance Allowance or DLA. Without proof, the carer pays the normal price.
    Dogs
    Dogs are allowed but not recommended, because of the noise, so I would think about how your dog will cope. They must be kept on a lead, or the owner can be asked to leave. There is also a seasonal dog ban from 1 May to 30 September on the stretch of beach from the Pier south towards Pleasureland.
    Banned items
    Drones, fireworks, weapons, lasers, smoke canisters and transmitting devices aren’t allowed, and random bag searches take place, so please see the rules before you go.
    If the weather turns
    Rain, low cloud or strong wind off the Irish Sea can change the programme or stop some displays. In 2026 heavy rain cut the Red Arrows’ Sunday display short. I would check the official channels on the morning and pack a waterproof.
