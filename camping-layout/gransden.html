    The show at a glance

    Little Gransden is a working grass airfield in Cambridgeshire, home to the Global Stars aerobatic team. Once a year it opens its gates for a family day out that's part air show, part classic car show and part village fête, all raising money for children's charities.

    The show has been running since the early 1990s and has raised more than £400,000 for BBC Children in Need and other charities. It's run by volunteers, and that friendly, community feel is a big part of why people come back year after year.

    The day starts with the gates opening at 10:00, which gives you time to walk the lines of more than 300 classic, vintage and specialist cars and motorcycles before the flying starts at around 12:30. Expect vintage aircraft, warbirds and aerobatics, with live music, trade stalls and food through the day. I would suggest it as a first air show, or as a relaxed day out if your group isn't all into aviation.

    The car show

    Little Gransden is as much a car show as an air show. More than 300 classic, vintage and specialist cars and motorcycles line the airfield, and the owners are usually happy to chat about them, so it’s a great day out if not everyone in your group is into aircraft.

    Do I need to book parking?
    The official information says parking is available on site and clearly signposted from the road, and it doesn't mention booking or a separate charge. Check the official booking page when 2027 tickets go on sale, in case anything changes.
    Is there parking for Blue Badge holders?
    The official information says the event is accessible for most mobility needs, with open grass areas and toilets on site, but it doesn't say whether proof such as a Blue Badge is needed, or whether you should book ahead. At a recent show, accessible bays were signed beside the entry gate, which looks like first come first served, but I would advise contacting the organisers through the official contact page before you go, so you know what to expect when you arrive.
    Should I follow my sat nav?
    Only until you're close. Use SG19 3BP to reach the area, then follow the show signs and marshals. Some sat navs send drivers down the wrong lane on the way into airfields here, so look out for the signs.
    Will there be traffic?

    How do I get home easily?
    Everyone leaves along the same lanes, so the first half hour after the flying is the slowest. Either leave a little before the end, or stay on, look round the cars and stalls and let the rush clear. If you came by train, book your return taxi before the day.

      Get creative on the airfield

      Little Gransden is a small grass airfield, so the aircraft are close and you get wings and wheels in one day. The crowd faces south, into the sun around the middle of the day, so I would suggest the aircraft on the grass and the car lines in the morning, then the best flying shots from mid afternoon as the sun moves to your right.

      Ideas to try

      • Slow the shutter for the vintage aircraft. 1/125 to 1/250 gives a proper prop disc, so pan smoothly and take a few frames each pass.
      • Shoot the take offs and landings on the grass. Aircraft taxi close to the crowd, so a shorter lens works well.
      • Head to the bend at the east end for a different angle as aircraft run in.
      • Pair an aircraft with a classic car in the same frame for a real Wings and Wheels shot.
      • Walk the car lines and the aircraft on the grass before 12:00, while the light is soft and the crowds are small.

      Help On Show Days

      Little Gransden is a friendly, compact show run by volunteers, so help is never far away. Even so, it’s worth knowing what to do if something goes wrong, especially on a hot day on open grass. Here’s my advice, plus quick answers to the questions I see asked most.

      In an emergency, call 999. For anything else, ask the nearest steward or marshal.

      If Something Goes Wrong

      Lost Each Other

      Agree a meeting point as soon as you arrive, such as a landmark by the car show, and write your phone number on younger children’s wrists. Tell a steward straight away if someone goes missing.

      Feeling Unwell

      The airfield is open grass with very little shade. Drink plenty, wear a hat and sun cream, and ask a steward if you need help.

      Loud Aircraft

      The organisers recommend ear defenders for younger children during the displays. The Typhoon is the loudest of the day.

      Leaving And Coming Back

      Re-entry is allowed, but keep your wristband on, as you’ll need it to get back in.

      Bad Weather

      Displays may change with the weather, and the grass can get soft after rain. Pack a waterproof whatever the forecast says.

      Bringing Your Dog

      Dogs are welcome on a lead. Bring water for them, and think about a quiet spot if your dog is nervous of loud engines.

      Paying For Things

      The official information doesn’t say whether the stalls take cards. I would suggest bringing a little cash for stalls and the charity collections, as well as a card.
