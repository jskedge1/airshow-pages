    The show at a glance

    Little Gransden is a working grass airfield in Cambridgeshire, home to the Global Stars aerobatic team. Once a year it opens up for a family day out that mixes flying, classic cars and a real village fête feel, and it all raises money for children's charities.

    The show has been running since the early 1990s and has raised more than £400,000 for BBC Children in Need and other charities. It's run by volunteers, and I think that friendly, community feel is a big part of why people come back year after year.

    The gates open at 10:00, which gives you time to walk the lines of more than 300 classic, vintage and specialist cars and motorcycles before the flying starts at around 12:30. You can expect vintage aircraft, warbirds and aerobatics, with live music, trade stalls and food through the day. I would suggest it as a first air show, or as a relaxed day out if your group isn't all into aviation.

    The car show

    Little Gransden is as much a car show as an air show. More than 300 classic, vintage and specialist cars and motorcycles line the airfield, and the owners are usually happy to chat about them. This might work well for your family if not everyone in your group is into aircraft.

    The gates open at 10:00 and the flying starts at about 12:30, so I would suggest saving your pitch on the crowd line first, then walking the car lines, the trade stalls and the aircraft parked on the grass before the displays begin. This has worked for me in the past.

      If Something Goes Wrong

      Lost Each Other

      I would suggest agreeing a meeting point as soon as you arrive, such as a landmark by the car show, and writing your phone number on younger children’s wrists. I see lots of families do this. If someone goes missing, please tell a steward straight away.

      The airfield is open grass with very little shade. I'd drink plenty, wear a hat and sun cream, and ask a steward if you need help.

      Get creative on the airfield

      Little Gransden is a small grass airfield, so the aircraft are close and you get wings and wheels in one day. The crowd faces south, into the sun around the middle of the day, so I would suggest the aircraft on the grass and the car lines in the morning, then the best flying shots from mid afternoon as the sun moves to your right.

      Ideas to try

      • Slow the shutter for the vintage aircraft. I have found 1/125 to 1/250 gives a proper prop disc, so pan smoothly and take a few frames each pass.
      • Try the take offs and landings on the grass. Aircraft taxi close to the crowd, so a shorter lens works well.
      • I'd suggest you head to the bend at the east end for a different angle as aircraft run in.
      • Think about pairing an aircraft with a classic car in the same frame for a real Wings and Wheels shot.
      • It's a good idea to walk the car lines and the aircraft on the grass before 12:00, while the light is soft and the crowds are small.
