Blackpool Air Show

One of the biggest free air shows in the country

Flying out over the Irish Sea with the Tower right behind you, the whole of Blackpool on your doorstep, and no ticket needed. Here’s how I would plan a day on the prom, based on what has worked for me.

The show at a glance

Blackpool is a free, two day seaside show over the Irish Sea. There are no gates and no tickets, so you can just turn up and find a spot on the promenade. More than 350,000 people were expected over the 2026 weekend, and the prom fills up early, so I would suggest planning your arrival.

The display is flown out over the sea in front of the Tower, roughly between North Pier and Central Pier, and the promenade is closed to traffic for the day. In 2026 the flying ran from about 14:00 to 17:40 on both days, with the RAF Typhoon, the Battle of Britain Memorial Flight, the MiG-17 on its first UK display, US warbirds, the Aerosuperbatics Wingwalkers, Team Raven and plenty of aerobatics. The Red Arrows are usually the headline act, but they missed 2026 because of their North America tour.

Before the flying there’s the Air Show Village on the Tower Festival Headland, with stalls, food, armed forces stands and things for children to do, and around it you have the whole resort: the Tower, the piers, the beach and more chip shops than you can count. I find that makes it a good choice for a mixed group, as not everyone in your party has to love aircraft.

The main things I would think about are getting there early enough to claim a spot near the centre, the road closures on the promenade, and the split tram service on show days. I would advise coming by train if you can. If you drive, parking in one of the big town centre car parks and walking down to the sea has worked well for me.

The Air Show Village

The Air Show Village sits on the Tower Festival Headland, between Central Pier and North Pier, and in 2026 it was open from 11:00 to 18:00 on both days. You’ll find stalls, food, armed forces stands with their ground crews and activities for children, and it sits right behind the best viewing on the promenade.

Children love to see the armed forces stands, and it’s a great way for them to learn about the different roles. If anyone in your group is thinking about joining up, I would suggest asking the crews any questions they have while you are there. Try to walk round it before the flying starts at about 14:00, as it gets very busy once the crowds arrive. This has worked for me in the past.

    Ways to arrive

    Based on the 2026 show. Trains, trams, road closures and parking for 2027 are still to be confirmed.

    Do I need a ticket?
    No. Blackpool Air Show is free and there are no tickets or gates. You can just find a spot on the promenade. The only things you might pay for are parking, the trams and anything you buy in the Air Show Village.
    Which roads close?
    In 2026 the promenade was closed to all traffic from 10:30 to 19:00 on both days, between Chapel Street and Springfield Road, with signed diversions. That covers the main viewing stretch around Central Pier, the Tower and North Pier, so it is pedestrian only for the day.
    Do the trams run?
    Yes, but not past the display. In 2026 the trams ran in two sections, Starr Gate to Manchester Square every 12 minutes and North Pier to Fleetwood, with extra trams every 7 minutes between Little Bispham and North Station. No trams stopped at the Tower or Central Pier.
    Where can I park?
    In the council car parks. Central car park on Seasiders Way has over 1,300 spaces and is a short walk from Central Pier. South car park and Bloomfield Road are cheaper and a little further out, and Talbot Road multi storey is handy for North Pier. I checked the prices in October 2026, and they may change for event days.
    Is there a park and ride?
    I couldn’t find one for the 2026 show. Blackpool Airport ran a summer park and ride in 2021, but it hasn’t been confirmed since, so take a look at Visit Blackpool closer to the show.
    Can I fly a drone?
    No. A temporary restricted area covers the display, and police seize drones flown inside it. I would leave the drone at home.

    Where to stay

    Blackpool has more hotel beds than almost anywhere in the UK, from big chain hotels by the piers to hundreds of guest houses a few streets back from the sea. Air show weekend is in the middle of the summer holidays, so the sea front fills early. I would advise booking as soon as the dates are confirmed, as this has worked for me in the past.

    Help On Show Days

    The air show fills miles of promenade over two days, so it’s easy to lose each other in the crowd or get caught out by the weather. Here’s what I would do if something goes wrong, plus quick answers to the questions I see asked most. I hope it helps you plan.

    In an emergency, call 999. For anything else, ask the nearest steward or police officer. They can point you to first aid and help you find someone.

    If Something Goes Wrong

    Lost Each Other

    Phone signal can be poor on a packed prom, so I would advise agreeing a meeting point before you split up. The Tower is easy to find from anywhere on the front. A band or label with your number on is a good idea for younger children, and please tell a steward or police officer straight away if someone goes missing.

    Feeling Unwell

    Any steward or police officer can direct you to first aid. There’s almost no shade on the prom, so I would suggest taking breaks in the cafes behind the front on a hot afternoon.

    Getting Home

    The promenade stays closed to traffic until early evening and the trams run as a split service, so the stations get very busy straight after the flying. I would suggest staying for tea in town while the crowds clear. See .

    Loud Aircraft

    The Typhoon is very loud, and the noise bounces off the sea wall and the buildings along the front. I would advise ear defenders for babies, toddlers and anyone sensitive to noise.

    Quick Answers

    Is it really free?

    Yes. There are no tickets, so you can just turn up on the promenade.

    When are the 2027 dates?

    They haven’t been announced yet. The show is usually in August, and in 2026 it ran on Saturday 8 and Sunday 9 August.

    What time does the flying start?

    In 2026 the flying ran from about 14:00 to 17:40, with the Air Show Village open from 11:00 to 18:00. The 2027 times will come out nearer the show.

    Can I bring my dog?

    From 1 May to 30 September dogs aren’t allowed on the beach between North Pier and the Mirror Ball opposite the Solaris Centre, and they must be on a lead on the promenade between North and South Piers. Guide dogs are exempt. The crowds and the jets are loud, so I would think carefully before bringing your dog.

    Can I drink alcohol on the prom?

    No. There’s a ban on drinking in public on the promenade, and glass isn’t allowed on the beach or the sea wall.

    Can I fly my drone?

    No. Drones aren’t allowed in the restricted area over the display.

    Where are the toilets?

    There are promenade toilets, including the Undercroft at North Pier, and Changing Places toilets at the Tower and the Holiday Inn on Talbot Road. Full details in .

    Are the trams running?

    Yes, but the promenade closes to traffic and the trams run as a split service while the show is on. See for the details.

    Where can I park?

    The council car parks fill early, and the prom roads close from mid morning, so I would advise coming by train if you can. Blue Badge holders get 3 hours free in most council car parks. More in .

    Can I camp on the beach?

    No. Camping and tents overnight aren’t allowed, so please see the rules before you go. Have a look at for places nearby.

    Are the Illuminations on?

    Not during the air show. In 2026 the Illuminations were switched on on 4 September, a few weeks after the show.

    Seafront guide

    Three more things to know about the seafront

    Sea Mist And Wind

    A seafront show really has its own weather. The Irish Sea is right in front of you, and it can be bright in town while a bank of mist sits over the water, just where the aircraft fly. I think it’s worth knowing what to look out for, so a grey morning doesn’t put you off.

    Who Decides?

    Fly or notThe display pilots and organisers
    WhenOn the day, display by display
    Latest newsVisit Blackpool and the show radio
    Why mist forms

    When warm, damp air drifts over cooler sea, it cools and turns into mist. It often sits just off the coast while the town stays sunny.

    It can lift quickly

    A misty morning doesn’t always mean a misty afternoon. The flying doesn’t start until the afternoon, so I would suggest coming anyway and seeing how it looks.

    Lower cloud, different show

    Display teams fly a full looping routine when the cloud is high, and a rolling or flat one when it’s lower. You’ll still see them, just a different display.

    The wind off the sea

    Blackpool faces straight out to sea, so there’s little shelter. Strong gusts can stop lighter aircraft and parachute jumps, and the prom feels much colder in the wind.

    Jack’s tipOn a misty day, I would tune in to the show radio for updates and look out to sea rather than straight up. The mist is often worse over the water than over the town.

    Get creative on the sea front

    At Blackpool you get the Tower and the piers with the sea behind them, which you just won’t find at an airfield. The catch is the light, as the sun moves in front of you through the afternoon. I would suggest using the sun guide to plan where you stand, and treating the later displays as a chance for silhouettes, smoke and sparkle on the water.

    Ideas to try

    • Try walking out on North Pier or Central Pier and looking back along the display line, so you can get aircraft with the Tower or the other pier in the frame.
    • Later in the afternoon, you can turn the backlight into a feature. I would expose for the sky and go for silhouettes, smoke trails and sun on the water.
    • I would stand south of the Tower, towards Central Pier, so more of the display is to your right and away from the sun.
    • I like to leave room in the frame for smoke, so it leads the eye to the aircraft.
    • You can try zooming out now and then to include the crowd, the beach or the Tower, so the photo says Blackpool.

    This is a seaside resort, so food, drink and toilets are never far away. The Air Show Village has its own food stalls, and there are cafes, chip shops and pubs all along the front and in the streets behind it. You’ll find the queues peak just before the flying starts.

    Food & drink

    There are food stalls in the Air Show Village, plus hundreds of cafes, chippies and restaurants in the town. Picnics are fine, but the promenade has a ban on drinking alcohol in public, and glass isn’t allowed on the beach or sea wall.

    Kids’ activities

    There are activities in the Air Show Village and the beach in front of the display, with the Tower, the piers and the arcades nearby if they need a break from the flying.

    Toilets

    Promenade toilets, including the Undercroft at North Pier, open 08:00 to 20:00 in summer, with accessible toilets on a RADAR key. I would go before the flying starts, and if you aren’t waiting for a particular display, use that slot as your break.

    Shade and seating

    There is almost no shade on the prom, so I would bring hats, sun cream and your own chairs. The sea wall steps make a handy seat if you get there early.

    Accessibility
    The promenade is flat and paved, and the trams are wheelchair accessible. Blue Badge holders get 3 hours free with a clock in most council car parks, and Central car park has 42 Blue Badge spaces. Accessible toilets along the prom use a RADAR key, and there are Changing Places toilets at Blackpool Tower and the Holiday Inn on Talbot Road. I couldn’t find a dedicated accessible viewing area, so I would advise arriving early for a clear spot by the sea wall.
    What can I bring?
    Folding chairs, blankets, food and soft drinks. There’s no drinking alcohol in public on the promenade, as police and council staff can confiscate it, no glass on the beach or sea wall, no camping or tents overnight, and no drones in the restricted area over the display. Please see the rules before you go.
    Display times and commentary
    The display times for each day go online before the show, and a show radio station on DAB carries the commentary and traffic updates.
    Medical help
    Ask any steward or police officer and they will point you to the nearest first aid point.
    The tide
    If you sit on the sand, it’s worth keeping an eye on the tide. On a big high tide the sea comes up to the sea wall, so be ready to move up to the promenade. The weather box on the Plan your day tab shows the approximate tide times.
    If the weather turns
    Low cloud, sea mist or strong wind can change the programme or stop some displays. It is a two day show, so if Saturday looks poor, Sunday may be better. I would pack a waterproof either way.
