The show at a glance

Headcorn Aerodrome, also known as Lashenden, is a working grass airfield just south of Headcorn village in Kent. For one weekend in June it hosts a warbird air show with a big ground show, over 100 traders and an on site campsite.

The Headcorn International Airshow runs over two days, Saturday 26 and Sunday 27 June 2027, with a Futures and Arrivals Day on Friday 25 June when the display aircraft fly in. It took over from the Battle of Britain Airshow in 2026, and that first year brought more than 30 aircraft from 18 operators in four countries, four Hurricanes, more than 20 Second World War veterans and an RAF Chinook for Armed Forces Day. The 2027 line-up is due to be revealed in the new year.

It’s a smaller, more relaxed show than the big military air shows, on open grass with the flying close to the crowd. About 20,000 people come over the weekend. Parking is free with no booking, and you can stay on the airfield campsite. I would suggest it if you love historic aircraft and want a calmer day, and I would advise booking your tickets early, as there are no sales on the gate.

The airfield

Headcorn is a working grass airfield in the Kent Weald. In 1944 it was Lashenden, an advanced landing ground for fighter squadrons, so watching a Spitfire or Mustang take off from the grass here gives you a real sense of how it was done.

The Lashenden Air Warfare Museum sits on the airfield. Its opening on show weekend isn’t published, so I would suggest checking its own website before you go.

    Our view

    Headcorn is a proper warbird show on a grass airfield, with the aircraft close to the crowd and none of the queues of the biggest events. If you love Spitfires and Hurricanes it will work well for you, and camping on the airfield makes a weekend of it. If what excites you is fast jets and military noise, I would suggest pairing Headcorn with RIAT or the RAF Air Show at Cosford.

    Headcorn’s Story

    I always think a day at an air show means more when you know where you’re standing. Headcorn looks like a quiet grass airfield in the Kent countryside, but in the summer of 1944 it was a busy fighter base, and that history is a big part of why warbirds feel so at home here.

    1. 1943

      Lashenden Is Built

      Ahead of the invasion of Europe, a string of advanced landing grounds was laid out across Kent. Lashenden, near Headcorn, was one of them: grass runways, steel matting and tents, built to get fighters as close to the Channel as possible.

    2. 1944

      Fighters Over The Weald

      In 1944 Lashenden was home to fighter squadrons supporting D-Day and the push into France, including American P-51 Mustangs. Aircraft flew from the grass much as the warbirds still do on show weekend.

    3. After

      Back To Farmland

      When the war moved on, the advanced landing grounds were handed back to the land. Lashenden was the one that came back to life as a flying field, and today it’s Headcorn Aerodrome.

    4. Museum

      Lashenden Air Warfare Museum

      A volunteer run museum on the airfield keeps the story alive, with wartime relics and the history of the landing ground. Its opening on show weekend isn’t published, so check its website before you go.

    5. 2026

      A New Airshow

      The Headcorn International Airshow replaced the Battle of Britain Airshow in 2026, with more than 30 aircraft from 18 operators in four countries, four Hurricanes together and more than 20 veterans as guests.

    6. Today

      A Living Airfield

      Today it’s a busy grass airfield all year, with flying lessons, Spitfire flights, wing walking, balloon flights and skydiving. When you watch a warbird lift off from the grass here, you’re seeing it where fighters flew in 1944.

      Jack’s tipI would suggest a slow wander along the hangars before the crowds build. Small details like the old aircraft and the museum make the history feel very real.

    How arrival works

    Headcorn is reached by country roads, so the last mile can be slow on show mornings. Here’s what to expect, so a short wait doesn’t catch you out.

    1

    Car parks open at 09:00

    I would advise arriving about an hour before the gates open, so around 09:00 when the car parks open. Follow the local signs and the marshals rather than your sat nav, keep to 5 mph on site, and park on the grass where you’re directed.

    3

    Gates open at 10:00

    The ground show starts as the gates open. If you want a front row spot, head to the crowd line first and save your pitch, then look round the static aircraft, speak to the pilots, visit the trade stands and check the food.

    General parking is free and first come first served. Mustang Lounge guests have their own VIP car park. Overnight parking isn’t allowed in the day car parks, so if you want to stay over, book the airfield campsite. Re-entry is allowed on the same day if you keep your wristband on.

    Ways to arrive

    Driving, the train, the number 12 bus and taxis. Times and fares change, so check them before you travel.

    Do I need a ticket?
    Yes. Every ticket is booked in advance as an e-ticket, and there are no sales on the gate. For 2027, General Admission starts at £30 per adult, Fighter Row seats on the flightline start at £75 and the Mustang Lounge VIP experience starts at £140. Children aged 5 and under go free with a standard adult booking. Carers can book a carer ticket, priced like a child ticket. Tickets are only refunded if the whole show is cancelled.
    Do I need to book parking?
    No. General parking is free on grass fields beside the site, first come first served, with no booking. The car parks open at 09:00. Mustang Lounge guests have a private car park. Overnight parking isn’t allowed, so book the campsite if you want to stay.
    Is there parking for Blue Badge holders?
    Yes. Blue Badge parking is near the main entrance. It’s typically free, but spaces are limited and first come first served, with no booking process published, so I would advise arriving early. Display your Blue Badge, and if you don’t have one but need help, ask a marshal when you arrive.
    Should I follow my sat nav?
    Use TN27 9HX to get close, as the aerodrome is on Shenley Road just off the A274. Then follow the local signs and the marshals, who will direct you to the car park in use.
    Which roads close?
    No road closures are published. Headcorn is reached by country lanes, so the A274 and Shenley Road get busy as the car parks open and again when the show ends. I would suggest checking National Highways for the M20 and Kent County Council for local roadworks before you set off.
    How do I get home easily?
    Either leave before the flying display ends, or stay on: look round the static aircraft and the trade stands, grab some food and let the rush clear. The show closes at 18:00. If you’re going home by train, book a taxi back to Headcorn station before the day.

      Help On Show Days

      Show days are busy, and little things can catch you out, like a lost ticket, no signal or losing each other in the crowd. Here’s what to do if something goes wrong, plus quick answers to the questions I see asked most before every show.

      In an emergency, call 999. For anything else on the day, find the nearest marshal or member of the show team. They’re there to help.

      If Something Goes Wrong

      Lost Each Other

      Agree a meeting point as soon as you arrive, like the entrance or a hangar. Children must be with an adult at all times, and for younger ones I would advise writing your phone number on their wrist. If you can’t find them, tell a marshal straight away.

      Feeling Unwell

      Tell the nearest marshal or member of staff and they can get help to you. On a hot day people get caught out by the sun on the open crowd line, so drink plenty and take a break in the shade of the trade stands.

      Lost Your Ticket

      Check your junk folder for the booking email first, and screenshot your e-ticket before you leave home. Still nothing? Use the contact form on the official show website with your booking details.

      Need Water

      Bring a refillable bottle and plenty to drink. With more than 20 food and drink stands on site, you can top up through the day.

      Paying

      Payment methods for food and traders aren’t published, and there’s no cashpoint listed on site, so I would advise bringing a bank card and a little cash.

      Phone Signal

      Signal on a rural airfield with 20,000 people can be poor. I would suggest saving your tickets and this guide to your phone before you leave home.

      Bad Weather

      The show runs rain or shine, but historic aircraft are sensitive to wind and low cloud, so some displays may change or be cut short. Refunds are only given if the whole show is cancelled.

      Finding Your Car

      The car parks are big grass fields and they all look the same at 18:00. Drop a pin on your phone’s map when you park, it saves a lot of wandering.

      Quick Answers

      What time should I get there?

      The car parks open at 09:00 and the gates open at 10:00 with the ground show. Flying starts at about 12:00. I would suggest arriving around 09:00 if you want a front row pitch and time to explore before the flying.

      Can I buy tickets on the gate?

      No. Every ticket is an advance e-ticket, and there are no sales on the gate, so book before you travel.

      Can I bring chairs and a blanket?

      The official pages don’t say either way about folding chairs, so check the FAQ before you go. Ladders and step stools may be taken off you. Reserved seats come with Fighter Row and the Mustang Lounge.

      Can I bring my own food?

      The rules don’t mention bringing your own food and drink, so I would suggest checking the official FAQ. There are more than 20 food and drink stands on site.

      Can I bring my dog?

      No. Pets aren’t allowed at the airshow; only assistance dogs are welcome.

      Can I fly my drone?

      No. Drones are banned completely, and anyone flying one can be removed from the show and have it confiscated.

      Can I leave and come back?

      Yes, on the same day, as long as you keep your wristband on.

      Can I have a barbecue?

      Only on a camping pitch, and only a gas barbecue with a fire extinguisher. Open fires, fire pits and charcoal on the ground aren’t allowed.

      Can I take photos?

      Yes, photography is welcome. See the Photography section for the sun guide and settings.

      Is it on if it rains?

      Yes, the show runs rain or shine. Flying depends on the weather, and refunds are only given if the whole show is cancelled.

      Get creative on the airfield

      Headcorn gives you a grass airfield and Kent countryside as your backdrop, with uncluttered skies behind the aircraft. With the sun behind the crowd for much of the day, I would suggest the static aircraft in the morning and the flying from midday, favouring passes to your right later on.

      Ideas to try

      • Slow the shutter for the warbirds. 1/160 to 1/250 gives a proper prop disc, so pan smoothly and take a few frames each pass.
      • Watch the start ups and taxiing on the grass. Fighter Row puts you closest to them.
      • Wait for the formation passes and mock dogfights, and zoom out so both aircraft fill the frame with sky around them.
      • Aircraft pass close to the crowd, but a 400 to 600mm lens will help for the far passes.
      • Use the sun guide above to see where the light is for your spot.

      Food, facilities & what to expect

      More than 20 food and drink stands are spread across the showground, alongside over 100 traders and exhibitors. Here’s what to expect, what you can bring and what to leave at home.

      Food & drink

      The ground show has more than 20 food and drink stands, open all day. Through the year the aerodrome also has Stevie’s Cozy Dive Café and The Wingman Bar & Restaurant, though whether they open on show days isn’t confirmed.

      • Food and drink stands ↗ Spread across the showground. Expect queues around lunchtime.
      • Aerodrome café and bar ↗ On the south side by the hangars. Check show day opening with the aerodrome.
      • Mustang Lounge ↗ Catering is included for Mustang Lounge guests, from £140.
      • Bringing your own food. The rules don’t mention bringing your own food and drink, so I would suggest checking the official FAQ before you pack a picnic. Bring a refillable water bottle either way.

      Our advice: eat a little earlier or later than the lunchtime rush, so you’re back on the crowd line for the flying. Food options can change, so check the official site before your visit.

      Stay the weekend

      Camping on the airfield

      The official campsite is beside the Mustang entrance, and the price includes entry to both show days for everyone on the booking. For 2027 the early bird price is £149 for up to two people or £240 for up to four, booked in advance. There are toilets, showers, fresh water and a chemical disposal point, but no electric hook ups. Quiet generators and gas barbecues are allowed with a fire extinguisher; open fires, fire pits and charcoal on the ground aren’t. Quiet hours run from 22:30 to 07:00, and you leave by 12:00 on Monday.

      What to expect on the day

      Toilets

      Accessible toilets are confirmed on site. Details of the other toilets and baby changing aren’t clear on the official FAQ, so I would suggest checking before you go.

      Shade & weather

      The show runs rain or shine, and flying depends on the weather. It’s an open grass airfield, so bring sun cream, a hat and a waterproof.

      Paying

      Payment methods for food and traders aren’t published, so I would advise bringing a bank card and a little cash.

      Banned items

      Drones of any kind, pets other than assistance dogs, and open fires or charcoal barbecues. Ladders and step stools may be taken off you.

      Accessibility

      Blue Badge parking. Blue Badge parking is near the main entrance. It’s typically free, but spaces are limited and first come first served, so I would advise arriving early. Display your badge, and if you don’t have one but need help, ask a marshal.

      Carers. Carers can book a carer ticket, priced like a child ticket, one per disabled visitor. The show asks you to contact the team about access needs before visiting.

      Viewing and toilets. There’s an accessible viewing area and accessible toilets. The showground and car parks are grass, so they can be uneven, soft or muddy after rain.

      Wheelchairs and scooters. There’s no wheelchair or scooter hire on site, so bring your own.

      By train. At Headcorn station, Platform 2, for trains away from London, is step-free; Platform 1 is reached by a footbridge. Southeastern can arrange assistance if you contact them before you travel.

      Assistance dogs are welcome.

      Dogs
      Pets aren’t allowed at the airshow, only assistance dogs. On normal days the aerodrome welcomes dogs, but that doesn’t apply on show weekend, so leave pet dogs at home.
      Lost children
      Children must be with an adult at all times. Agree a meeting point when you arrive, write your phone number on younger children’s wrists, and if you get separated, tell the nearest marshal.
      If it rains
      The show runs rain or shine, but the flying depends on the weather, and historic aircraft are sensitive to wind and low cloud. Tickets are only refunded if the whole show is cancelled, not for reduced flying. Check the official social pages on the morning, and pack a waterproof whatever the forecast says.
      Ear protection
      Ear defenders are a good idea, especially for children, and baby ear muffs for babies. Engines start up close to the crowd.
