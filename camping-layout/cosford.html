RAF Cosford Air Show

The Royal Air Force’s own air show

Cosford is the only air show run by the RAF itself, on a working station next to the RAF Museum Midlands. You get the Red Arrows, the Typhoon and the Memorial Flight over a proper airfield crowd line, and below I’ve shared how I would go about planning your day.

The show at a glance

Cosford is a one day, ticket only show on an RAF station in Shropshire, a few miles from Wolverhampton and Telford. Around 50,000 people come, and in 2026 every ticket went before the day. Nothing is sold at the gate, so please don’t turn up without a ticket.

The thing that makes Cosford a bit different is that the RAF runs it. The flying display is built around the RAF’s own teams, with the Red Arrows, the Typhoon Display Team, the Falcons parachute team and the Battle of Britain Memorial Flight, plus visiting warbirds like the B-17 Sally B. Gates open at 08:00 and the flying starts late morning, so you have a few hours to take a look around the static park and the hangars first.

There’s quite a lot to see on the ground too. The static display sits on the airfield in front of the crowd line, the museum hangars are open, and the STEM and engineering areas are aimed at families and anyone thinking about a career in the RAF. This might work for your family if you are a mixed group, as long as everyone is happy to be on an airfield for the day.

The main things I would plan for are buying your tickets early and the queues on the A41 and at Cosford station. It’s also a one day event, so if the weather is poor there’s no second chance. I would advise arriving as the gates open, as this has worked for me in the past. If you are coming by car, parking is included in your ticket.

The RAF Village

This is the RAF’s own show, so the RAF Village is bigger here than anywhere else. It’s a great way for your kids to learn about and explore the different roles in the RAF, and if anyone in your group would like to join the RAF, it’s the place to ask any questions they may have.

Children love to see the RAF display teams on the ground, like the famous Red Arrows pilots and their ground crew, and they can get stickers and take photos with the pilots. The RAF Typhoon Display Team, the Black Cats and the Battle of Britain Memorial Flight are often there too.

    Ways to arrive

    This is based on the 2026 show. Train services, road closures and parking for 2027 haven’t been confirmed yet.

    Do I need a ticket?
    Yes, for everyone over 16, and only in advance. Nothing is sold at the gates and the 2026 show sold out. Under 16s go free with a paying adult, but you do need to say how many children are coming when you book. Tickets are non refundable and non transferable, and the only official resale is through See Tickets.
    Is parking really included?
    Yes. Parking on the airfield is included in the admission price. It is on grass, so vehicles must be no longer than 7 metres and under 7.5 tonnes. You can’t be dropped off at the airfield itself. The nearest drop-off point is the BP garage on the A41.
    Which roads close?
    In 2026 Newport Road in Albrighton by the fire station and at the junction with the A41, Rectory Road, Green Lane, Bowling Green Lane and Kennel Lane were closed from 07:00 to 17:30. Residents kept access, but there was no public parking, waiting or viewing on those roads. The A41 and A464 next to the airfield are clearways on show day, so you can’t stop or park on them.
    Which gate do I use?
    Once you see the air show signs, I would follow them rather than your satnav. In 2026 Gate 4 was open for arrivals from about 08:00 to 10:00 and for departures from about 18:00, and the one way system usually reverses around 13:00 so you can leave through Gate 2.
    Can I use the train if I can’t manage steps?
    Cosford station has no disabled access, so I wouldn’t recommend it if you can’t manage steps. I would use the disabled car park instead. Follow the wheelchair signs and display your Blue Badge. Spaces are limited and first come first served.
    Can I bring my bike or motorbike?
    Yes, there is parking for both at the airfield, but security is your own responsibility.

    Get creative on the airfield

    Cosford is kinder to photographers than a seafront show. The displays are close, there is a runway for take offs, landings and taxiing shots, and the hangars and control tower give you a background that says RAF. The crowd line faces towards the sun for much of the day, so I would suggest using the sun guide to plan your close up work for the afternoon, and spending the morning on the static park and the arrivals.

    Ideas to try

    • I would suggest shooting the take offs and landings. Heat haze off the runway softens things on a hot day, so try to get them early or wait for the climb out.
    • It is worth waiting for the banking passes at the 06 end. A topside view shows far more of the aircraft than a straight flypast.
    • Try leaving room in the frame for the Red Arrows’ smoke, so it leads the eye to the aircraft.
    • You can try zooming out now and then to include the hangars, the tower or the crowd, so the photo says Cosford.
    • I would walk the static park before 10:00, for clean shots without the crowds.

    Food, toilets & access

    Everything is on the airfield, so I would plan to eat, drink and rest on site. You can bring your own food and drink, and there is a food village, but there is no town to pop out to and the queues peak at lunchtime.

    Food & drink

    A catering village with hot and cold food, plus stalls across the showground. Picnics are welcome but cooking equipment isn’t allowed. Queues peak around lunchtime, so I would eat a little earlier or later, and bring cash in case the card machines lose signal.

    Kids’ activities

    The STEM and engineering hangars, simulators, the static park and the RAF Village keep younger visitors busy between displays, and the museum next door is free.

    Toilets

    Toilet blocks across the showground with baby changing next to them, and disabled toilets by the disabled car park. I would go before the flying starts, and if you aren’t waiting for a particular display, use that slot as your break.

    Shade and seating

    There is almost no shade on the crowd line, so think about bringing hats, sun cream and your own chairs. Gazebos aren’t allowed on the crowd line, but small shelters are fine further back.

    Accessibility
    The showground is flat but mostly grass, and it is a long walk from the car parks. A disabled car park sits close to the showground, first come first served, and Event Mobility Charitable Trust provides electric scooters and manual wheelchairs to book in advance. Qualifying adults can bring a registered carer free. Cosford station has no step-free access.
    What can I bring?
