Eastbourne Airshow

Airbourne, the UK’s biggest free seafront air show

Airbourne is one of my favourite air shows in the UK calendar. Classic warbirds, jets and display teams over the sea, a big RAF Village on the Western Lawns, and it doesn’t cost a penny to watch. Here’s how I would plan your days by the sea.

The show at a glance

Airbourne stretches along around two miles of Eastbourne seafront, so you can watch from the beach, the promenade or the lawns without a ticket. It has been voted the UK’s best free airshow six times.

What’s great about Eastbourne is that it’s a free four day event, bringing a mixture of classic warbirds, jets, display teams and more. The flying usually starts around lunchtime, so you have the morning to settle in, find your spot and explore the showground on the Western Lawns.

There’s a large ground presence of local stalls and food outlets, plus a few kids’ funfair activities. With the town, the pier and the beach right behind you, it works really well if some of your group aren’t as into aviation as you are.

The main things to plan for are the crowds, especially at the weekend, and the seafront road closures. I would advise travelling by train or bus and arriving early.

The RAF Village

The RAF come in force at Eastbourne with a large RAF Village. It’s a great way for your kids to learn about and explore the different roles in the RAF, and if anyone in your group would like to join the RAF, it’s the place to ask any questions they may have.

You’ll also see many of the RAF display teams on the ground, like the famous Red Arrows pilots and their ground crew, where kids can get stickers and take photos with the pilots. The RAF Typhoon Display Team, the Black Cats and the Battle of Britain Memorial Flight are often there too.

    Do I need a ticket?
    No. Watching from the seafront is free. You only pay if you want extras like official seating, pre-booked parking or a souvenir programme.
    Which roads close?
    The seafront road closes for the whole event, from Silverdale Road by the Grand Hotel to Marine Parade Road by the Crown and Anchor. At past shows Wilmington Square, Devonshire Place and Cavendish Place also closed. Residents keep access, and the 2026 order covered 10 to 18 August.
    Where do the buses go during the show?
    With the seafront closed, town buses turn in at the Crown and Anchor and use Pevensey Road. Out of town buses divert along Seaside. From Hastings, the 99 terminates in Gildredge Road near the station.
    Is there a day ticket for the buses?
    At past shows the Airbourne Flyer ticket covered all Stagecoach buses in the Eastbourne Dayrider zone: adult £5, under 16s £3, or a family ticket for £10. Concessionary passes weren’t accepted on it. Check the 2027 price before you travel.
    Can someone drop me off?
    Taxis aren’t allowed inside the road closure, so drop-off and pick-up is from the side roads. Agree a time and place in advance, as phone signal can be poor in the crowds.
    Can I bring my bike?
    You can cycle into town, but bikes can’t be taken into the main Airbourne site.

    Help On Show Days

    Airbourne is spread along miles of seafront, with hundreds of thousands of people over the four days, so it’s easy to get separated or caught out by the sun. Here’s what to do if something goes wrong, plus quick answers to the questions I see asked most.

    In an emergency, call 999. For anything else, head to the Information Tent on the Western Lawns or ask the nearest steward.

    If Something Goes Wrong

    Lost Each Other

    In 2026 there were free Kidzsafe wristbands for children, so I would advise picking one up when you arrive. Agree a meeting point too, like the Bandstand or the pier, and tell a steward straight away if someone goes missing.

    Feeling Unwell

    There were first aid points around the site in 2026, and any steward can get help to you. On a hot day the seafront has very little shade, so drink plenty and wear sun cream.

    Information Tent

    On the Western Lawns. They can give you a map, point you to the nearest cashpoint and toilets, sell programmes and give you the latest flying times.

    Flying Times

    The times are in the official programme. Show it to a programme seller, the Visitor Services Centre or the Information Tent for the daily updates.

    Bad Weather

    Sea mist and low cloud can change the flying at short notice. Keep an eye on Airbourne’s social media through the day for the latest.

    Loud Aircraft

    The Typhoon is very loud, and the sea wall can make it louder. Hearing protection is recommended for babies, toddlers and anyone sensitive to noise.

    Getting Home

    The seafront roads close and the station gets very busy at the end of the flying. I would suggest staying for something to eat in town while the crowds clear.

    In 2026 the Western Lawns showground was open 10:30 to 17:00, with the flying from around lunchtime until about 17:00. On the Saturday it started earlier, at 11:45. The 2027 times come out with the programme.

    Sea Mist And Wind

    A seafront show has its own weather. It can be bright and sunny in town while a bank of mist sits out over the sea, right where the aircraft fly. It’s worth knowing what to look out for, so a grey morning doesn’t put you off.

    Who Decides?

    Fly or notThe display teams and organisers
    WhenOn the day, display by display
    Latest newsAirbourne’s social media
    Why mist forms

    When warm, damp air drifts over the cooler sea, it cools down and turns into mist or fog. It often sits just off the coast while the town stays sunny.

    It can lift quickly

    A misty morning doesn’t always mean a misty afternoon. As the day warms up, the mist often thins or drifts away, so I would suggest staying put rather than giving up early.

    Lower cloud, different show

    Teams like the Red Arrows fly a full looping display when the cloud is high, and a rolling or flat display when it’s lower. You’ll still see them, just a different routine.

    The wind off the sea

    Strong or gusty wind can stop some displays, especially lighter historic aircraft and parachutists. It also makes the beach feel much colder, so bring layers.

    Jack’s tipOn a misty day, keep an eye on Airbourne’s social media for any changes, and look out to sea rather than at the sky above you. The mist is often worse over the water than over the town.

    Get creative over the sea

    Eastbourne has its challenges for photographers. The crowd line faces out to sea and towards the sun for much of the day, so aircraft can look dark against a bright sky, and sea haze can soften detail and flatten the colour. I would suggest using the sun guide to plan when you shoot, adding a little exposure compensation when you are shooting into the sun, and saving your close up work for the afternoon, when the light swings round behind you.

    Ideas to try

    • Wait for the banking passes. A topside or underside view shows far more of the aircraft than a straight flypast.
    • Leave room in the frame for smoke trails, so they lead the eye to the aircraft.
    • Zoom out now and then to include the pier, the beach or the cliffs, so the photo says Eastbourne.
    • Bright sea and sky fool the meter, so check your histogram early and add exposure compensation.

    Pick what’s flying and the weather for a starting point. It never goes faster than 1/2000 or above ISO 1200, so the files stay clean and the shutter stays realistic for the subject. Check your histogram after the first pass and adjust.

    Keep Airbourne Free

    Airbourne is free to watch, but it isn’t free to put on. The show can only carry on with donations and support from businesses, and even the evening concerts and fireworks rely on sponsors. If you enjoy your day, there are lots of ways to give a little back, and most of them get you something nice too.

    Food, toilets & access

    Eastbourne has a real advantage over airfield shows: a whole town sits right behind the display line. Eat on the showground, bring a picnic, or step back into town for a sit down meal.

    Food & drink

    Food outlets and local stalls across the Western Lawns, plus the cafés, pubs and restaurants in town. Queues peak around lunchtime, so eat a little earlier or later.

    Kids’ activities

    A few funfair rides, simulators and the RAF Village keep younger visitors busy between displays.

    Toilets

    On the showground and in town. Go before the flying starts, and if you aren’t waiting for a particular display, use that slot as your break.

    The beach

    The beach is shingle, so a low chair or cushion makes a long afternoon far more comfortable.

    Accessibility
    The promenade is flat and the Western Lawns are close to the town centre. The shingle beach is hard going for wheelchairs and pushchairs, so the promenade or lawns will be easier. Check the official website for accessible viewing and Blue Badge parking for 2027.
    Seating
    Official reserved and unreserved seating has been sold at past shows near the crowd centre. This may work for you if you have older relatives with you, or don’t want to arrive early to claim a spot.
    Drones
    Drones, balloons and other unmanned aerial devices aren’t allowed within the airshow site and the restricted airspace.
    Programme
    A souvenir programme is sold at the show, with the flying times and a site map. Proceeds help keep the show free.
    Supporting the show
    Airbourne is free to watch but costs a lot to put on. There are lots of easy ways to help, from buying a programme to a small monthly pledge. See .
    If the weather turns
    Sea mist, low cloud or strong wind can change the programme or stop some displays. Check the official channels on the morning and pack a waterproof.
