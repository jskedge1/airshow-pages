The world’s largest military air show

The show at a glance

RIAT is a three day show at RAF Fairford in Gloucestershire, on the edge of the Cotswolds between Cirencester and Swindon. It is run by the RAF Charitable Trust Enterprises and sells tickets only in advance. Nothing is sold at the gates apart from Park & View.

What makes RIAT different is the scale. Air forces from across NATO and beyond send their display teams, solo jets, bombers, tankers and transports, and the flying runs for more than seven hours on Saturday and Sunday. The 2027 headline theme is the Tiger Gathering, marking 50 years since the Silver Tiger Trophy was first presented at the Air Tattoo in 1977, so expect tiger striped jets from squadrons across the alliance.

The showground stretches for up to two miles along the south side of the runway, with a static park mostly on hard standing, the RAF Village, the Techno Zone, trade stands and food all day. Friday is a shorter highlights day with the western zone closed, and the aircraft arriving and leaving on Wednesday, Thursday and Monday can be watched from the Park & View enclosures.

The main things to plan for are booking everything early, from tickets to parking, camping and the shuttle buses, and the long walks. It is a big site, so I would advise thinking about how into aviation you are and which day suits your group, then arriving as the car parks open at 06:00.

The RAF Village

RIAT is run in support of the RAF Charitable Trust, so the RAF Experience and RAF Village are a big part of the showground, and the organisers have said it will be larger for 2027. It’s a great way for your kids to learn about and explore the different roles in the RAF, and if anyone in your group would like to join the RAF, it’s the place to ask any questions they may have.

You’ll also see many of the RAF display teams on the ground, like the famous Red Arrows pilots and their ground crew, where kids can get stickers and take photos with the pilots. The RAF Typhoon Display Team, the Black Cats and the Battle of Britain Memorial Flight are often there too.

    Do I need a ticket?
    Yes, and only in advance. Tickets aren’t sold at the gates, although you can still buy online on the day if any are left. Under 18s go free with a ticket holding adult, but you must order their free child passes, and 17 year olds should bring ID. Only Park & View can be bought at the gate. Tickets can be cancelled within two working days of buying, then they are non refundable.
    Do I have to pre-book parking?
    Yes. All parking at or near the showground is booked and paid in advance at £15 per car or £7.50 per motorbike, and you can’t pay on the day. If you haven’t booked parking, the organisers ask you to use the free Fast Track Park & Ride at South Cerney. Enclosure, FRIAT and official camping packages include parking.
    Which day should I go?
    Saturday and Sunday have the full flying display, 10:00 to 17:30, and the whole showground open. Friday is cheaper, with a limited number of tickets, a shorter highlights display from 11:00 to 15:00 and more aircraft arriving afterwards, but the western zone is closed. Arrivals and departures on Wednesday, Thursday and Monday are Park & View only.
    Can I be dropped off?
    No. Because of the one way system there is no drop-off or pick-up at the airfield. Use the South Cerney Fast Track Park & Ride for drop-off and pick-up, then take the free bus in.
    Can I get in with my Blue Badge on the day?
    Not for accessible parking. It must be booked in advance, and arriving on the day with a Blue Badge won’t get you access. Call Customer Services on 01285 713456 before you buy tickets to arrange parking, a free carer ticket and the Restricted Mobility Viewing Area.
    Can I bring a campervan?
    Yes, if your ticket includes parking, but gas bottles must be disconnected, and there is no cooking or overnight stay in the car parks. Book the official RIAT Campsite or a local campsite if you want to stay over.

    Help On Show Days

    RIAT stretches for up to two miles along the runway, with huge crowds over three days, so it’s easy to get separated or lose track of your things. Here’s where to go if something goes wrong, plus quick answers to the questions I see asked most.

    In an emergency, call 999. For anything else, ask a member of the Air Tattoo CREW, in turquoise uniforms with big foam aircraft hands, or go to an information point.

    If Something Goes Wrong

    Lost Each Other

    Lost and found children are looked after at the Central Visitor Information Point. Pick up a free wristband at any entrance gate or information point when you arrive, as it helps reunite families much faster.

    First Aid

    The first aid post is in the centre, in the aircraft bay next to the blue customer entry point. Anywhere else, ask a member of the team and they’ll call for help.

    Information Points

    The main one is in the centre of the showground, with two more in the east and west areas. They’re the best place for any question.

    Lost Property

    Anything handed in is kept at the Central Visitor Information Point for at least 30 days. After the show, you can ask about it by email.

    Ear Defenders

    RIAT doesn’t hire them out. The merchandise units sell some, but they may not suit very young children, so I would advise bringing your own.

    A Quiet Space

    There’s a multi faith room at the Central Visitor Information Point, and a Wellness Room quiet space too. More in .

    Bad Weather

    Low cloud or strong wind can change the programme. With three days, keep an eye on the official channels, as a poor day may push your plans to another.

    Finding Your Car

    The car parks are huge grass fields. Drop a pin on your phone’s map when you park, and note the colour of your car park.

    Quick Answers

    Can I leave and come back in?

    Tickets are valid for a single entry only, so I would plan to stay in once you’re through the gate.

    Do children need a ticket?

    Under 18s go free, but under 17s must be with a paying adult ticket holder. You need to order their free child passes when you book.

    Can I bring my bike?

    Bikes and scooters aren’t allowed on the showground. You can lock them to the crowd barriers in the Red and Blue car parks.

    Where can I smoke or vape?

    Only in the designated outdoor smoking areas, never in event structures or within 10 metres of an aircraft.

    What can I bring, dogs, drones or a BBQ?

    Dogs, drones and barbecues aren’t allowed, but your own food, chairs and small shelters are. The full list, plus the Drop Zone, Wi-Fi and cashpoints, is in .

    Where do I meet my group if we split up?

    I would suggest agreeing on one of the three information points, east, central or west, before you go in. Phone signal can be poor on a busy airfield.

    What’s the Aircraft Checklist?

    A booklet listing the aircraft with their serial numbers, codes and squadrons. It’s brilliant for spotters, and sold in the tented shops and at the customer entry points.

    Get creative on the airfield

    RIAT gives you a long runway for take offs, landings and taxiing shots, a huge static park and light from behind you nearly all day. I would suggest spending the early morning on the static park before the crowds build, then working the flying from the crowd line, and thinking about Park & View on the arrivals days if you want close runway action.

    Ideas to try

    • Shoot the take offs and landings. Heat haze off the runway softens things on a hot day, so get them early or wait for the climb out.
    • Wait for the banking passes as aircraft turn back in at each end of the display line. A topside view shows far more of the aircraft than a straight flypast.
    • Leave room in the frame for the Red Arrows’ smoke, so it leads the eye to the aircraft.
    • Zoom out now and then to include the crowd, the flags or the static park, so the photo says RIAT.
    • Walk the static park soon after the gates open at 07:30 for clean shots without the crowds.
    • Look out for the Tiger Gathering jets in their special schemes, both flying and on static.

    Freemans runs the public catering, with street food, coffee, ice cream and bars along the showground, and the number of outlets is up for 2027. BBQs aren’t allowed anywhere on the site. Eat a little earlier or later to miss the lunchtime rush.

    Things to do

    The RAF Village and RAF Experience, the Techno Zone, the Vintage Village, trade stands and the static park keep everyone busy between displays, with new themed zones promised for 2027.

    Toilets

    Toilet blocks along the showground, each with an accessible toilet. Go before the flying starts, and if you aren’t waiting for a particular display, use that slot as your break.

    Shade and seating

    There is very little shade on the crowd line, so bring hats, sun cream and your own chairs. Shelters up to 1.5 metres are allowed, but no full height gazebos or tents, and nothing tall within 10 metres of the crowd line.

    Accessibility
    The site is up to two miles end to end. The static park is mostly hard standing, but the grass to the crowd line can be long and uneven. Accessible parking is on base in the Red Car Park and must be pre-booked. There is a Restricted Mobility Viewing Area for wheelchair and scooter users at the west end, with a High Dependency Unit toilet there, and an accessible toilet in every block. A free carer ticket is available for Blue Badge holders, or Access Card holders with +1 or +2: call Customer Services on 01285 713456 before you buy. Event Mobility hires electric scooters at £35 and manual wheelchairs at £15 a day, online only. There are Easy Access Lanes at the entry points and a Wellness Room quiet space near mile marker 34.
    What can I bring?
    Folding chairs, blankets, food and drink. Shelters and stepladders up to 1.5 metres, but not within 10 metres of the crowd line. No BBQs, no drones (flying one is a criminal offence), no dogs, and don’t leave pets in the car, as the police patrol the car parks. Bags are searched on entry, so pack light and leave camping trolleys at home. Bikes aren’t allowed on the showground.
    Left luggage, Wi-Fi and getting around
    There is a free Drop Zone for chairs and bags at the central information point, a free bus around the site and free Wi-Fi. ATMs are at the east, central and west information points and the Take Off Zone, with a small fee.
    Smoking
    Only in the designated outdoor smoking areas.
    Medical help
    Medical help is on site. Ask any steward or information point and they will direct you.
    If the weather turns
    Low cloud or strong wind can change the programme or stop some displays. With three show days, check the forecast and the official channels before you set off, and pack a waterproof.
