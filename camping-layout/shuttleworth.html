Shuttleworth Air Shows

Vintage aircraft flying from a grass airfield in the Bedfordshire countryside

From Edwardian machines to Second World War fighters, Shuttleworth is like nowhere else I have been. Here’s how I would plan a day at any of the seven 2027 shows, from the new parking set up to where I like to stand for those lovely evening displays.

The shows at a glance

I like to think of Shuttleworth as a living museum of flight, on its own grass aerodrome at Old Warden, near Biggleswade. Most of the aircraft in the hangars still fly, and on show days you will see them take to the air alongside visiting warbirds and display teams.

Shows here feel very different from a big military air show. The airfield is small, the crowd line curves round the grass and you're close to the aircraft, both in the air and in the hangars. Most shows run from 09:00, with the flying starting at about 14:00, and several carry on into the evening with a second display as the light softens. That evening display is my favourite part of the day.

Your ticket also covers the Collection's hangars, the Swiss Garden and ground attractions such as the fun fair, Discovery Zones and vintage bus rides. Under 18s go free at every show apart from the Flying Proms, and parking is free, so I think it's one of the better value days out in the calendar. I would suggest picking your show by theme. The Military Air Show and Summer Air Show suit warbird fans, while Flying Proms is a special evening out with a live orchestra, and this might work well if you are planning something for the whole family.

The Collection

Your ticket includes the Shuttleworth Collection itself, with hangars full of aircraft that still fly, from Edwardian pioneers to Second World War fighters. Many of them take part in the display later in the day, so I would suggest walking the hangars in the morning. It's a great way to see them up close before you watch them fly.

The crews are often working on the aircraft between flights, and in my experience they’re usually happy to chat. Children love asking them questions, and if you're an enthusiast it's a lovely way to learn the history behind each aircraft.

    Do I need to book parking?
    No. Parking is free at every 2027 show and you don't need to book it. If you are part of a car club, you can arrange display parking through Shuttleworth's groups team (groups@shuttleworth.org).
    Is there Blue Badge parking?
    Yes. Shuttleworth has dedicated Blue Badge parking in a space central to the air show event area, entered through Alder Drive like everyone else. When I last checked, nothing was said about booking it in advance or sending proof beforehand, so in practice it works as turn up on the day. I'd suggest you have your Blue Badge on display and tell the stewards at the ticket check that you need the Blue Badge area. Spaces aren't unlimited, so I would advise arriving early and calling 01767 627927 before the show if you'd like to check.
    Can I use the Hill Lane entrance?
    Not on show days. Hill Lane (SG18 9EP) is for daily admission only, and the lane is closed while the flying displays are on. For every air show you will need Alder Drive (SG18 9DT).
    Will there be traffic?
    The lanes round Old Warden are narrow, so queues build before the gates open and again after the last display. It's a good idea to check the A1 before you set off, particularly around the Black Cat roundabout north of the site, where major road works were under way in 2026.
    How do I get home easily?
    Everyone leaves at once after the finale. I would suggest waiting 30 to 45 minutes with a flask and the last of the picnic. In my experience it usually gets you home just as quickly and with far less stress. If you're on the shuttle bus and want to see everything, think about booking a later return time.

    Shuttleworth’s Story

    I have found that knowing the story behind Shuttleworth makes a visit feel completely different. This whole place exists because of one young man who loved cars and aeroplanes, and a mother who wanted his name to live on. When you watch his aircraft fly today, you’re seeing that wish come true.

    1. 1872

      The Old Warden Estate

      Joseph Shuttleworth bought the Old Warden estate in 1872. The Swiss Garden was already here, created in the 1820s by Lord Ongley, and Joseph added the grotto and rock cascades you can still walk through today.

    2. 1909

      Richard Is Born

      Richard Ormonde Shuttleworth was born on 16 July 1909, the same year Louis Blériot flew across the English Channel. Funnily enough, a Blériot would one day become one of his prized aircraft.

    3. 1928

      It Starts With A Car

      The collection began in 1928 with an 1898 Panhard et Levassor, Richard’s first historic car. He went on to race at Brooklands under the name R. Ormonde, and in 1935 he won the Donington Grand Prix.

    4. 1932

      Into The Air

      Richard inherited the estate in 1932, and the same year his first aircraft arrived, a de Havilland Moth and a Comper Swift. More soon followed, including Desoutters and a de Havilland Dragon.

    5. 1936

      The Blériot And The Deperdussin

      In 1936 he bought a Blériot XI and a Deperdussin, and restored the Blériot to fly at a Royal Aeronautical Society garden party later that year. Sadly, a serious crash at the South Africa Grand Prix ended his racing career that year too.

      Seeing the Edwardian aircraft fly on a calm evening is the moment I would never miss here. They are among the oldest flying aircraft anywhere, and I would recommend staying for them if you can.

    6. 1940

      A Life Cut Short

      Richard joined the RAF Volunteer Reserve in 1939. In 1940, aged just 31, he was killed in a training flight in a Fairey Battle.

    7. 1944

      In His Memory

      His mother, Dorothy Shuttleworth, turned the estate into the Richard Ormonde Shuttleworth Remembrance Trust in 1944, to help people learn about agriculture, aviation and motoring. That trust is why the Collection is here today.

    8. 1961

      A Spitfire With A Story

      Spitfire AR501 and Sea Hurricane Z7015 joined the Collection in 1961. In 1942 AR501 escorted the famous B-17 Memphis Belle on a bombing mission, and in 1968 both aircraft starred in the film Battle of Britain.

    9. 1963

      Open To Everyone

      The Collection opened to the public in 1963, and the first air show followed in 1964. In 1965 the de Havilland DH.88 Comet arrived, the red racer that won the 1934 race from England to Melbourne.

    10. Today

      Still Flying

      Shuttleworth celebrated 60 years of air shows in 2024, and the aircraft are still kept flying with huge help from volunteers. It’s a family story as much as an aviation one, and I always feel that the moment I walk in.

      Jack’s tipI would suggest leaving time for the Swiss Garden as well as the hangars, and Shuttleworth House if it’s open on your day. It’s all part of the same story, and it makes the day feel complete.

      Help On Show Days

      Shuttleworth shows are relaxed and friendly, but a few things still catch people out, like the dog rules, paying by card and no published display times. Here’s what I would do if something goes wrong, plus quick answers to the questions I see asked most.

      More helpIf the forecast looks grim, have a look at my rainy day plan. If you or someone in your group finds noise and crowds hard, my calm visit guide may help. And for the end of the day, there’s getting home from an airshow.
      In an emergency, call 999. For anything else on the day, find the nearest steward or member of the Shuttleworth team. They’re there to help.

      If Something Goes Wrong

      Lost Each Other

      I would suggest agreeing a meeting point as soon as you arrive, like a hangar number, and writing your phone number on younger children’s wrists. I see lots of families do this. If you can’t find them, please tell a steward straight away.

      Feeling Unwell

      Tell the nearest steward or member of staff and they can get help to you. There’s very little shade on the crowd line, so I’d drink plenty and use the hangars to cool down.

      Paying

      Shuttleworth prefers cashless payment, and cards and phone payments are taken. Cash is only accepted where there’s no other option, and change isn’t guaranteed, so you might want to bring a card.

      Programmes And Maps

      If you’ve pre-booked a programme or poster, you can collect it from the Visitor Centre shop or the Information Pod. Before the big shows a site map is emailed out, so I would keep an eye on your inbox.

      Bad Weather

      Vintage aircraft are very sensitive to wind and rain, and line-ups can change even on the day. I have found the hangars are your best shelter until it clears.

      No Display Times

      Shuttleworth doesn’t publish times for each display, so if there’s an aircraft you really want to see, I would advise not wandering too far once the flying starts.

      The Shuttle Bus

      The bus from Biggleswade station is booked with your tickets, with set return times. I'd suggest you check yours before the evening display, and take a look at for taxis if you miss it.

      Finding Your Car

      The car parks are grass fields with no shade, and they look very different after dark at the evening shows. I always drop a pin on my phone’s map when I park, and I would suggest you do the same.

      Quick Answers

      What time should I get there?

      The gates open at 09:00 at most shows, and 10:00 at the Flying Proms. If you’re driving, I would advise arriving about an hour before, around 08:00. The flying starts at about 14:00.

      Which entrance do I use?

      You will need the Alder Drive entrance, postcode SG18 9DT, for every air show, or you can follow the event signs on the day.

      Can I bring my dog?

      Well behaved dogs on leads are welcome on the airfield paddocks, in the Runway Café Sunroom and in the Visitor Centre. They aren’t allowed in the hangars, the main café, the Swiss Garden, Shuttleworth House or the parkland, and not at all at the Flying Proms. Service dogs are welcome everywhere.

      Can I bring a tent or gazebo?

      Gazebos and oversized umbrellas aren’t allowed. Well tethered beach tents up to 1.2m high are fine, and I see plenty of families bring one.

      Can I bring my own food?

      Yes, you can bring your own food and drink. Barbecues aren’t allowed, and please don’t bring sharp knives. There’s also the Runway Café and food stalls on the day if you would rather buy something.

      Do they take cash?

      Card and phone payments are preferred. Cash is only taken where there’s no other option, and change isn’t guaranteed.

      Do children go free?

      Under 18s go free at every show apart from the Flying Proms, where children aged 4 and over pay £22.

      Is there baby changing?

      Yes, there’s baby changing around the site, including the Visitor Centre and the hangar toilets.

      When does each aircraft display?

      Shuttleworth doesn’t publish times for individual displays, and the line-up can change on the day because of the weather, so it’s a good idea to stay close once the flying starts.

      Can I fly my drone?

      No, drones aren’t allowed.

      Can I smoke or vape?

      Only in the designated smoking areas.

      Can I fly in to the show?

      Only with prior permission from the airfield first, known as PPR.

      Good To Know

      No dogs on the airfield

      Dogs can’t go on the tour. If you’re bringing one, someone will need to stay with it on the paddocks, so think about who that might be.

      Support for younger visitors

      Under 18s need someone with them to support them at all times on the tour.

      Mobility

      The ground is uneven over some distance, and anyone with mobility issues needs support at all times, so please bear this in mind when you book.

      No tour? Still get close

      Visiting and Collection aircraft line up on the grass between flights, and I have found the crews are often happy to chat.

      For photographersA tour is a brilliant chance for close detail shots, like cockpits, engines and the wonderful old wooden propellers. I would suggest a wider lens and taking your time with each aircraft.

      Get creative on the airfield

      Old Warden is a small, curved grass airfield, so the aircraft are close and you see a lot of topsides. The morning is into the sun, so I would suggest spending it in the hangars and with the aircraft parked on the grass, then saving your best cards for the afternoon and the evening display.

      Ideas to try

      • Slow the shutter for the old propellers. I have found 1/125 or slower gives a full disc on the Edwardian and Golden Age aircraft.
      • Try the take offs and landings on the grass. The aircraft taxi close to the crowd, so a shorter lens works well here.
      • You might want to wait for the banking passes as aircraft follow the curve of the crowd line. A topside view shows far more of the aircraft.
      • Stay for the evening display if you can. With the sun low behind you, the light is warm and the wind usually drops for the oldest aircraft.
      • I would walk the hangars and the aircraft on the grass before 12:00 for clean shots without the crowds.

      Good To Know

      The quick answers I get asked most about Shuttleworth Air Shows. I last checked these on 3 November 2026, so I would still look at the show’s own rules before you go.

        I couldn’t find anything about pushchairs in the show rules. The paddocks and car parks are grass, so I would bring one with sturdy wheels and ask Shuttleworth before you go if you’re unsure. Baby changing is in the Visitor Centre and the hangar toilets.

      Food & drink

      You’ll find a line of caterers set up near the crowd line, alongside Shuttleworth's own Runway Café for a sit-down meal. I have found the queues are longest just before the flying starts at about 14:00, so I would suggest eating early or bringing a picnic.

      • Picnics welcome. You can bring your own food and drink, but please leave sharp knives and glass bottles at home.
      • No barbecues. BBQs and open fires aren't allowed anywhere on site.

      What to expect on the day

      Toilets

      Accessible toilets at the Visitor Centre, the Runway Café, between Hangars 4 and 5 and in the Swiss Garden, plus extra show day units. Baby changing in the Visitor Centre and hangar toilets.

      Shade & weather

      There's no shade in the car parks and very little on the crowd line. Small beach tents up to 1.2m high are allowed if well tethered, but gazebos and big umbrellas aren't.

      Ground attractions

      Included with your ticket at most shows: the fun fair, Discovery Zones, vintage bus rides, the Collection's hangars and the Swiss Garden.

      Banned items

      Drones, BBQs, fireworks and flares, Chinese lanterns, laser pens, ladders, powered segways, scooters and skateboards, glass bottles and weapons.

      Accessibility

      Blue Badge parking. There's dedicated Blue Badge parking central to the air show event area, entered through Alder Drive. When I last checked, there was nothing saying you need to book it or send proof beforehand, so it's turn up on the day. I would display your Blue Badge (the national disabled parking permit) and tell the stewards at the ticket check. Spaces are limited, so I'd arrive early. If you want certainty, you can call 01767 627927 before the show.

      Carers go free. Carers of disabled visitors can get a free ticket, which you book by emailing enquiries@shuttleworth.org. Staff do random spot checks of carer paperwork on the day, so you might want to bring proof with you, such as a benefit award letter or an Access Card.

      Getting around. The Visitor Centre has step-free access with a ramp, and the hangars have ramps throughout. The paddocks and car parks are grass, which can be uneven or soft after rain, and some parking near the hangars is on gravel.

      Mobility scooters. Shuttleworth's banned list includes powered segways, scooters and skateboards. This is aimed at leisure scooters, but if you use a mobility scooter I would advise contacting Shuttleworth before your visit to confirm.

      Assistance dogs are welcome across the whole site, including areas where pet dogs aren't allowed. The on-site Garden Suite and the Flat aren't wheelchair accessible.

      Dogs
      Well-behaved dogs on leads are welcome on the airfield paddocks, in the Runway Café Sunroom and in the Visitor Centre. They're not allowed in the hangars, the main café, the Swiss Garden, Shuttleworth House and its parkland, or at the Flying Proms. Vintage engines are loud, so I would think about how your dog will cope with the noise.
      Lost children
      I would agree a meeting point when you arrive, such as a hangar number, and write your phone number on younger children's wrists.
      If it rains
      Vintage aircraft are more weather sensitive than modern ones, so some displays may be shortened or cancelled in strong wind or low cloud. The hangars give shelter. I'd suggest you check Shuttleworth's social media on the morning, and pack a waterproof whatever the forecast says.
      Smoking and vaping
      Only in the designated areas.
