International Ayr Show

Festival of Flight. Free on Ayr seafront, Friday evening and Saturday

Ayr’s free seafront show has become one of Scotland’s biggest days out, with around 260,000 people along the shore over the 2025 weekend. It runs over two days in early September: a Friday evening display that flies into the sunset over the Firth of Clyde, and a full Saturday afternoon programme. You get the Red Arrows, the RAF Typhoon, the Battle of Britain Memorial Flight and international visitors, with the Festival Village on the Low Green behind you and the Isle of Arran across the water.

The show at a glance

Ayr is a free, two day seafront show over the Firth of Clyde. There are no gates and no tickets for the main viewing areas, you just turn up and find a spot on the Esplanade, the Low Green or the beach. Around 260,000 people came over the 2025 weekend, with the biggest crowd on the Saturday.

The display is flown out over the sea in front of the Low Green, and the roads round the seafront close for the weekend. In 2026 the Friday evening flying ran from about 18:10 to 20:40, finishing with Airborne Pyrotechnics against the sunset, and the Saturday ran from 13:30 to 17:30. The Red Arrows flew on both days, alongside the RAF Typhoon, the Battle of Britain Memorial Flight, a Chinook, the Irish Air Corps Silver Swallows and the Red Devils.

Behind the crowd line the Festival Village fills the Low Green, with the RAF, Army and Navy, a STEM village, a funfair, a family zone, food and an Ayrshire trade village. The beach and the town centre are right there too, which makes it a good choice for a mixed group, as not everyone has to love aircraft.

The main things to plan for are which day suits you, how you’ll get there, and parking, which has to be booked in advance at the official car parks. I would advise the train from Glasgow if you can, as ScotRail runs extra trains and Ayr station is under a mile from the Low Green.

The Festival Village

The Festival Village takes over the Low Green, and in 2026 it was open from 16:00 to 21:00 on the Friday and 12:00 to 18:00 on the Saturday. You’ll find the RAF, Army and Navy in the Military Village, the RAF Benevolent Fund marquee, a STEM village, a funfair, a family zone, a food village with a bar, and local traders in the Ayrshire Trade Village.

It’s a great way for your kids to learn about and explore the different roles in the armed forces, and if anyone in your group would like to join up, it’s the place to ask any questions they may have. In 2026 the Red Arrows pilots met visitors in the RAF Village from 12:30 to 13:30 on the Saturday, first come first served. I would suggest walking round before the flying starts, as it gets very busy once the crowds arrive.

    International Ayr ShowParking & arrival guide

    Ayr is easy to reach from Glasgow and the west of Scotland. Ayr station is under a mile from the Low Green, ScotRail runs extra trains on show days, and the bus station is only a few minutes’ walk away. If you drive, the official car parks have to be booked in advance, and the roads round the seafront close for the weekend. I would advise the train if you can, or booking a park and ride space as soon as booking opens.

    Based on the 2026 show. Trains, buses, road closures and parking for 2027 are still to be confirmed.

    Do I need a ticket?
    No. The International Ayr Show is free and there are no tickets for the Esplanade, the Low Green or the beach. You only pay for parking, funfair rides, food and the two optional adults only zones, the Friday Flightline (£25 in 2026) and the Saturday Chill Out Zone (£15).
    Do I have to book parking?
    Yes, if you want an official car park. In 2026 spaces at Ayr Racecourse, Rozelle and Seafield were booked online through Tracsis, with number plate cameras at the entrance. Paying on the day cost £7 more, if there were spaces left, and bookings weren’t refundable.
    Is there a park and ride?
    Yes. In 2026 Stagecoach ran shuttle buses every 5 to 10 minutes from Ayr Racecourse on both days, and from Rozelle Estate on the Saturday only. The buses are low floor and take a limited number of wheelchairs, scooters and unfolded buggies.
    Which roads close?
    The Esplanade, Pavilion Road, Wellington Square, Bath Place, Blackburn Drive and the streets round the Low Green are closed to unauthorised vehicles, with no waiting zones across Ayr, Prestwick and Monkton. Cars in restricted areas are towed, and in 2026 it cost £200 to get them back. The full orders are published on Tell Me Scotland.
    Are there extra trains?
    Yes. On the 2026 Saturday ScotRail ran three trains an hour from Glasgow Central to Ayr, plus an hourly express, with extra carriages and a queue system at Ayr after the show. No special late trains were announced, so check the times for your day.
    Can I fly a drone?
    No. Restricted airspace covers the Low Green and the display area on both days, so leave the drone at home.

    Where to stay

    Ayr has hotels on the seafront and in the town, chain hotels by the A77, and plenty of holiday parks along the Ayrshire coast. In 2025 every place the council spoke to was fully booked for the show, so I would advise booking as soon as you know your plans.

    Get creative on the seafront

    Ayr gives you something an airfield can’t: the sea, the hills of Arran on the horizon and a Friday evening display flown into the sunset. The flip side is the light, as the sun moves in front of you through the afternoon. I would suggest using the sun guide to plan where you stand, and treating the Friday as a chance for silhouettes, smoke and pyrotechnics.

    Ideas to try

    • On the Friday evening, use a long focal length to line up aircraft against the hills of Arran and the setting sun.
    • Later in the afternoon, turn the backlight into a feature. Expose for the sky and go for silhouettes, smoke trails and sun on the water.
    • On the Saturday, stand south of the centre, towards Seafield, so more of the display is to your right and away from the sun.
    • Leave room in the frame for smoke, so it leads the eye to the aircraft.
    • Zoom out now and then to include the crowd, the beach or the Festival Village, so the photo says Ayr.

    Food, toilets & access

    Food, drink and toilets are close by, with a food village and bar in the Festival Village, cafes and takeaways round the beach, and Ayr town centre a few minutes’ walk away. The queues peak just before the flying starts.

    Food & drink

    In 2025 the Food Village had more than 30 street food traders, plus a bar and ice cream vans. Pirate Pete’s at the Pavilion has a cafe with sea view decking, and there are cafes, restaurants and takeaways round the beach and in town. A picnic on the seafront is a good way to save money.

    Kids’ activities

    The family zone, a funfair, a STEM village, a zip line and a drone soccer arena in 2026, plus the Red Arrows flight simulator. The funfair rides cost extra, and the beach is right in front of the display.

    Toilets

    Toilets across the site, accessible portable toilets, RADAR toilets at the Pavilion and a Changing Places toilet on the Low Green. Go before the flying starts, and if you aren’t waiting for a particular display, use that slot as your break.

    Shade and seating

    There is almost no shade on the Esplanade, so bring hats, sun cream and your own chairs. The ticketed Friday Flightline and Saturday Chill Out Zone have seating if you would rather book a seat.

    Accessibility
    Two free accessible viewing platforms, one at each end of the Esplanade, are for a disabled visitor and one companion, first come first served and not covered. The Blue Badge car park on Blackburn Drive had over 400 free spaces in 2026, right next to the Festival Village. There are accessible portable toilets, RADAR toilets at the Pavilion, a Changing Places toilet with a hoist on the Low Green and a quiet space in the welfare marquee by the Wellington Square entrance. Ear defenders aren’t provided, so bring your own.
    What can I bring?
    Folding chairs, blankets, food and soft drinks for the seafront. BBQs, fires, chairs, tables and awnings aren’t allowed in the car parks, and drones aren’t allowed anywhere near the display. I couldn’t find the official rules on alcohol or gazebos for the seafront, so check the show FAQs before you go.
    Display times and the programme
    South Ayrshire Council publishes the full flying schedule for each day before the show. In 2026 the official programme was £5 from the Destination South Ayrshire marquee in the Festival Village.
    Medical help
    Ask any steward or police officer and they will point you to the nearest first aid point.
    The tide
    If you sit on the sand, keep an eye on the tide. On a big high tide the beach in front of the Esplanade gets much narrower, so be ready to move up. The weather box on the Plan your day tab shows the approximate tide times.
    If the weather turns
    Low cloud, sea mist or strong wind can change the programme or stop some displays. The Friday and the Saturday are different shows, so if one looks poor, the other may be better. Pack a waterproof either way.
