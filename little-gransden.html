Little Gransden Air & Car Show

A charity air show and car show on a grass airfield

A friendly one day show on a grass airfield south east of St Neots, run by volunteers for BBC Children in Need and local charities. It mixes vintage aircraft, warbirds and aerobatics with more than 300 classic and specialist vehicles. This page has everything you need to plan your day, from roads and parking to where to watch, where to stay and the latest aircraft confirmations. I would advise reading it through before you go, especially if it’s your first visit.

      The show at a glance

      Little Gransden is a working grass airfield in Cambridgeshire, home to the Global Stars aerobatic team. Once a year it opens its gates for a family day out that's part air show, part classic car show and part village fête, all raising money for children's charities.

      The show has been running since the early 1990s and has raised more than £400,000 for BBC Children in Need and other charities. It's run by volunteers, and that friendly, community feel is a big part of why people come back year after year.

      The day starts with the gates opening at 10:00, which gives you time to walk the lines of more than 300 classic, vintage and specialist cars and motorcycles before the flying starts at around 12:30. Expect vintage aircraft, warbirds and aerobatics, with live music, trade stalls and food through the day. I would suggest it as a first air show, or as a relaxed day out if your group isn't all into aviation.

      The car show

      Little Gransden is as much a car show as an air show. More than 300 classic, vintage and specialist cars and motorcycles line the airfield, and the owners are usually happy to chat about them, so it’s a great day out if not everyone in your group is into aircraft.

      The gates open at 10:00 and the flying starts at about 12:30, so I would suggest saving your pitch on the crowd line first, then walking the car lines, the trade stalls and the aircraft parked on the grass before the displays begin.

      Our view

      Small airfield, big heart. If you want a relaxed day with proper flying, a huge car show and the feel of a village fête, Little Gransden is a gem, and every ticket helps children's charities. If what excites you is a big military line-up, I would suggest pairing it with one of the larger shows, but few days out feel this friendly.

      Little Gransden Airfield sits in the countryside between St Neots and Cambridge, just off the B1046. Most people drive, and parking is on site on the airfield, clearly signposted from the road. The nearest stations are St Neots and Sandy, both a taxi ride away.

      Ways to arrive

      Driving, the train and a taxi, and local buses. Times and fares change, so check them before you travel.

      Do I need to book parking?
      The official information says parking is available on site and clearly signposted from the road, and it doesn't mention booking or a separate charge. Check the official booking page when 2027 tickets go on sale, in case anything changes.
      Is there parking for Blue Badge holders?
      The official information says the event is accessible for most mobility needs, with open grass areas and toilets on site, but it doesn't say whether proof such as a Blue Badge is needed, or whether you should book ahead. At a recent show, accessible bays were signed beside the entry gate, which looks like first come first served, but I would advise contacting the organisers through the official contact page before you go, so you know what to expect when you arrive.
      Should I follow my sat nav?
      Only until you're close. Use SG19 3BP to reach the area, then follow the show signs and marshals. Some sat navs send drivers down the wrong lane on the way into airfields here, so look out for the signs.
      Will there be traffic?
      The roads are quiet country lanes, so even a small show creates queues near the entrance. Arriving early makes a real difference. Check National Highways for road works on the A428 and A1 before you set off.
      How do I get home easily?
      Everyone leaves along the same lanes, so the first half hour after the flying is the slowest. Either leave a little before the end, or stay on, look round the cars and stalls and let the rush clear. If you came by train, book your return taxi before the day.

        Little Gransden has a grass main runway, 10/28, running roughly east to west. The crowd line follows the runway on the side nearest the road, and curves round at the eastern end. The car show, trade stalls and food are behind the crowd line, with parking beyond them.

        Get creative on the airfield

        Little Gransden is a small grass airfield, so the aircraft are close and you get wings and wheels in one day. The crowd faces south, into the sun around the middle of the day, so I would suggest the aircraft on the grass and the car lines in the morning, then the best flying shots from mid afternoon as the sun moves to your right.

        Ideas to try

        • Slow the shutter for the vintage aircraft. 1/125 to 1/250 gives a proper prop disc, so pan smoothly and take a few frames each pass.
        • Shoot the take offs and landings on the grass. Aircraft taxi close to the crowd, so a shorter lens works well.
        • Head to the bend at the east end for a different angle as aircraft run in.
        • Pair an aircraft with a classic car in the same frame for a real Wings and Wheels shot.
        • Walk the car lines and the aircraft on the grass before 12:00, while the light is soft and the crowds are small.

        Food, facilities & what to expect

        Little Gransden is a field event, so food and drink come from stalls and vendors on the showground rather than permanent cafés. Here's what to expect, and what's worth bringing yourself.

        Food & drink

        The official site lists trade, craft and food stalls on the showground. Exact vendors change each year and are confirmed by the organisers nearer the show.

        • Food stalls on site ↗ At a recent show the food court included Cambridge Burger Co., Avalon Farm, Market Kebab, Pizza Mondo, donuts and pancakes and an ice cream van. Vendors change each year, so check the official information page nearer the day.
        • Bringing your own food. A picnic is a great way to save money, especially for families. Bring plenty of water as well, as the airfield is open grass with very little shade.
        • The After Show Party ↗ New for 2026, a separate ticketed evening with live music, a twilight flying display by Global Stars, a licensed bar and a hog roast, finishing with fireworks at dusk. Numbers were limited.

        Our advice: food stalls get busy around lunchtime. Consider eating a little earlier or later to avoid the queues.

        What to expect on the day

        Toilets

        The official information says there are toilet facilities on site. At a recent show there were portable toilets, including an accessible one, just inside the entry gate. Baby changing isn't mentioned, so ask the organisers if you need it.

        Shade & weather

        The airfield is open grass with very little shade. Bring sun cream and hats, and a waterproof as well. Displays may be adjusted for safety in bad weather, but the show continues.

        Paying

        The official information doesn't say whether stalls take cards. I would suggest bringing a little cash for stalls and charity collections, as well as a card.

        Not allowed

        Drones aren't allowed, as the airfield is a safety restricted zone. Respect the flying zones and follow the stewards' directions at all times.

        Accessibility

        Getting around. The official information says the event is open to all ages and accessible for most mobility needs, with open grass areas and toilet facilities on site.

        Parking and proof. At a recent show, accessible parking bays were marked with signs right beside the entry gate. The official site doesn't say whether you need to show a Blue Badge or book ahead, or whether it's first come first served, so I would advise contacting the organisers through the official contact page before the show.

        The ground. The whole site is grass, which can be bumpy, and soft after rain. Think about this if you use a wheelchair or mobility scooter.

        Dogs
        Dogs are welcome when kept on a lead. Bring water for them, as there's little shade, and think about ear protection or a quiet spot away from the crowd line if your dog is nervous of loud engines.
        Re-entry
        Re-entry is allowed, but keep your wristband on, as you'll need it to get back in.
        Lost children
        Agree a meeting point when you arrive, such as a landmark by the car show, and write your phone number on younger children's wrists.
        If it rains
        Displays may be adjusted for safety, but the show continues. Vintage aircraft are sensitive to wind and low cloud, so some may be shortened or cancelled. Check the show's Facebook page on the morning, and pack a waterproof whatever the forecast says.
