A1Pick your shows

Choose your 2027 shows

November to February

Start with the big dates that are already fixed, then fill the gaps. I pick one main show for the summer and one or two smaller ones around it.

  • Lock in the big ones first. RIAT is 16 to 18 July, and Cosford, Duxford and Shuttleworth have named their dates.
  • Be patient with the seaside shows. The August ones usually confirm over the winter, once councils set their budgets.
  • Match the show to you. Fast jets and big crowds, warbirds on grass, or a free seafront day all suit different people.

A2Budget and book

Set a budget, then book early

January to April

Tickets are only part of the cost. Work out the whole day first, then book the things that sell out.

  • Early bird tickets save the most. The big shows put prices up as the date gets closer, and some days sell out.
  • Book the bed before the ticket rush. Hotels near RIAT and Cosford go months ahead. Campsites at the show go fastest.
  • Car park or enclosure passes are often sold separately and in limited numbers, so buy them with your ticket.

My advice: put the date tickets go on sale in your phone calendar. That one reminder has saved me more money than anything else.

A3Getting there

Plan the journey

Spring, once you have tickets

The roads into a big show are the slowest part of most days. Pick how you travel before you pick what time to leave.

  • Train and shuttle bus often beats the car queue, and you can have a drink at the show.
  • Park and ride means a short walk at both ends, but a queue for the bus after the finale.
  • Driving? Pre-book parking, and print the route the show sends you rather than trusting the sat nav.

A4Pack the kit

Pack for sun and rain

The week before

An airfield is a big open field with no shade and not much shelter. You carry everything, so pack light but pack right.

  • Low folding chairs and a blanket. Higher chairs block the people behind you on the crowd line.
  • Sun cream and a waterproof. You will often need both on the same day.
  • Ear defenders for children, plus a power bank, water and a packed lunch to skip the food queues.

A5Night before

The night before

The evening before the show

An early start goes wrong in the morning when the tickets are lost or the phone is flat. Do it all the night before.

  • Check the forecast and the show news. Rain rarely cancels a show, but cloud changes the flying.
  • Save the tickets to your phone and print a copy. Signal at the gate is often poor.
  • Charge everything and leave the bag by the door, with the car keys on top.

B1Show day

Arrive early, walk the static

Show day morning

The gates open hours before the flying. That quiet first part of the day is when the show is at its best.

  • Get there as the gates open. The queues are shorter and you get the pick of the crowd line.
  • Find the toilets and a meeting point first, then put your chairs down.
  • Walk the static display before the flying. The crews are free to chat and the aircraft are clear of people.

B2Find your spot

Find the best place to watch

Before the flying starts

The display is flown to the middle of the crowd line. Where you sit decides what you see and what your photos look like.

  • Aim for the centre. The best passes and the formation breaks happen in front of the commentary box.
  • Check where the sun is. Shooting into it all afternoon makes every jet a silhouette.
  • Paid enclosures give you a seat, shade and a toilet with no queue, if the budget allows.

B3Getting home

Beat the rush home

After the finale

Everyone leaves at once. You either go before the last display or settle in and leave an hour later.

  • Leaving early? Go before the finale and you will miss the worst of the car park.
  • Staying late? Keep food and a drink back, let the queue clear and enjoy the quiet field.
  • On the train? Check the last shuttle and train times before the day, not in the queue.
