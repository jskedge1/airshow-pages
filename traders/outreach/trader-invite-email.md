## 1. What the email is for, in plain words

The guide already has a traders section: the Airshow Traders directory, the Trader Hub (where traders log in and run their own listing) and a "Traders at this show" block on every show guide page. What it needs now is traders in it.

The email asks every trader who has stood at a UK airshow to send in a free listing for the 2027 season. It explains:

* what the listing is and where it appears (directory, every show guide they list, searchable by show and by type of stall)
* why it helps them (visitors find them in the week before the show, not only when they are already walking round the trade village)
* what it costs (a free card forever, or paid plans from £19 a season, with the first 25 traders getting Flypast free for 2027)
* what they need to do (about five minutes on the sign up form)
* that nothing is listed without their say so, and how to opt out

The tone is Jack writing to a fellow regular on the circuit: short, friendly, honest about the site being new, and no hard sell.

## 4. Opening lines for each type of trader

Swap the second paragraph of the main email for the one that fits. Keep the rest the same.

**Food vans and street food**
> Food is the first thing most visitors ask about when they plan a show day: what is there, is there anything vegetarian or gluten free, and will I need cash. Your card answers all three before they arrive (it has dietary and payment tags), and food and drink always shows first in each show's trader list.

**Coffee, ice cream, sweets, fudge and doughnuts**
> Early arrivals, families with children and anyone queuing at the gate want to know where the first coffee or the best ice cream is. Food and drink always shows first in each show's trader list, so you are one of the first things they see.

**Bars, gin, beer, cider and prosecco**
> Plenty of visitors plan the end of the day as carefully as the flying: where to sit for the finale and where to get a drink. Your card lets them find your bar before they go, and you can post updates on the day (new barrel on, last orders, quiet right now).

**Aviation models, toys and diecast**
> Collectors follow the line ups closely and often travel to several shows a year. A listing lets them see in advance that you will be there, so they come with a shopping list (and a budget) instead of stumbling across you at 4pm.

**Aviation art, prints and photography**
> Enthusiasts and photographers are some of the guide's most regular readers. Your card links straight to your website or gallery, so people can browse your prints before the show and come and find you on the day.

**Clothing, patches, badges and gifts**
> Squadron patches, display team shirts and gifts are often an impulse buy. A listing turns that into a planned visit: people see you are at the show, look at your range at home and come and find your stand.

**Camera kit, binoculars and airband scanners**
> The guide has a full photography section (camera guides, kit planners and settings for each show), so readers are already thinking about kit when they find you. Your card sits under Camera and show kit, which is its own filter in the directory.

**Family fun, rides and activities**
> Parents plan around their children: something to do between displays, somewhere to let off steam. Family fun has its own filter in the directory, so they can find you while they are planning.

**Charities and associations with a stand**
> Visitors like knowing which charities they can support on the day. A free card tells them where to find you and links to your fundraising page.

## 8. Questions traders are likely to ask

**Is it really free?**
Yes. The Static Display card is free for good. The first 25 traders also get Flypast free for 2027.

**Do you take a cut of my sales?**
No. There is no commission on anything.

**Do I have to sign a contract?**
No. Paid plans are one payment a season with nothing to cancel. Ask to be removed whenever you like.

**When do I pay?**
Paid plans open before the 2027 season. Nothing is charged until your listing is checked and live, and you get your money back if you change your mind before your first show.

**How many people use the site?**
The guide is building its audience for 2027. Jack will share real figures as they come in, and won't promise numbers he can't show.

**Will you say I'm at a show I'm not at?**
No. A trader is only shown at a show once the trader or the organiser has confirmed it.

**Can I change my shows later?**
Yes, any time in the Trader Hub, and changes to shows go live straight away.

**I'm not on the twelve guides. Can I still join?**
Yes. Any trader who stands at UK airshows can have a directory card.

**Can you do it for me?**
Yes. Reply with your business name, what you sell, your shows and dates, a photo and your website or social link, and Jack will set it up.

## 9. Before you send: the rules for emailing businesses

This is a summary to help, not legal advice. The ICO's guide to the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR) is the place to check: ico.org.uk, "Business to business marketing".

* **Limited companies and LLPs** ("corporate subscribers"): you can send a marketing email without asking first, as long as you say who you are, give a valid address to reply to, and offer an easy way to opt out (the line at the bottom of the email does this).
* **Sole traders and some partnerships** ("individual subscribers"): marketing emails need their consent first. Many food vans and small stalls are sole traders. For these, the safer route is to contact them the way they invite enquiries (a phone call, or the enquiry route on their own site), ask the show organiser to pass the invitation on to their traders, or reach them at the show.
* Use only business details the trader publishes for enquiries. The list in this folder only contains details found on the traders' own sites or the show's pages. Never guess an email address.
* Send one to one, not a big mailing with everyone in the To line. If you send in bulk, use BCC or a mailing tool with an unsubscribe link.
* Say where you found their details (the line at the bottom of the email).
* Keep a "do not contact" list and honour every "no thanks" straight away. The tracker column `status` below has a value for this.
* Under UK GDPR, a business contact list is still personal data where it names a person. Keep it private (not on the public website), and delete details you no longer need.

A good extra route: email the trade and catering managers at each show and ask them to forward the invitation to their traders. That reaches traders you can't find online, and comes with the organiser's blessing.
