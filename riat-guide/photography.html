Photographing RIAT

From your first long lens to a full burst of afterburner, here’s how I’d shoot the Air Tattoo. Pick an aircraft type in the settings helper for a starting point, then tweak it for the light on the day.

Kit

What to bring

  • Long zoomSomething reaching 300 mm or more (on full frame) is the usual choice for the flying display. A 100–400 or 150–600 covers most of it.
  • Standard zoomFor the static park, crews and the crowd. A 24–70 or similar.
  • SparesBatteries and memory cards. You’ll shoot far more than you expect over seven hours.
  • FiltersA polariser helps with haze and glare; an ND filter helps you get prop and rotor blur on bright days.
  • ComfortA low chair, a strap that spreads the weight, and a rain cover for the camera.
  • Phone usersUse burst mode, don’t zoom past the optical lens, and tap to lock focus on the aircraft.

Light and the sun

You want the sun behind you, lighting the side of the aircraft you can see. Where the sun sits through the day depends on which way the crowd line faces, and at Boscombe Down that’s not been published yet.

As soon as the showground layout is out, I’ll add a sun guide here showing the best light for each part of the day. Apps like The Photographer’s Ephemeris or PhotoPills let you check it yourself once you know where you’ll be standing.

Haze and heat. On hot July afternoons heat shimmer softens long lens shots of distant aircraft. Morning and early evening usually give the crispest results.

Panning like a pro

  • Stand square to the crowd line and twist from the hips, not the arms.
  • Pick the aircraft up early and keep following it after you release the shutter.
  • Use continuous autofocus (AI Servo or AF-C) with a small group of focus points.
  • Turn image stabilisation to its panning mode if your lens has one.
  • Start at a faster shutter speed and slow it down as your hit rate improves.

The static park

RIAT’s static park is one of the biggest anywhere, with aircraft and crews from around the world. Go first thing in the morning, before the flying starts and before the crowds build. A wide or standard lens works best close up. Many crews love to chat, and some swap patches, but always ask before photographing people.

Rules and etiquette

  • Leave the drone at home. Boscombe Down is a military airfield with restricted airspace, and drones aren’t allowed at the show.
  • Don’t block other people’s view with tall tripods or stepladders on the crowd line.
  • Follow any photography restrictions signed around military aircraft.
  • Keep bags out of walkways, as the crowd line gets very busy.
