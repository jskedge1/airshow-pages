What we know about Boscombe Down

The move was announced on 4 November 2026. This is everything RIAT has confirmed, and I’ll keep it up to date.

Venue

A new, permanent home

The flying and the showground move to MOD Boscombe Down, a military test airfield just south of Amesbury, with the A303 and Stonehenge a few miles north. RIAT’s office stays in Fairford, but the show won’t go back there.

Tickets

Your tickets still stand

Bookings, including RIAT26 tickets rolled over to 2027, stay valid. Under 18s still go free with a ticket holding adult, and Friday Futures and FRIAT both carry on. Local resident discounts are due in early 2027.

The show

The same show you know

The flying display, the big static park and the chance to meet crews from around the world are all staying, along with the Techno Zone and the RAF Experience. The 2027 headline theme is the Tiger Gathering.

General admission

A general admission day ticket gets you onto the showground for the flying display, the static aircraft park, the Techno Zone, the RAF Experience, the shops and the food. It’s also the ticket you need before you can add an enclosure, book camping or ride the planned rail shuttles.

Pick the day, or days, you want. Friday has a four hour plus flying display; Saturday and Sunday have seven hours plus, and are the busiest days.

Price guide. RIAT26 day tickets at Fairford started at about £47 for an adult. Check RIAT for the RIAT27 price before you budget.

Which day should I go?

  • FridayShorter display, usually a little quieter. Good for a first visit or if you can take the day off.
  • SaturdayThe longest days, and the busiest. Arrive early for a front row spot.
  • SundaySame long display. Leaving is often the hardest part, so have your way home planned.
  • More than one dayLets you do the static park one day and the flying the next. Pair it with camping.

Pre-paid parking from Fairford

Any parking you bought for RIAT26 will be refunded automatically to the card you paid with (£15 a car, £7.50 a motorbike) by the end of December 2026. You don’t need to fill anything in.

If parking is offered at Boscombe Down, people who had pre-paid parking will get priority booking, so keep an eye on your email.

Arriving by car

  • The airfield is just south of Amesbury, close to the A303. Expect that road and the routes into Amesbury and Salisbury to be busy from early morning.
  • Follow the event signs and the traffic marshals, not your sat nav. Your route will depend on where your car park is.
  • Have your parking and admission tickets ready to show, on your phone and printed.
  • Take a photo of your car park row or a nearby sign. Big show car parks look the same at the end of a long day.
  • Car share if you can. Fewer cars means shorter queues for everyone.

Getting away at the end

At any big airshow, leaving is the slowest part of the day. Thousands of cars head out at once.

  • Stay for the final display and let the first rush go. Pack up slowly, get something to eat and leave later.
  • Fill up with fuel before the day, so you’re not hunting for a petrol station in the queues.
  • Keep water and snacks in the car for the wait.
  • Staying locally? A walk or shuttle back to your room beats sitting in traffic.

Park & View

Watch the aircraft fly in before the show and head home after it. For spotters and photographers these are some of the best days of RIAT week, with real arrivals, practice displays and departures, and far smaller crowds.

Arrivals

Thursday 15 July

Enclosure locations are still to be revealed. At Fairford there were two Park & View areas, east and west. RIAT says Boscombe Down’s will be announced in the coming months, and I’ll add maps here as soon as they are.

What is Park & View?

Park & View enclosures are fields next to the airfield where you park your car and watch from beside it. You’re not on the showground, so there’s no static park, but you see the aircraft land, taxi and take off.

Nothing runs to a timetable. Aircraft arrive when their transit flights allow, and RIAT warns that a few may arrive or leave outside the Park & View days.

Park & View tickets

Park & View is a separate ticket from the show days, sold per day through RIAT. Prices and booking for RIAT27 will come with the enclosure locations.

RIAT tickets → Ticket guide

What to bring

  • Chairs, and a windbreak or sunshade if allowed, as you’ll be there for hours
  • A full cool box, water and a flask, in case catering is limited
  • Binoculars and a notebook or spotting app for serials
  • An airband scanner, if you have one, to hear when aircraft are inbound
  • Sun cream, a hat and a waterproof
  • A power bank for your phone and camera batteries

Dogs aren’t allowed in RIAT’s Park & View enclosures, the showground or its car parks.

Spotter tips

  • Arrive early. The first arrivals can come in during the morning.
  • Follow RIAT’s social media and spotter forums for expected arrival times on the day.
  • Wind direction decides which way aircraft land, and so which enclosure gets the closer view.
  • Monday departures tend to be busiest in the morning, so don’t leave it too late.

For every camera

Photographing RIAT

From your first long lens to a full burst of afterburner, here’s how I’d shoot the Air Tattoo. Pick an aircraft type in the settings helper for a starting point, then tweak it for the light on the day.

Camera settings helper Tool

Kit

What to bring

  • Long zoomSomething reaching 300 mm or more (on full frame) is the usual choice for the flying display. A 100–400 or 150–600 covers most of it.
  • Standard zoomFor the static park, crews and the crowd. A 24–70 or similar.
  • SparesBatteries and memory cards. You’ll shoot far more than you expect over seven hours.
  • FiltersA polariser helps with haze and glare; an ND filter helps you get prop and rotor blur on bright days.
  • ComfortA low chair, a strap that spreads the weight, and a rain cover for the camera.
  • Phone usersUse burst mode, don’t zoom past the optical lens, and tap to lock focus on the aircraft.

Light and the sun

You want the sun behind you, lighting the side of the aircraft you can see. Where the sun sits through the day depends on which way the crowd line faces, and at Boscombe Down that’s not been published yet.

As soon as the showground layout is out, I’ll add a sun guide here showing the best light for each part of the day. Apps like The Photographer’s Ephemeris or PhotoPills let you check it yourself once you know where you’ll be standing.

Haze and heat. On hot July afternoons heat shimmer softens long lens shots of distant aircraft. Morning and early evening usually give the crispest results.

Panning like a pro

  • Stand square to the crowd line and twist from the hips, not the arms.
  • Pick the aircraft up early and keep following it after you release the shutter.
  • Use continuous autofocus (AI Servo or AF-C) with a small group of focus points.
  • Turn image stabilisation to its panning mode if your lens has one.
  • Start at a faster shutter speed and slow it down as your hit rate improves.

The static park

RIAT’s static park is one of the biggest anywhere, with aircraft and crews from around the world. Go first thing in the morning, before the flying starts and before the crowds build. A wide or standard lens works best close up. Many crews love to chat, and some swap patches, but always ask before photographing people.

Rules and etiquette

  • Leave the drone at home. Boscombe Down is a military airfield with restricted airspace, and drones aren’t allowed at the show.
  • Don’t block other people’s view with tall tripods or stepladders on the crowd line.
  • Follow any photography restrictions signed around military aircraft.
  • Keep bags out of walkways, as the crowd line gets very busy.

Questions about Boscombe Down

Tickets and bookings

Is RIAT still happening in 2027?
Yes. RIAT27 runs from Friday 16 to Sunday 18 July 2027 at MOD Boscombe Down in Wiltshire, with Park & View days on Wednesday 14, Thursday 15 and Monday 19 July.
Will it ever go back to Fairford?
No. RIAT has called Boscombe Down its new, permanent home. The RIAT office stays in Fairford, but the show itself won’t return there.
Are my RIAT26 rollover tickets still valid?
Yes. Existing bookings, including RIAT26 tickets rolled over to 2027, stay valid for Boscombe Down.
Can I buy tickets on the day?
No. Tickets are advance only, and they’re on sale now through the official RIAT website.
Do children still go free?
Yes. Under 18s go free with a ticket holding adult. Friday Futures and FRIAT are both carrying on too.
Can I get a refund?
RIAT asks you to wait for the transport, Park & Ride, hotel and camping details first. If you still want a refund after that, use RIAT’s refund form with your original booking reference.
What happens to the parking I paid for at Fairford?
It’s refunded automatically to your card (£15 a car, £7.50 a motorbike) by the end of December 2026. If parking is offered at Boscombe Down, you’ll get priority booking.
I booked an enclosure or the grandstand. What now?
Packages and upgrades are paused while the new options are worked out. RIAT will contact you about switching or cancelling.

Getting there and staying over

How do I get there without a car?
RIAT plans free rail shuttles from Salisbury, Andover and Swindon stations, plus Park & Ride. You’ll need a general admission ticket to board. Routes and times aren’t out yet. See Getting there.
Is there official camping?
Yes, on a new site beside the airfield. Rollover bookings get first pick, then the waitlist, then general sale. See Camping and hotels.
What about accessible parking and viewing?
There are no Boscombe Down details yet. RIAT Customer Services can help with your own needs on 01285 713456.
When do we find out which aircraft are coming?
RIAT usually starts weekly line-up updates in February. The headline theme for 2027 is the Tiger Gathering.

At the show

Can I bring my dog?
No. Pet dogs and other animals aren’t allowed on the showground, in the Park & View enclosures or in RIAT’s car parks. The aircraft are very loud and would distress them. If you need an assistance dog, contact RIAT before you book.
Can I fly a drone?
No. Boscombe Down is a military airfield with restricted airspace, and drones aren’t allowed at the show.
Can I bring food, drink and a chair?
At Fairford you could bring your own picnic and a folding chair, and most people did. RIAT will publish the Boscombe Down rules nearer the time.
Is Park & View included in a show ticket?
No. It’s a separate ticket for Wed 14, Thu 15 and Mon 19 July. See Park & View.

First time at RIAT

What I’d tell a first timer

RIAT is one of the biggest military airshows in the world. The flying runs for hours, the showground is huge and you’ll be on your feet all day, so a bit of planning goes a long way.

Arrive

Get there early

Car parks open at 06:00. The roads get busy fast, and arriving early means a better spot on the crowd line before the flying starts.

Pace yourself

It’s a long day

Saturday and Sunday have seven hours plus of flying. See the static park in the morning, then settle in for the display.

Leave

Have a plan home

Everyone leaves at once. Stay for the last display and let the queues ease, or know your shuttle times before you set off.

What to pack

My RIAT bag

  • Your tickets, on your phone and printed
  • Sun cream, a hat and sunglasses, as there’s little shade on an airfield
  • A waterproof, because July in Wiltshire can go either way
  • Ear defenders for children, and earplugs for you
  • A folding chair or a blanket
  • Water, plus snacks to keep you going between queues
  • A power bank for your phone
  • Comfortable shoes, ready for a lot of walking
Photography

Bringing a camera?

A long lens (300 mm or more) is the usual choice for display flying, with a wider lens for the static park. Bring spare batteries and cards, as you’ll shoot more than you think.
