The Night Before An Airshow

My evening checklist, so the morning is calm and nothing gets left on the kitchen table

An early start goes wrong the evening before, usually around ten o'clock when someone asks where the tickets are. Do the checks tonight, pack the bag tonight and put it by the front door before you go to bed.

Most shows now send tickets by email or through an app. That's fine at home on the wifi. It's less fine at a gate with thirty thousand other people all trying to load the same page on one phone mast.

So tonight, open every ticket and save it. Then take a screenshot of each one, barcode and all. I would also print a copy and fold it into the bag. It sounds old fashioned. It has saved me twice.

Check you have the right ticket for each part of the day. Some shows sell the car park, a grandstand or an enclosure separately from entry, and you need both. At the Royal International Air Tattoo, for example, you show your admission ticket and your enclosure ticket to get into an enclosure. If a pass or a wristband came through the post, put it in the bag or on the dashboard now while you can still find it.

Camping wristbands are usually put on at check in, so there's nothing to do there tonight apart from knowing where your booking email is.

The gate queue is not the place to find out your ticket won't load.

I would advise turning your screen brightness up before you reach the gate. Scanners struggle with a dim phone, and the stewards will thank you for it.

Grass car parks fill row by row. Follow the marshals and remember your row.

Shows put out their traffic plans a week or two before, and they do change them. Have one last look at the show's own travel page tonight. Road closures, one way systems and which car park gate to use are all on there, and your sat nav won't know about any of them.

Save the postcode or the pin for the car park entrance, not the airfield. They are often miles apart. Put some fuel in the car or plug it in tonight, because a garage queue at six in the morning is no fun.

Going by train or bus? Check the times for your actual day. Weekend engineering work is common, and replacement buses add a lot of time. Look at the last train or bus home too and make a note of it. If you use an English older person's bus pass, it is valid at any time at weekends and on bank holidays, but only from 09:30 on weekdays, which matters for a Friday show.

All the routes, stations and shuttle buses are on the public transport page, and the driving and parking side is in Getting there.

This might work for your family: work out your leave time backwards. Gates open, minus the drive, minus at least an hour for the last few miles and the car park. That number goes on the fridge.

Unfortunately the weather on the day will be what it will be, so plan accordingly. If it's fresh, dress in layers to keep warm.

Look at the forecast tonight and again in the morning. Airfields are flat and open, so it always feels a few degrees colder than at home, and the wind never stops. I lay out a T shirt, a fleece and a waterproof for each of us on the sofa, and everyone gets dressed from their own pile.

Keep an eye on Met Office warnings. A yellow warning usually means you carry on but take care. Amber means there's a good chance of real disruption, so think about whether to change your plans. Red is rare and means don't travel. Shows sometimes cancel a day for high winds, and they will post it on their own website and social pages first.

Sun cream goes in the bag whatever the sky looks like. Cloud doesn't stop you burning on an airfield. The full kit list for hot and wet days is on the what to pack page.

The flying still goes on in the drizzle. The people with waterproofs stay for all of it.

  1. Plug everything in first

    Do it straight after tea, before anything else. Phones, the power bank, camera batteries, a tablet for the kids. Things that start charging at eleven at night are never full by six.

  2. Pack the cables in the bag now

    Not in the morning. A full power bank with no lead is just a heavy brick.

  3. Lay it all out on the floor

    Chairs, blanket, bag, cool box. Seeing it in one place shows you what's missing far quicker than a list in your head.

  4. Pack for the bag search

    Most shows check bags at the gate. Put the things they will want to see on the top, and keep it to one bag each if you can. I go through it properly in the security guide.

  5. Cash and a card

    Food stalls, programmes and the trade stalls. Some aircraft are opened up so you can take a look inside, and you can buy exclusive merch in or outside the aircraft, so it's worth having a bit of money for that too.

Lay it all out and check it before it goes in the car.

Camping for the weekend? Charge the power station the night before you leave home.

The food and drink is part of the fun. I still take my own water.

Fill the water bottles tonight and put them in the fridge. On a warm day you will drink far more than you think, and the queues for drinks are at their longest when it's hottest.

Make the sandwiches now too. Pack the cool box tonight apart from the ice packs, which go in the freezer. I leave a note on top of the cool box that just says "ice packs". It looks daft. It works.

Keep a few snacks back for the drive home. The car park queue at a big show can take a while, and a bag of crisps makes it a lot calmer. Prices and what's on offer at each show are on the food and drink page.

I would advise checking your show's rules on food and drink before you make a full picnic. Most are happy for you to bring your own, but a few have limits on glass or alcohol.

Your child's ears are delicate, so try to protect them as much as possible. Ear defenders are the one thing I would never leave behind. A fast jet on full power is loud even for adults, and children love to see them, so they will be right at the front.

Get the ear defenders out tonight and let the children try them on. Little ones who have never worn them sometimes pull them off, so a few minutes wearing them in the living room helps. One pair each. Sharing doesn't work when two jets come through together.

Pack a spare set of clothes in a carrier bag. Write your phone number on a wristband or inside a sleeve, and tell older children the plan if they get lost: stand still, then find a steward.

Something for the journey home helps too. A game, a book or a charged tablet. There's more on spending the day with little ones in On the day.

Children head straight for the front. Make sure their ear defenders go with them.

Set up and ready. Most of that work happened the night before.

If you are taking a camera, this is the bit that catches people out. Batteries on charge, spares too, and memory cards backed up and formatted. There's nothing worse than finding last month's photos still on the card when the Red Arrows are running in.

Give the lenses a clean and check the sensor for dust. Then set the camera up for the show tonight: shutter speed, continuous focus, burst mode. I would suggest leaving it in the mode you'll start the day in, so you can switch it on and shoot.

A rain cover and a cloth go in the bag whatever the forecast says. If you are just using a phone, a charged power bank is your camera kit.

The settings themselves are in the photography hub.

This is roughly how my evening goes before a show with a two hour drive. Move the times to suit your own trip.

  1. 18:00
    Everything on charge

    Phones, power banks, camera batteries.

  2. 18:30
    Last look at the show's travel page

    Road closures, car park gate, any news.

  3. 19:00
    Food made, bottles in the fridge

    Ice packs in the freezer.

  4. 19:30
    Lay it all out and pack

    Clothes in piles, bag packed, chairs by the door.

  5. 20:00
    Run the checklist

    Tick it off together, so everyone knows the plan.

  6. 21:00
    Alarms set, early night

    Two alarms. One on the phone and one somewhere else.

Agree a leave time with everyone tonight. Out of the door, not wake up. Then set the alarm for that time minus however long your house takes to get going, plus fifteen minutes because someone always needs the toilet at the last moment.

I set two alarms. One on my phone and a second one on another phone or a clock across the room. If you are camping, put the phone somewhere you can hear it but not reach it from the sleeping bag.

How about this: load the car tonight if it's safe to leave things in it, and leave the keys on top of the bag by the door. Tomorrow you just get dressed, grab the cool box and go.

Every show has its own list of things it won't let in, and the stewards at the gate will take them off you. Have a look at your show's list tonight and take anything on it out of the bag. It's much easier at the kitchen table than in a queue.

Drones

Leave them at home. Airshows have restricted airspace around them. At RIAT, flying drones in or around the airfield is banned and drones can't come onto the showground at all.

Barbecues

Not allowed in the public areas at most shows, including disposable ones. RIAT bans them on the showground. Campsites have their own rules.

Tall Shelters

Shelters and windbreaks are often limited in height and kept back from the crowd line. At RIAT the limit is 1.5 metres, and not within 10 metres of the crowd line.

Dogs

Many shows only allow assistance dogs, and RIAT doesn't allow other pets even in a car in the car park. Check before you plan to bring yours.

Glass And Alcohol

Some shows, often the seafront ones, have limits on glass or bringing your own alcohol. Decant into plastic bottles if you are not sure.

Flags And Big Kit

Flags, banners, kites and step ladders are on some lists too. If it's big or tall, check first.

Please see the rules before you go. Lists change from year to year and I can't keep up with every show's small print. The security guide has the bag search in more detail, and each show page links to its own rules.

Our view

I used to do all of this in the morning, half asleep, and I always forgot something. Now it's done by nine the night before and the morning is just getting dressed and going. The checklist is the bit I'd keep. Print it, stick it in the bag, and you've got it for every show this year.
