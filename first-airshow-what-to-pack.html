What to pack

Top ten lists for every kind of show, with a tick box checklist

Everything you bring, you carry across a field and back again. Pack light, pack for sun and rain on the same day, and pack so that a bag search takes seconds.

Different shows need different kit. Pick the tab that matches your day and tick the items off as they go in the bag. Ticks are just for you and clear when you leave the page.

The big ticketed shows such as RIAT, Cosford and Duxford. Long walks, no shade, long days.

  • Low folding chairsLight to carry and kind to the people behind you
  • A picnic blanketSaves your pitch and gives children somewhere to sit
  • Water, and more waterTwo litres per person on a hot day. Freeze a bottle overnight
  • Packed lunch and snacksSaves money and queueing. See the food and drink guide
  • Sun cream, hat and sunglassesThere is no shade on the crowd line
  • Waterproof jacketShowers come through. The flying carries on
  • Ear defenders for childrenFast jets are loud enough to hurt small ears
  • Tickets and parking passOn your phone and printed, with the battery charged
  • A power bankSignal drains the phone. The photos drain it faster
  • A small rucksack, not a big bagQuick to search at the gate and easy to carry all day

Free promenade shows such as Eastbourne, Clacton, Wales, Blackpool, Southport and Ayr.

  • LayersIt is colder on the seafront than in town, even in August
  • Windproof jacketSea breezes build through the afternoon
  • Something to sit onThe beach is shingle at some of them. A blanket or a low chair
  • Sun cream and a hatSea light burns faster than you expect
  • Cash and cardTown prices and small traders. Card machines fail when the signal goes
  • WaterEasy to buy, but queues are long at the busy spots
  • BinocularsThe display line is further out over the water than on an airfield
  • Train timesCheck the last train and the engineering work before you leave
  • A plan for the toiletsTown toilets and temporary units, both busy. Know where they are
  • Patience for the roadsThe promenade closes. Arrive mid morning or take the train

Anything from a toddler to a teenager. Build the day around them and everyone enjoys it.

  • Ear defendersThe single best buy for a child at an airshow
  • Snack bagA bag of their favourites that lasts all day
  • Refillable bottle eachKeeps them drinking without you buying bottles every hour
  • Wipes and a small first aid kitPlasters, antihistamine, their medication
  • A buggy that copes with grassBig wheels. Small wheels stop dead in a wet field
  • Contact details on themA wristband or a note with your mobile number
  • Something for the quiet bitsA book, a colouring set, a tablet with downloads
  • Spare clothesMud, ice cream and rain. Usually all three
  • A small treat budgetAgree one bought treat and stick to it
  • A meeting point agreed on arrivalSay it out loud and point at it

Bringing a camera? The full kit guide is in the Photography Hub. This is the short list.

  • One or two bodiesTwo saves lens changes in the dust. One is fine for most people
  • A long lens for the flyingSomething in the 300mm to 600mm range
  • A wide lens for the staticThe one that stays on the second body
  • Spare batteries and cardsMore than you think. Cold and long days eat batteries
  • Lens cloths and a blowerDust, rain and fingerprints all day
  • A monopod, not a tripodTripods take room on the crowd line and some shows limit them outside the enclosures
  • A bag you can carry all daySling or backpack. See the bags guide in the hub
  • The sun guide for the showKnow where the light is before you pick your pitch
  • A small step or stoolGets you over heads if you are not on the front row
  • Rain cover for the cameraFlat shows still fly in drizzle

Shows with camping on site or nearby, including RIAT, Duxford and the Midlands Air Festival.

  • Tent, pegs and a malletHard ground in a dry summer, soft in a wet one. Bring both kinds of peg
  • Sleeping mat and warm bagSummer nights in a field are colder than you think
  • Head torchHands free for the walk to the toilet block
  • Stove, fuel and a kettleBreakfast and a brew before the queue for the gate
  • Cool boxMilk, lunch and the evening meal for two days
  • Camp chairsThey double as your crowd line chairs
  • Ear plugsCampsites near airfields are noisy. So are campers
  • Wet wipes and a towelShowers are limited or a long walk away
  • Bin bagsRubbish, wet clothes and muddy boots
  • Power bank or a car chargerNo sockets in a field
I would advise packing the night before and leaving the bags by the door. The morning of a show is early enough without hunting for sun cream.

Children get hot, cold, hungry, bored and tired faster than adults, and usually all before lunch. A small amount of kit chosen for them turns a long day into a good one.

Ear defenders come first. The fast jets are loud enough to upset a child who was enjoying everything else, and a cheap pair of children's defenders from any DIY shop or online does the job. After that it is snacks, drinks, shade and something to do in the gaps. A kite is a surprisingly good one on a breezy airfield, as long as you stay well back from the crowd line.

For toddlers, bring everything you would bring for a day at the beach, plus a buggy that copes with grass and a change of clothes.

What I would buy

  • Children's ear defenders
  • A refillable bottle with a sports cap
  • A sun hat that stays on, with a chin strap for little ones
  • A lightweight picnic blanket with a waterproof back
  • A small backpack they can carry themselves
  • Wristbands you can write a phone number on

Budget end of the range is fine for all of these. They only need to last the summer.

Banned

Glass

Bottles and jars are banned almost everywhere. Decant into plastic.

Banned

Barbecues and flames

Disposable barbecues, camping stoves and candles stay at home or at the campsite.

Banned

Drones

A criminal offence near an airshow. Leave it in the car, better still leave it at home.

Think twice

The big cool box

Heavy to carry and often over the bag size limit. A small cool bag and frozen bottles do the same job.

Think twice

Tall chairs and gazebos

Block the view for everyone behind you and usually not allowed on the crowd line.

Think twice

More than you can carry

You will walk a long way with everything. If it does not fit in one bag per person, it is too much.

Our view

One rucksack per person, a low chair each and a blanket between you covers almost everything for an airfield show. Add the waterproof even if the forecast is good. Photographers, the kit list in the Photography Hub picks up where this one stops.
