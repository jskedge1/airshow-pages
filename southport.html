Southport Air Show

Two days on the seafront. August bank holiday weekend

Southport Air Show has been flying over the Sefton coast since 1991. It’s a two day seafront show on the August bank holiday weekend, with the Red Arrows, the Battle of Britain Memorial Flight, warbirds and civilian display acts over the beach. On the ground there’s a Military Village, market stalls and food traders along Marine Drive, and fairground rides for the children.

The show at a glance

The show runs along Southport seafront, with the crowd on Marine Drive, the seawall and the beach, looking out over the sand towards the Irish Sea. It draws tens of thousands of people across the bank holiday weekend.

Southport is a proper seaside show with a strong RAF presence. In 2026 the Red Arrows flew both days, billed as their only public display in the North West that year, alongside the Battle of Britain Memorial Flight, the RAF Tutor, a P-51 Mustang and a busy line-up of civilian acts. The RAF Typhoon closed the show on the Sunday.

Car parks open at 09:00, the gates open at 10:00 and the flying runs from about midday to 17:00, so you have the morning to save your pitch, look around the Military Village and find the food traders on Marine Drive. Lord Street and the town centre are a short walk behind you if some of your group need a break from the flying.

The main things to plan for are the bank holiday crowds, the weather coming in off the Irish Sea and the road closures along Marine Drive. I would advise coming by train if you can, and arriving early.

The Military Village

The Military Village is the place to head before the flying starts. In 2026 it had a replica Chinook, the RAF Typhoon Display Team stand, the Mid Lancs Military Vehicle Trust, the Garrison Artillery Volunteers and the Veterans Living History Museum, plus flight simulators nearby. It’s a great way for your kids to learn about the different roles in the forces, and if anyone in your group is thinking of joining up, it’s the place to ask questions.

At many UK shows the RAF display teams spend time on the ground too, like the famous Red Arrows pilots and their ground crew, where kids can get stickers and take photos with the pilots. Keep an eye out for them between displays.

    Do I need a ticket?
    Yes. In 2026 adults paid £13 plus a booking fee, groups of 10 or more paid £12 each and under 16s went free. The Lancashire Post also reported a £26 family ticket. You could buy online or pay on the gate, and carers went free with proof of Carer’s Allowance, Attendance Allowance or DLA.
    Which roads close?
    In 2025 Marine Drive closed between the Pleasureland roundabout and the Marine Way bridge roundabout from the Wednesday to the Monday, and to through traffic from 07:00 to 19:00 on show days. The Esplanade ran one way northbound until 15:00, then southbound. Expect something similar in 2027, and check the official page before you travel.
    Is there a park and ride?
    No park and ride was offered in 2026. The official car parks are on Marine Drive, Princes Park, the Esplanade and Victoria Park, about five minutes’ walk from the show, at £10 per car for the day.
    Are there cheap train fares?
    On the 2026 show weekend Merseyrail offered £2 adult and £1 child returns to Southport. Check the Merseyrail event page nearer the 2027 show to see if the offer returns.
    Can someone drop me off?
    Agree a drop off point away from the closed part of Marine Drive, such as near the station or Lord Street, then walk to the seafront. Pick a meeting time for the journey home in advance, as phone signal can be poor in big crowds.

    Where to stay

    Southport is a seaside resort, so there are plenty of beds within walking distance of the show, plus holiday parks and campsites a short drive away. It’s the August bank holiday weekend, so I would advise booking as soon as the 2027 dates are announced.

    Get creative over the beach

    Southport has its challenges for photographers. Aircraft fly further out than at an airfield, sea haze can soften detail, and from mid afternoon the sun moves round in front of you, so aircraft can look dark against a bright sky. The good news is that the sun is behind you for the first half of the flying. I would suggest using the sun guide to plan when you shoot, saving your close up work for the early displays, and adding a little exposure compensation later in the afternoon.

    Ideas to try

    • Wait for the banking passes. A topside or underside view shows far more of the aircraft than a straight flypast.
    • Leave room in the frame for smoke trails, so they lead the eye to the aircraft.
    • Zoom out now and then to include the pier, the beach or the crowds on the seawall, so the photo says Southport.
    • Late in the day, turn the backlight into silhouettes and glowing smoke rather than fighting it.

    Food, toilets & access

    Southport has a real advantage over airfield shows: a whole town sits right behind the seafront. Eat from the traders on Marine Drive, bring a picnic, or step back to Lord Street or Ocean Plaza for a sit down meal.

    Food & drink

    Food traders and market stalls along Marine Drive, plus the cafés, pubs and restaurants in town. You can bring your own food, but BBQs, gas and open fires aren’t allowed.

    Kids’ activities

    Bumper cars, bungee trampolines, Hook a Duck, flight simulators and the Military Village keep younger visitors busy between displays.

    Toilets

    Toilets are within the site, with accessible toilets at the two accessible viewing areas. Go before the flying starts, and if you aren’t waiting for a particular display, use that slot as your break.

    The beach

    The beach is soft sand, so a blanket or low chair makes a long afternoon far more comfortable. Seating on site is very limited.

    Accessibility
    There are two fenced, hard standing accessible viewing areas on the seawall, near the fish wind display and near the Pier, with accessible toilets and stewards. The promenade has ramped, step free access. There is no Changing Places toilet on site, but a pass out stamp lets you leave and come back, and the nearest are at Victoria Park, Dunes Splashworld, Southport Market, The Life Rooms and The Atkinson. Ear defenders are advised, and arriving early is quieter. Questions go to Tourism@sefton.gov.uk.
    Carers
    In 2026 carers went free with proof of Carer’s Allowance, Attendance Allowance or DLA. Without proof, the carer pays the normal price.
    Dogs
    Dogs are allowed but not recommended, because of the noise. They must be kept on a lead, or the owner can be asked to leave. There is also a seasonal dog ban from 1 May to 30 September on the stretch of beach from the Pier south towards Pleasureland.
    Banned items
    Drones, fireworks, weapons, lasers, smoke canisters and transmitting devices aren’t allowed, and random bag searches take place.
    If the weather turns
    Rain, low cloud or strong wind off the Irish Sea can change the programme or stop some displays. In 2026 heavy rain cut the Red Arrows’ Sunday display short. Check the official channels on the morning and pack a waterproof.
